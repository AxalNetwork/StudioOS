## D8526 — A selected company is a filter, never authorization: the inventory, its guard and the three-account test (#8526)

- **Status:** built by S14, 2026-11-25 (#8526, row E2 of #8509).
- **Migration:** none.
- **Why:** `middleware/activeCompany.ts` turns the browser's `X-Company-Id` into a verified company id, or null. For a list, null is safe. For a request that acts on one company or venture, a stale or forged selection must not change what is authorized. Until now nothing listed the routes that read the selection, or said for each whether it is a filter or a target.

### 1. The inventory

`node scripts/build-company-scope-inventory.mjs` reads every mounted Worker route (the auth inventory's `routeTable`, so a route reads as its handler plus the helpers it reaches). It keeps those that read the selection: they call `resolveActiveCompany` or `activeCompanyFor`, or name `ACTIVE_COMPANY_HEADER` or the header. It writes `documentation/architecture/COMPANY_SCOPE_INVENTORY.json`, which is build output and never committed (D5612). Each row has the route, its file and line, its kind and its checks.

- **Kind.** A **target** names a record in its path or writes. A **filter** is a GET with no record in its path: a list the selection narrows.
- **Checks.** The ownership and membership evidence the auth inventory reads, plus the cap table's `canReadProject` and `canWriteProject`. Those two rest on `computeProjectPermissions` → `canAccessProject`, one call deeper than the auth inventory reaches. `resolveActiveCompany` is never a check: it proves the caller may *select* a company, not that they may act on a record.

On this tree, **621 routes** read the selection: **531 targets**, every one naming a check of its own, and **90 filters**. The issue estimated about 62 references in `routes/`. The real count is larger because a route reads the selection through any helper it calls, and many routes reach `activeCompanyFor` that way.

### 2. The guard

`scripts/baselines/company-scope/` is the reviewed ledger: one entry per route, `{ "kind": "filter" | "target" }`, split by route family like the auth ledger. `--check` runs in `npm run test:guards` (`scripts/guard-commands.json`) and fails on:

- a route that reads the selection with no ledger entry. A new reader is a change a person looks at; `--seed` adds it once they have.
- an entry for a route that no longer reads it, or whose kind changed;
- **a target with no check:** its authorization would be the active company alone.

### 3. What the audit found

- **No target route trusts the selection.** Five routes first read as having no check: three cap table record reads (history, export, a close) and two scenario routes (today, simulate). Each reaches `canAccessProject` through `canReadProject`, which is why that name is in the checks. The data room's co-founder path never reads the selection at all.
- **A disclosure, fixed here.** The cap table record answered a stranger to a venture with 403 "You don't have access to that project" for a venture that exists, and 404 for one that does not. Project ids are sequential, so this told anyone signed in which ventures exist. The writer's plan check did the same with its 402.
  - The record's reader and writer (`captable.ts`, `recordReader`, `recordWriter`, `strangerTo`) now answer someone with **no relation** to the venture as a missing venture, 404 "Project not found". No relation means not an admin, not its owner and no project seat. The writer does this before the plan check.
  - A member who lacks a capability keeps D5074's 403. They already know the venture exists.
  - Five existing assertions that pinned 403 for a stranger now expect 404: D1017 (an unrelated partner and another founder; another venture's record), D1021 (an outsider's close), D1025 (another founder's history), D1026 (an unrelated partner's export) and D5074 (the unrelated partner and the other founder on writes).
  - Three auth-ledger entries for those reads (`record_without_ownership_check`, unreviewed) are removed, because the reader now finds their membership check.

### 4. The three-account test

`cloudflare-worker/test/company_scope_three_accounts_8526.test.ts` runs on the full migrated schema.

**The accounts:**

- A owns Northwind and Southwind, each with a venture.
- B is a member of Northwind with a co-founder seat on its venture.
- C has no access.

**The route classes,** all on that venture:

- read settings: `GET /api/captable/record/:id`
- write settings: `POST …/proposals`; a granted write fails validation with 400
- export: `GET …/export.csv`
- download: `POST /api/data-room/:uid/files/:uid/download`

**The events.** After each event, the very next request is checked:

- B's seat is revoked;
- Northwind is archived;
- the venture moves to Southwind;
- a header names another company. This includes C joining Southwind, then Northwind, with no seat.

**The rules:**

- A header (none, each company, a missing one, garbage) never answers more permissively than no header. For B and C, who own no company here, the answer is identical.
- A revoked seat is refused on every class, even when B still belongs to the venture's company and selects it.
- C is refused throughout, and C's refusal for the venture equals the refusal for a venture that does not exist.
