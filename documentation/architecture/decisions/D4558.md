## D4558 — D1 backups carry a manifest, and the drill restores only what it vouches for (#4558)

Part of #4558 (D1 disaster recovery v2). This is the part that needs no
provider, account or key: every backup gets an integrity record, and the
monthly drill fails closed against it. The independent encrypted copy waits on
the owner's answers to the four questions on the issue (recovery objectives,
where the copy lives, who holds the key, and how long it is kept).

It extends the existing pipeline: `backup-d1.yml`, `scripts/dr-drill.sh`,
`scripts/dr-drill-split.mjs` (D523), the `drill-d1.json` marker (D263) and
D167's runbook. It adds no scheduler, export format or owner-action tracker.

### What the current state showed

These facts were read from the repository and from the two workflows' run
history, read-only, on 2026-11-06.

- **No restore has ever passed.** The 2026-11-01 drill (run 41307767798) was
  the first after D523's chunking.
  - It split the 271,749,675-byte export into 276 chunks (218 tables, 3 cyclic,
    8 split across chunks).
  - It imported 274 of them, then failed at chunk 275 (data) with
    `SQLITE_CONSTRAINT_FOREIGNKEY`.
  - Each chunk is its own import, so `defer_foreign_keys` lasts only one
    chunk. A reference that crosses a chunk boundary, or an orphan row already
    in production, would fail exactly there.
  - Neither cause is established: the drill logged only the chunk number.
- **The daily backup succeeds,** but it runs 07:12 to 09:26 UTC, not at its
  02:10 cron. A recovery point is as old as the export's real start, not the
  date in its key.
- **There was no integrity record.** The heartbeat held a key, a time and a
  size. There was no checksum, migration version or row count, and nothing to
  tell a truncated upload from a whole one.
- **A failed backup never paged.** `backup-d1.yml`'s pager step tested its own
  `env:` in its `if:`, the defect D263 fixed in `dr-drill.yml`.
- **A stale backup only paged.** The drill paged `rpo_breach` and went on.

### Decision

1. **A manifest beside each backup, never inside it.**
   - `backup-d1.yml` builds `d1/<db>/backup-YYYY-MM-DD.manifest.json` with
     `scripts/d1-backup-manifest.mjs build`:
     - sha256 and size;
     - the export's **start** time, which is the recovery point;
     - the D1 Time Travel bookmark read just before the export (null when it
       cannot be read, never invented);
     - every table with its row count and its `REFERENCES` parents;
     - the last migration in `schema_migrations`, the ledger
       `scripts/migrate-d1.mjs` writes.
   - It holds names, counts, hashes and times only, never a row value.
2. **The upload is read back before it counts.** The workflow downloads the
   object it just put and compares sha256. Only then does it write the manifest
   and the heartbeat, which gains `sha256`, `manifest_key` and `exported_at`. A
   truncated or corrupted upload never becomes "the latest backup".
3. **The drill verifies before it creates anything.** A new `verify` step
   fetches the manifest and the backup, then runs `d1-backup-manifest.mjs
   verify`. It fails closed, exit 2, with the reason in the marker, on:
   - `manifest_missing` or `manifest_invalid`;
   - `key_mismatch`;
   - `size_mismatch` (a truncated download);
   - `sha256_mismatch` (a tampered or corrupted one);
   - `stale`: the export started more than `DR_MAX_BACKUP_AGE_HOURS` (default
     36) before the drill. It still pages `rpo_breach`, and the drill is now
     red;
   - `migration_unknown`: the backup's schema is newer than the checkout's
     tooling.

   No throwaway database is created for a backup that fails.
4. **Every table's row count is compared.** After the restore, the drill counts
   rows in every table the manifest lists, 40 tables to a query. A difference,
   or a table missing from the restore, fails the smoke step
   (`row_counts_differ`, `row_counts_unreadable`). It names the tables, never
   their rows.
5. **A failed chunk says where to look.** The drill logs the tables the failed
   chunk writes and their parents from the manifest, names only. The next run's
   log narrows the chunk-275 failure to tables, and the marker's `reason` is
   `chunk_<n>_of_<total>`.
6. **The marker reports what the drill measured:**
   - `recovery_point`, and `recovery_point_age_s` (the RPO this drill saw);
   - `duration_s` (its RTO, from start to teardown);
   - `sha256`, `migration` and `tables`.

   HQ's Security page reads the marker as before; the new fields are additive.
7. **The backup pager works.** Its `if:` reads a job-level `PAGER_CONFIGURED`,
   as `dr-drill.yml` does.

### The independent copy (the owner's answers, 2026-11-07)

The owner answered A on Q1–Q4. This part was built in the follow-up PR.

1. **Targets.** RPO 24 h from the export's real start; RTO 4 h for an export
   restore.
   - The drill's marker gains `rpo_target_s` (86,400), `rto_target_s`
     (14,400), `rpo_met`, `rto_met` and `copy`.
   - A passing run that misses a target pages `rpo_target_missed` or
     `rto_target_missed`.
   - The staleness limit (`DR_MAX_BACKUP_AGE_HOURS`) defaults to 30 h: the
     24 h cadence plus the 5 to 7 h GitHub scheduling delay seen in the run
     history.
2. **The copy, `scripts/dr-second-copy.sh`.** It runs in `backup-d1.yml` only
   when `vars.DR_SECOND_COPY_ENABLED` is `true`, in a step with no Cloudflare
   credential.
   - It encrypts the export on the runner with `age` to `vars.DR_AGE_RECIPIENT`,
     the owner's public key.
   - It puts the ciphertext and a copy manifest (the export's manifest plus the
     ciphertext's sha256 and size and the recipient) under
     `d1/<db>/daily/<recovery point>/`, and the month's first export under
     `d1/<db>/monthly/<YYYY-MM>/`.
   - Every put is write-once (`If-None-Match: *`) and checksummed (SHA-256).
     `age` is randomized, so each object also carries the plaintext export's
     sha256. An existing key is accepted only for the same export (a retried
     attempt); any other is refused, and nothing is replaced.
   - It reads the daily object's metadata back and checks its size and hash.
   - The credential is write-only, and the script calls only `put-object` and
     `head-object`.
3. **Freshness.** A separate step writes `heartbeat-d1-independent.json` to the
   primary bucket. With the copy on, the monthly drill fails
   `independent_copy_missing` or `independent_copy_stale`.
4. **The owner-run drill.** `DR_SOURCE=independent` fetches a chosen recovery
   point with a read key, checks the ciphertext, decrypts it with
   `DR_AGE_IDENTITY_FILE` (the owner's offline key, supplied for that run),
   and restores it through the same verification and row-count comparison.
   The refusals are `decrypt_failed`, `ciphertext_mismatch` and `key_missing`,
   each before any database exists.
5. **The owner's actions** are in `D1_RECOVERY.md` §7: the account outside
   production, the bucket with object lock and lifecycle (35 daily, 12
   monthly), the write-only key, the offline `age` key and its rotation, and
   the drill.

Tests: `frontend/test/dr_second_copy_d4558.test.mjs` (10) runs the copy and
the drill against a fake `aws` that enforces `If-None-Match` and the checksum,
with the real `age`. It covers:
- decryption by the owner's key only;
- only put and head being called;
- a retried attempt accepted, while another export at the same point is
  refused with nothing replaced;
- the monthly point kept once;
- misconfiguration, a mismatched export and an unverifiable copy failing
  closed;
- the workflow's gating and credentials;
- the owner-run drill passing, and refusing a wrong key, a tampered
  ciphertext or no key;
- the scheduled drill's heartbeat checks;
- the targets.

Each assertion was mutation-checked both ways.

### What this does not do

- **No provisioning.** The account, bucket, keys and variables are the
  owner's to create (`D1_RECOVERY.md` §7). Until then the copy is off.
- **The chunk-275 failure is not fixed.** Its cause needs the evidence point 5
  collects, on the next owner-run or scheduled drill (2026-12-01).
- **The first drill after this merges needs a backup that has a manifest.** The
  daily backup writes one from its next run. A drill pointed at an older
  backup fails `manifest_missing`, by design.
- **No restore, export, deploy or production write was run** for this
  decision. `scripts/restore-d1.sh` is unchanged: moving it onto the splitter
  and the manifest is its own task (D523).
