## D1530 — The agent engine's planner, role templates and team wizard (E4 of #3742)

- **Status:** built by the S24 session (#3848, 2026-11-01). It is behind `AGENT_ENGINE` (`"off"` in both `wrangler.toml` blocks) and `TEAM_AGENTS_ENABLED`. Not deployed.
- **Builds on:**
  - D1529 (goals, tasks and dependencies; `routes/agent_engine.ts`);
  - D1496 (the roles, rule books, the read and manage gates, and `agentRun`);
  - D764 (the rail matrix, from which the planner's option comes);
  - `ai_rail_proposals` (`proposeRail`, the decided statuses).
- **Migration:** none. Templates and the wizard live in code. Custom roles (`/:scope/:id/roles/custom`) need migration 728 (#3847) and are not in this change.

### Why

A goal with no tasks gives the team nothing to do, and an empty team page gives a founder no idea which roles to add. E4 drafts the tasks for one goal and suggests the roles for one team, and both are proposals a person reviews.

### What was decided

1. **The planner is a rail option.** The option is `propose:goal-plan` on Founder · Studio (`/studio`), with `kind: 'plan'` and `handler: 'draft'`. It is a row in `frontend/src/lib/railOptions/founder.js`, and `railMatrix.json` is regenerated by `scripts/build-rail-matrix.mjs` (task `rail_plan`), never edited by hand. The CEO role's `may_propose` gains the option.
2. **Who runs it:**
   - The scope's active CEO / Chief of staff agent, through `agentRun`. Its rule book, cap and sponsor apply, and the run is charged to the sponsor.
   - With no such agent, the asking manager's own Eadwyn runs the same option through `run`, charged to that manager.
   - Either way, `GET …/plan/quote` shows the hold first: an upper bound, or `{ available: false }` when it cannot be read, never 0.
3. **What it sees:**
   - the goal;
   - the open build plan items (`landed_id IS NULL`);
   - the open tasks;
   - the team, as people by name and agents by role.
   
   It never sees an email or a numeric id.
4. **What it may answer:**
   - **Items.** At most 20 items, `{ title, module, suggested_assignee: { role_key } | { person: true }, depends_on: [index], due_in_days }`. Extra items are cut at 20.
   - **Drops.** An unknown module or an unknown role drops its item, and the drop is noted in the proposal's `dropped`. A wait on a dropped item is removed and noted too.
   - **Loops.** A dependency loop invalidates the whole plan: the proposal is discarded and the route answers 422 `plan_invalid`.
5. **Accept writes everything at once.**
   - **Claim.** The proposal is claimed with `WHERE status = 'proposed'`, as `accepted`, or as `edited` when the manager removed or retitled items. A second accept, or a racing one, gets 409 `plan_decided`.
   - **One batch.** One `DB.batch` claims the proposal and writes every kept item as a task (`source_kind 'plan'`, `source_ref` the proposal uid, `due_at` from `due_in_days`), its `created` event and its dependencies. A per-call marker guards the inserts when a racing claim loses; it is replaced by the public decision before the transaction commits.
   - **Failure.** If the batch fails, the claim and every task roll back together and the route answers 503 `plan_not_saved`. No second release write is needed. This atomic claim was confirmed by the owner during S29's follow-up on #4033.
6. **An item goes to the role's agent only when the agent may work in the module.** The agent must be the scope's active agent of the suggested role, and the module must be in its effective `works_in` (D1529's `agentFits`). Otherwise the task is for no one yet. Accepting never invites a person.
7. **Role templates.** There is one template per catalogue role, `template:<role>`. Each carries:
   - a model tier (`fast`, `balanced` or `best`);
   - a suggested monthly cap, which only pre-fills the form (the real cap is set on D1496's POST);
   - its duties `{ key, label, module, option, runs_per_month }`. Each duty's option is in the role's `may_propose` and offered in that module.
   
   The research analyst has no duty yet, because no option it may propose is a text run.
8. **The wizard is rule-based** (`GET /:scope/:id/suggested-team?goal=`, manage only). No model is called, nothing is charged and nothing is written. Its suggestions:
   - **Stage mapping** (a draft for the owner to review):
     - idea and validate → Research analyst, CPO;
     - build → CTO, CPO, COO;
     - launch → CMO, Sales;
     - raise → CFO, Investor relations, General counsel (an assistant);
     - grow → CMO, Sales, Head of People;
     - a company → CEO / Chief of staff, CFO, General counsel.
   - **Open tasks.** A role is also suggested when an open task's module is in its `works_in`.
   - **Each suggestion** says why, whether the role is already on the team, and a month's estimate of its template's duties. The estimate is unknown when there is no duty or any quote cannot be read, never 0.
9. **`GET /templates` and `GET /capabilities`** are signed-in reads of the catalogue:
   - the templates;
   - the modules with their cycles;
   - the read keys;
   - each module's options;
   - the always-asks floor.

## E5 (#3849): duties, handoffs and memory

Built by S26 on 2026-11-02, on E3's runtime (#4060, migration 728). There is no new migration: `agent_duties`, `agent_handoffs` and `agent_memory_notes` are 728's.

### Duties (`services/agents/duties.ts`)

1. **The templates are the role templates' duties** (`templates.ts`), keyed `<role_key>.<duty key>`, for example `cfo.runway`.
   - They use rail options already in `railMatrix.json`, so no option was added or regenerated.
   - The issue's example option names (`draft:raise/runway-note` and others) were examples; E4's templates named existing options instead.
2. **Adding a duty.**
   - A duty of another role is 422 `template_not_in_role`, as is one whose module or option the agent's rule book no longer allows.
   - One live duty per template per agent: 409 `duty_exists`, from 728's unique index.
   - At most 12 per agent: 409 `too_many_duties`.
   - The default schedule is the template's cadence: weekly at 4 runs a month, monthly below that, daily at 20 or more. The defaults are Monday, the 1st of the month and 08:00 UTC.
3. **Claim before act.** The sweep (`sweepAgentDuties`, in the `% 5 === 1` block) claims each due duty with:
   ```
   UPDATE agent_duties SET next_due_at = ?, last_claimed_at = ? WHERE id = ? AND next_due_at = ? AND status = 'active'
   ```
   Only a claim that changed one row acts. The new `next_due_at` is the first slot after the claim's own time, so a duty missed in an outage runs once and jumps forward.
4. **What a claim does:**
   - **A paused agent:** `skipped:agent_paused`.
   - **A sponsor with no AI tokens left** (`budgetAllows` false, owner Q2 A): `skipped:no_tokens`. Nothing is queued.
   - **Otherwise:** a task with `kind` and `source_kind` both `duty`, assigned to the agent, and the sponsor is told in-app.
   - With `AGENT_AUTONOMY` `manual`, nothing runs until Start. With `self_start` and the engine `on`, the scope's object is asked to admit the task.
   - A balance that cannot be read does not skip: a task spends nothing, and its run still passes every spend check.
   - Off or halted, nothing is claimed.

### Handoffs (`services/agents/handoffs.ts`)

5. **How an agent proposes one.** A run may end its output with one line:
   ```
   {"handoff":{"to_role","title","why","note"}}
   ```
   - The prompt names the roles this agent may hand off to and the line's form.
   - The `propose` step reads the line, and the handoff becomes its own proposal on `propose:handoff`, beside the run's draft.
   - **Why a line and not a task option:** a task's option must be offered in a module of the role's `works_in`. Making `propose:handoff` a task option would have meant adding a module and an option to every role's default rule book.
   - **Why it is not in `railMatrix.json`:** no model runs on `propose:handoff`, so it has no models. A matrix row would also put a runnable chip in the Ask panel.
6. **`hands_off_to`** is a map in code, D1530's draft for the owner to review: the CEO to every other role, the CFO to General counsel, Investor relations and the CEO, and so on. A custom role reads its own `hands_off_to_json`.
7. **Refusals.** Each one is recorded as a `refused` step, so the trail (TM13) shows it:
   - `not_allowed`: the role is not in the list;
   - `no_target`: no live agent of that role in the scope, nor the project's company agent while the project is under that company;
   - `depth_exceeded`: past 3 deep;
   - `handoff_loop`: the target is already in the chain;
   - `chain_ended`: the chain already has a step at that depth. Migration 728 keeps one step per depth, and a discarded step ends its chain.

   A refusal with no place in its chain is a one-step chain of its own.
8. **Accept and discard.**
   - No handoff runs before a person's Accept.
   - Accept (or edit, which may retitle) creates the target's task in its own scope: `kind = 'handoff'`, `source_kind = 'handoff'`, `source_ref` the step's uid.
     - **The module:** the from task's module when the target works there, else the target's first module.
     - **The option:** one the target may propose there.
   - The target is read again at Accept: one removed in between makes the step `refused` with `no_target`.
   - Discard ends the chain.
9. **Untrusted text.** The why and the note never enter a task's title or detail. The target's run reads them only inside the untrusted-data wrapper (`<agent_data source="handoff" trust="data">`).

### Memory (`services/agents/memory.ts`)

10. **Notes.**
    - `decision` notes stay E3's: written by `after`, deterministically.
    - `fact` notes are a manager's. One over 1,000 characters is 422 `note_too_long`.
    - `summary` (the optional weekly `memory_digest` duty) is not built: it needs a summary option and a costed run of its own.
    - "Forget this" (`DELETE /memory/:uid`) and "Forget everything" (`DELETE /agents/:uid/memory`) are a manager's.
11. **Retention (owner Q6 A; counsel still confirms it for EU members).** The values are `RETENTION` in code, so a change is a config change. The profile's sentence (`retentionText`) is built from them.
    - **Notes:** kept while the agent exists and 90 days after it is removed. The remove route sets `expires_at`.
    - **Run step summaries:** emptied 180 days after the run ends.
    - `purgeExpired` runs at the start of each duty sweep, even while halted, because it only forgets.
12. **Erasure (the product amendment).**
    - **A project hard-deleted** (`projectTrash`) removes its agents' memory notes and run summaries, and cancels their queued tasks. A project board card is moved to `done` / `not_planned`, as 762 requires. `eraseAgentData` does this.
    - **A company:** the platform has no company hard delete yet; `eraseAgentData` takes a company scope for when it does.
    - **An account:** the platform has no account delete (D168: erasure is manual). `eraseAccountAgents` is what that manual erasure runs: the runtime half, then the D1 half.
    - Removing an agent keeps its notes for the 90 days of the retention, then the purge removes them.
13. **The account export** (`GET /api/settings/data-export`) now has `agents`: `exportAgentData`, the agents the member sponsors or can read on their teams, each with its rule book, memory notes and run history. Each scope's read gate is checked again, so a sponsor who lost access to a team exports nothing of it.
