## D4717 — Community: one population per count, the roster from the server, one clock (#4717)

**Admin Community audit, slot S26 · 2026-11-06.** Packages 1 and the clock half of 3. No migration. The rest of #4717 follows in later PRs (see "Still to do").

### What was wrong

- **The check-in band counted everyone.** The band is labelled as founders, and its privacy floor is seven. It counted every distinct `user_id` in `wellbeing_daily_pulses`, so five founders and six investors showed a band that should have been withheld.
- **The roster was counted from a capped list.** The roster tile, the Circles card and the consoles strip all counted `GET /api/admin/network-profiles`, which answers 200 rows by default and says `truncated`.
- **Archived profiles were in the network totals.** The server's network aggregate (`blocks.network`) counted archived profiles (migration 551, `archived_at`) as inactive roster members.
- **The clock was frozen at mount.** The page's clock was `useState(() => Date.now())`, so Refresh fetched new rows but measured days open, waits and Overdue from the moment the page was first opened.

### What changed

- **Check-ins (`services/communityOverview.ts`).** The band counts distinct `user_id`s whose account's role is `founder`, joined to `users`. A pulse whose account is gone counts for nothing. Still no answer column is read.
- **Network block.** It is grouped by kind and state:
  - **active:** `is_active = 1`, not archived;
  - **hidden:** not active, not archived;
  - **archived:** `archived_at` set.

  Archived rows are reported only as `archived`, in no total. The new `by_kind_state` gives each kind's active and hidden counts. `by_kind`, `active` and `inactive` keep their meaning, without archived rows.
- **The page's roster (`communityModel.rosterOf`).** The roster comes from that block, over every row, and the page no longer calls `network-profiles`. A count that is missing, negative or not a whole number makes the roster unreadable, never 0.
- **The page's clock (`communityModel.clockOf`).** The clock is the Community read's `as_of`. Before that read answers, it is the time the page's reads last settled. Refresh brings a new `as_of`, so every time-relative figure moves with it.

### Package 4 — the Jobs and Circles lightboxes, paged on the server (2026-11-07)

**The problem.** CM4 read every page of `GET /api/admin/jobs`, and CM5 read all of `GET /api/admin/circles`. Both then searched, filtered, sorted and paged in the browser, so opening either one transferred the whole table. No lightbox search waited for the typing to stop, so every keystroke started a read.

**The new reads.** Two Community routes, beside the overview, with the SQL in `services/communityLists.ts`. Each returns one page (25 by default, 100 at most) and the total that matched:

- `GET /api/admin/community/jobs?q=&status=&place=&seniority=&sort=&limit=&offset=`
  - The search covers the role and the company, with LIKE's own characters escaped.
  - Sorts:
    - applications, most first;
    - newest;
    - days open: the longest-open published posting first; a posting that isn't open, or was approved before migration 609, last (D1121);
    - role.
- `GET /api/admin/community/circles?q=&type=&access=&published=&featured=&sort=&limit=&offset=`
  - Sorts: by name, or by members as an admin entered them.
- **Shared rules:**
  - The sort is a bound key inside `CASE`, and every sort ends on the id, so page boundaries are stable.
  - A value the read can't take is a 400 sent through `refuse()`.
  - The consoles' own lists, `GET /api/admin/jobs` and `GET /api/admin/circles`, are unchanged.
  - The rows carry what a lightbox row draws, and never a host, an applicant or a member.

**The lightboxes.**

- **Jobs and Circles** send one page's query (`jobsQuery`, `circlesQuery`) and draw the Worker's page (`listPage`).
- **An answer without a numeric `total`** is unreadable, never an empty list.
- **Search:** Events, Jobs and Circles wait 300 ms after the last keystroke (`useSettled`, `SEARCH_WAIT_MS`). An answer to an older query is dropped (`useBoxRead`'s `live` flag), so one search makes one read.
- **Removed:** the client-side `listView`, `pageOf`, and the per-row search, filter and sort functions.

**Tests.**

- **Worker** (`community_lists_4717.test.ts`, 260 postings and 130 circles):
  - every row appears exactly once across the pages, for every sort;
  - the totals match the filters, and LIKE characters are searched for;
  - an unknown value is a 400;
  - Days open order follows D1121;
  - the read is admin-only and carries no person.
  - Mutation caught: Days open sorted newest approval first.
- **Frontend** (`community_lightboxes_d934`):
  - the lightboxes read one page of the Community lists and never the whole lists;
  - three keystrokes then a pause make one read;
  - the query and page helpers are tested.
  - Mutation caught: the query built from the live text.

### Package 2 — one snapshot (2026-11-07)

**The problem.** The dashboard made seven reads. Five were raw lists: the Studio glance, every page of the events list, the events analytics, every page of the job board, and the circles list. The page then recomputed, in the browser, counts the Community read already held. So two definitions could disagree. The clearest case was Next up, which listed published events only, while the Upcoming tile counted published and waiting events. The figures also came from five different moments.

**Now the page makes one read,** `GET /api/admin/community/overview`, plus Recent activity's own. Every figure, and every row behind one, comes from that read under one `as_of`.

**The rows behind three counts, read from the same rows as the counts:**

- **`events.next_up`** (D918's `readOverview`): the soonest three of the events `upcoming` counts, published or waiting. A waiting event is listed, and marked "Waiting for review". `total` equals `upcoming.events`, so the list can never disagree with the tile. This settles package 1's Upcoming-events item.
- **`queue.event_rows`** (D918's `review`): the oldest six of the events `queue` counts. Each row has its wait, its Overdue flag (D915), and whether the wait counts from creation (D917).
- **`queue.jobs.review`:** the oldest six waiting postings. A posting submitted before migration 550 is listed last, undated, and never Overdue.
- **`jobs.most_applied`:** the five postings with the most applications of all time, each with its days open (D1121).

**Counts the page reads from the overview:**

- **`events.by_status`** (every event in every status) carries the page's empty rule. That rule now reads only the overview's own counts.
- **Waiting on you and the consoles strip** read the queues' counts instead of the glance's lanes. Needs a decision's total and Overdue figures are the queues' counts, not the number of rows listed.

**What travels in the response.** A record's `event_id` or `posting_id`, its title, its company, its times, capacity and seats taken. Never a host, an applicant or a member. The worker test allows an event title only inside these rows.

**From package 3:**

- The Events card is drawn whenever the read answered. Next up, the funnel and events by type each show their own failed state, so one block that can't be read no longer hides the others.
- A period figure without whole numbers is unreadable, never 0 and never a crash.
- A circles, jobs or queue count that isn't a whole number makes its block unreadable.

**If the read itself fails,** the page says nothing is a zero and draws no figure. The consoles strip still works.

**Tests:**

- **Worker** (`admin_community_d931`): 20 pass.
  - The next-up total equals Upcoming; the review total equals the queue.
  - Mutation caught: listing published events only.
- **Frontend** (`admin_community_*`, `community_lightboxes_d934`, `community_gaps_d1121`, `hq_studio_figures_d555` and the other files that read these pages): 248 pass.
  - The mounted page makes exactly two reads: the overview and activity.
  - Mutations caught: Needs a decision counting its listed rows instead of the queues, and a status count that isn't a whole number accepted.

### Package 5 — the reads stop reading all of history (2026-11-07)

**Measured on a large synthetic community** (`cloudflare-worker/test/_community_scale_fixture.ts`): three years of a busy network, with 60,000 registrations, 20,000 check-ins, 40,000 audit rows (15,000 of them community acts), 30,000 applications and 8,000 bookings. A fifth of the timestamps are ISO 8601, so a bare comparison would show. `scripts/perf/community-bench.mjs` prints the timings, median of 20, in-process node:sqlite (relative, not D1 latency):

| Read | Before | After | Statements |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Overview, 4 weeks | 106 ms | 54 ms | 20 → 22 |
| Overview, 12 weeks | 125 ms | 83 ms | 20 → 22 |
| Overview, 52 weeks | 272 ms | 228 ms | 20 → 22 |
| Recent activity, the card | 257 ms | 30 ms | 10 → 15 |
| Recent activity, one month of events | 150 ms | 4 ms | 4 → 6 |
| Recent activity, the last page (950–1,000) | 184 ms | 186 ms | 10 → 15 |

The extra statements are the price of the split. Each one now reads an index range instead of a table.

**What read all of history, and what it reads now:**

- **Recent activity.**
  - *Before:* each source was a UNION wrapped in an outer query that applied the window, the search and the admin filter after the fact. So every request read and sorted every audit row, and grouped every registration ever made before the window was applied.
  - *Now:* each source is one or two arms, each its own statement, with every filter inside it. An absent filter is absent from the text, because `(? IS NULL OR …)` cannot use an index. Registrations are cut by `date(registered_at)` before the GROUP BY.
  - *The unfiltered card:* the newest N groups lie on the N newest days that have one, so a first read takes that N-th day from the index and the grouping starts there.
  - *The last page:* it still reads the 1,000 newest groups, because that is what it shows.
- **D918's overview** (also the Events desk's).
  - *Seats:* it grouped every registration ever made to give every event its seats. Now it reads the events alone, then the seats of the events it reads, through one statement with the ids bound as one JSON list (`overviewSeatsFor`). Those events are: not ended, starting from now, ended in the period, or cancelled.
  - *Registration rows:* it read two periods of rows into JS to count the period before. Now it reads this period's rows only, and counts the period before in SQL from the index.
  - *Who registered and check-ins:* they read only this period, by `date()`.
  - The Events desk's own list still reads every event's seats, as its sort needs.
- **The Community overview's two-period counts** (applications, bookings) read every row and summed CASEs. Now they are bounded to the two periods.

**Indexes** (migration `202611070900_community_read_indexes`): each is on the expression the reads compare, `datetime(col)` or `date(col)` (D124), as migration 016's are.

- `admin_audit_log (report_type, datetime(exported_at), action)` and `(action, datetime(exported_at))`;
- `event_registrations (date(registered_at), event_id, datetime(registered_at))`;
- `job_applications (datetime(created_at))` and `expert_bookings (datetime(created_at))`;
- `event_checkins (date(checked_in_at))`.

No other index was added: the plans of the remaining statements did not call for one.

**Still read whole, by definition, and only from an index.** These counts are over all time:

- Recent activity's unfiltered total of registration groups (the lightbox's "of N");
- the Jobs card's most applications of all time.

The catalog tables (users, events, postings, circles, experts, profiles) are read whole. They grow with the network, not with history.

**Tests** (`community_reads_4717`, on the large fixture):

- **Recent activity is the same answer.** It is compared with the old statements, which are kept in the test as the reference, over fifteen cases. They cover every kind, from/to/both, the search, one day, an empty window, and the last page under the 1,000 cap, including a window with more than 1,000 groups.
- **D918's overview** with seats for the events it reads equals the overview with every event's seats, at 4, 12 and 52 weeks.
- **The counts moved into SQL** (the period before, check-ins, applications, bookings) equal counts made in JS from every row. The fixture has registrations exactly on each period boundary, in both formats.
- **The plans.** No statement of any read scans a history table, except the two all-time counts from their index.
- **The budgets.** Each read's statement count is pinned.
- **Mutations caught:**
  - the day cut at the first day instead of the N-th;
  - cancelled events left out of the seats;
  - the applications' bound removed (a scan);
  - the arms' `to` bound dropped;
  - the period before counted with `<=`.
