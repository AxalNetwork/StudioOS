## D8536 part 3 — the rest of sign-in, invitations and the alerts under the delivery contract (#8536)

Part 1 is `D8536.md`: the contract (`services/email/deliveryContract.ts`), the ledger `email_send_refs`, sign-in and company invitations. Part 2 is `D8536-p2.md`: signatures, billing and the flagged-score alert. This part moves the senders part 2 listed as remaining. It adds no migration: every new subject kind is a row that already exists, and three inserts now return their id (`RETURNING id`) so the send can name it.

- **Status:** built. Every sender the issue names for sign-in, invitations and the alerts now meets the contract. One email channel is still outside it: `notify()`'s own email (`sendNotificationEmail`), which the recovery notices also fan out through (see **Not moved**).

### New subject kinds

Each is one entry in `SUBJECTS`, with the one query that names its recipient.

| Kind | Flow | Recipient query reads | Attempt |
|---|---|---|---|
| `recovery_link` | sign_in | the ticket's account, while `layer = 'email_magic'` and `status = 'open'` | `1` (one ticket, one issuance) |
| `recovery_notice` | alert | the ticket's account | the notice and its step (below) |
| `recovery_contact` | alert | `auth_trusted_contacts.contact_email`, while `status = 'active'` | `t<ticket id>` |
| `user_verification` | sign_in | `users.email`, while the account holds a verification token | the token's issuance |
| `email_change_confirm` | sign_in | `email_change_requests.new_email`, while neither confirmed nor revoked | `1` |
| `email_change_revoke` | sign_in | `email_change_requests.old_email`, while not revoked | `1` |
| `founder_invite` | invitation | `founder_invites.invitee_email`, while neither accepted nor revoked | `1` |
| `branch_invitation` | invitation | `branch_invitations.email` (the id is its `uid`), while `status = 'pending'` | the token's issuance |
| `partner_invitation` | invitation | `partner_invitations.recipient_email`, while neither signed nor revoked | the token's issuance and the occasion (below) |
| `totp_enrolment` | alert | the account a `totp_enrolled` activity row names | `1` |
| `compliance_notice` | alert | the addressee an `admin_notices` row names (the id is its `uid`) | `1` |

A token's **issuance** is `issuanceOf(token)`: the first 16 hex characters of its SHA-256, as part 2 did for signing links. A replaced token is a new attempt, and the ledger never holds the token.

### Sign-in

**The account-recovery email link** (`POST /auth/recover/email/start`):
- The link is sent under `sign_in:recovery_link:<ticket>:1`.
- The bell is no longer mirrored. The person cannot sign in, and the row would hold a usable link.
- Once the send is accepted for delivery, earlier open `email_magic` tickets for the account are marked `superseded`, so `/email/verify` answers `invalid_or_used` for their links. A refused send leaves the person the link they already have. The response is `{ ok: true }` either way, as before, so nothing is enumerated.

**The email-verification link.** All four callers now send under `user_verification:<account>:<issuance>`:
- registration (`sendVerification` in `routes/auth.ts`);
- the admin resend (`services/verificationResend.ts`);
- the TOTP remediation sweep (`services/totpRemediation.ts`);
- and, with their own subjects, the email change and the co-founder invitation (below).

Rotating the token is a new attempt, and the old link stops verifying because its hash is overwritten.

Registration used to fall back to the legacy sender whenever the unified send was not delivered. That could mail the link a second time after a send that timed out. Now:
- a send refused for a transient reason (`provider_auth`, `provider_refused`, read from the ledger row) is tried once more under the same reference;
- a refusal for any other reason, or a send that timed out, is not repeated.

The bell is not mirrored for this email either, for the same reason as the recovery link.

**The email change** (`POST /settings/email-change/request`):
- The request row is the subject. The confirm link goes to the new address (`email_change_confirm`) and the revoke link to the old (`email_change_revoke`).
- A newer request already revokes the older one, so the older links have no recipient and are refused at confirm.
- Both use the body they were always sent with (`verificationEmailContent`).

### Invitations

- **Co-founder** (`POST /settings/founder/invites`): the invitation row is the subject, with the body it was always sent with.
- **HQ move** (`rpc/branchOps.ts`, `sendInvitation`): sent through `sendCompanyInvitationEmailBound`, with the content unchanged. A move re-sent under the same `move_ref` mints a new token, so it is a new attempt.
- **Partner** (`routes/admin_partners.ts`, create and resend): D923 re-sends the same link on purpose; the token never changes and a resend only extends its expiry. So, as for a signing email, the attempt is `<issuance>.<occasion>`, built by `partnerInvitationRef`:
  - `send` for the email at creation;
  - `resend-YYYYMMDDHH` for an admin's resend.

  A second click within the hour after an accepted send answers `email_sent: true` from the ledger without a second email. A resend the next hour is its own send. When the invitation's signing link is pending, a resend still renews that link through the part 2 signing reference (D927), as before.

### Alerts

- **Recovery notices** (`notifyAllChannels`, `transitionTicket`). The templated email is sent under `alert:recovery_notice:<ticket>:<notice>.<step>`. The address is the ticket's account, not the `user` argument, which on some paths fell back to `'unknown'`. The steps are:
  - `started.open` when a ticket opens;
  - then the status the transition wrote and, where the step counts people, how many: `started.awaiting_contacts.1`, `started.awaiting_admin`, `started.awaiting_admin_cosign.2`, `resolved.resolved`, `resolved.denied`;
  - `claim.trusted` and `claim.admin` for the two claim emails.

  Each step mails once, and a retry of the same step reconciles.
- **Trusted-contact invites** (`/trusted-contact/start`): each active contact is invited once per ticket.
- **The authenticator-added alert** (`POST /settings/totp/enrol/confirm`): the `totp_enrolled` activity row is now written with `RETURNING id`, and the alert names that row.
- **The compliance notice** (`admin_licences_notices.ts`): the notice row is the subject. `email_sent` keeps its meaning (`ok`, which is true once queued).

### Not moved

- **`notify()`'s own email channel.** `notifyAllChannels` also calls `notify()` with `channels: ['in_app', 'email', 'slack']`. Its email goes through the generic `sendNotificationEmail` (`services/notify.ts`), which has a free recipient and no reference, so the recovery notices still reach the account holder twice: once templated (now referenced), once plain. `sendNotificationEmail` is the generic sender part 1 listed with mixed callers. Moving it, or dropping `email` from this fan-out now that the templated email is referenced, is the owner's call.
- **The claim emails carry no claim link in their body.** `auth_recovery_resolved` has none, and the link (`ctaUrl`) only reaches the bell (`notifications_inbox.cta_url`). The person cannot sign in, so they cannot see the bell. This predates the contract and is unchanged here. Fixing it changes what the email says.
- **Both answered (2026-11-27: 1B and 2A) and built in `D8536-p4.md`.**

### Tests

`cloudflare-worker/test/email_delivery_contract_8536_part3.test.ts` (19 tests). It runs over the migrated schema, with Gmail stubbed at fetch.

- **Recovery link** (through the route):
  - the link's host matches the sending environment;
  - a replacement retires the earlier link, and an expired link answers `expired`;
  - a refused replacement keeps the earlier link;
  - refused, then accepted-but-timed-out: one email under one reference;
  - the recipient is the ticket's account;
  - no token is in a log line or a row.
- **Recovery notices:** each step is its own send, a settled step reconciles, and refused-then-timed-out is one email. Trusted contacts: one invite per active contact per ticket, and a removed contact has no recipient.
- **Verification** (through `resendVerification` and `GET /verify-email`):
  - the host matches the sending environment;
  - the recipient is the account's own address, not the caller's copy;
  - a replacement link makes the old one fail;
  - an expired link is refused;
  - refused-then-timed-out is one email;
  - no token is left behind.
- **Email change** (through the route): each side goes to the address the row names, on this host; a newer request voids the older links; refused-then-timed-out is one email.
- **Co-founder, HQ move, partner:**
  - the row names the address;
  - a revoked or non-pending invitation has none;
  - a re-minted HQ token is a new attempt;
  - the partner `send` and `resend` occasions are separate sends, and a resend within one hour reconciles, through the route too;
  - refused-then-timed-out is one email.
- **Authenticator added and compliance notice:** the row names the addressee, an unrelated activity row names nobody, and refused-then-timed-out is one email.

The mutation check ran 21 mutations: 20 caught, 1 equivalent.

The 20 caught:
- a fixed host;
- not retiring earlier links;
- retiring them regardless of the send's answer;
- dropping `status = 'open'` (`recovery_link`), `status = 'active'` (`recovery_contact`), `revoked_at IS NULL` (`founder_invite`), `status = 'pending'` (`branch_invitation`), the signed/revoked exclusion (`partner_invitation`) or `action = 'totp_enrolled'` (`totp_enrolment`);
- mirroring the recovery link into the bell;
- logging the recovery or verification link;
- a notice attempt without its step;
- claiming a `timed_out` row;
- the verification resend trusting the passed address;
- a constant verification attempt;
- swapping the email-change kinds;
- `partnerInvitationRef` ignoring the occasion;
- the partner create or resend route sending unreferenced.

The equivalent one is a random attempt on the partner create, which sends once per call either way.
