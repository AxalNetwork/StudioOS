## D4860 — HQ › Platform v2: one health state, a ranked attention queue, and freshness on every block (#4860)

- **Status:** canvas landed 2026-11-07 (`design/incoming/Platform v2 Page.dc.html`, `design/incoming/Platform v2 · Boards.dc.html`, boards PL01 to PL12). Not built. The build is a separate task.
- **Builds on:** D203 (switches), D209 (topology), D1170 (storage), D4559 (capacity); `admin_platform.ts`'s per-block states.
- **Migration:** none expected.

### The question

`/admin/platform` is technically honest ("unknown is not zero"), but important status is buried in long explanatory sections. The operator cannot tell in seconds whether the platform is healthy, what is broken, what needs attention and where to fix it.

### What the canvas decides

**One overall health state:** Healthy · Degraded · Unknown · Critical. It is not derived from cron alone; unknown or unavailable telemetry lowers confidence and says why in one line under the state.

**Needs attention** is a severity-ranked queue (Critical, High, Medium, Low, Info). Each item has a description, state, how long, last observed and one remediation action. When nothing readable is wrong but signals are Unknown, it says so instead of showing an all-clear.

**Four compact core-system blocks** (Infrastructure, Payments, AI, Integrations). Each shows status, a key metric, a few key/value rows, freshness (observed time with fresh, stale or unknown) and a console link.

**Fixed status vocabulary:** Healthy, Degraded, Critical, Unknown, Disabled, Not configured, Stale, Partial, Available. It is always a symbol plus words. Switches are named by business meaning ("Session charging: Disabled — no member is charged per session"), never "X off: off".

**Webhook health** has eight fields: health, last delivery, last success, last failure, consecutive failures, silence threshold, expected activity and endpoint. Zero deliveries against expected activity reads as Stale.

**Incidents:** detected incidents and published status incidents are separate. "0 published" is worded as manual-only, never a green KPI.

**Integration keys** show metadata only (requirement, configured, rotated, expiry, last good and failed use, verification freshness, rotation due), never secret material.

**Broadcast** distinguishes Verified recently from merely Configured.

**Flags and switches** split into Active overrides, Deployment configuration and Runtime safety systems, each row with scope, current user impact and last changed.

**Navigation:** consoles grouped by domain. Features that do not do what they advertise (Event syndication: "Not built yet: nothing posts") sit in Preview / Planned.

**Palette:** emerald and neutral system palette with ink primary buttons, and a strong dark mode. **Eadwyn** stays collapsed unless asked, pinned, or carrying an actionable insight (PL12).

### Data

- Integrations, scheduled jobs (`triggerState`, `STALE_GRACE_MINUTES`), monitoring, broadcast and switches come from `GET /api/admin/platform/summary` and `/switches`. Capacity comes from `/capacity` (D4559). Topology comes from `/topology`.
- The overall health rule is new logic over those reads. Proposal: Critical if a user-facing capability is failing; Degraded if any block errors or telemetry is unavailable; Unknown if core blocks cannot be read; Healthy only when every core block was read within its freshness threshold.
- Webhook "expected activity" needs a baseline (drawn as ≥ 1/day from the 30-day checkout volume); confirm the source.

### What the canvas does not decide

The sample content follows the live-state examples in the brief. Which 4 providers are connected, which 5 keys are unset, and the broadcast counts are assumptions to confirm.

### The build (#4860, S22)

`/admin/platform` now leads with the canvas's overview, `frontend/src/pages/hq/PlatformOverview.jsx` over `platformOverviewModel.js`. The page's existing zones stay below it under **System detail**, as the detail each block summarises. Like the rest of the page, the overview has no handler: every action is a link to the console that does it, and every caveat is a native `<details>` disclosure ("How this is measured", "Why this may be unknown", "View sync history").

**Order (PL-01):** header with version context, then Platform health, then Needs attention beside Active controls and Capability coverage, then Core systems, then Operational activity beside Detailed consoles. The indigo "All subsidiaries" band, which led with the cron sentence, is gone (finding 1). The cron line is still the rail's coverage line.

**Health rule, as built.** Each read becomes a signal (`signals`). The core signals are telemetry (traffic by branch, the Analytics Engine read), scheduled jobs, dead letters, integrations and the Stripe webhook.
- **Critical** when a known capability is failing: a branch that does not answer or whose database fails, a webhook refused `WEBHOOK_CRITICAL_RUN` (3) times in a row with no success since, a dead-letter backlog of `DLQ_CRITICAL` (100) or more, or the AI budget tripped.
- **Unknown** when the summary cannot be read, or when both scheduled jobs and the dead-letter queue cannot be read.
- **Degraded** when anything else is wrong, or when a core signal is unknown. An unread signal lowers confidence and is named in the line under the state. It never counts as zero.
- **Healthy** only when every core signal was read on this visit and is healthy.
- Configuration items (an unset recommended key, an unbound channel, X not provisioned, a partly inactive catalogue, the ticket mirror off) appear in Needs attention as Low or Info. On their own they never lower health.

**Needs attention** ranks Critical, High, Medium, Low and Info, keeping read order within a severity. Each item has a severity, description, state, "since" where a stored stamp gives one, when it was observed, and one link to its console. On a phone, Low and Info items are collapsed under "N more · Low and Info" (PL-08 A). When the queue is empty but some signals are Unknown, it says this is not an all-clear and names those signals (PL-02 C).

**Webhook health** (finding 5). `services/stripeWebhookDeliveries.ts` now also returns `last_success`, `last_failure` and `consecutive_failures`: the failures after the newest 2xx, counted up to `CONSECUTIVE_FAILURE_CAP` (1000), with `consecutive_failures_capped` set when the count reaches the cap. A delivery with no recorded status counts as neither a success nor a failure. The webhook's states:
- **Healthy** only when the newest delivery was 2xx and came within the 24 h silence threshold.
- **Stale** when no delivery came within the window. "0 not 2xx" never makes it healthy.
- **Unknown** when no delivery was ever recorded.
- **Degraded** when the newest delivery failed.
- **Critical** on a run of 3 or more failures.

Expected activity is drawn as Not recorded, because no baseline is stored (question C). The additive fields also reach Payments' PAY5 payload, which ignores them.

**Version context** (finding 15). There is a new route, `GET /api/admin/platform-build` (`routes/admin_platform_build.ts`, self-mounted, Super Admin only). It returns the commit the deploy stamped as `WORKER_DEPLOY_VERSION` (hex only, or null with its reason) and the `ENVIRONMENT` var. `deployed_at` is always null with its reason, because no version-metadata binding is declared, and this PR does not change `wrangler.toml`. The release tag is the SPA's own (`lib/release.js`).

**Switches by business meaning** (finding 9, PL-06). `switchRows` names each registry switch by what it means: Eadwyn Available or Unavailable, AI budget protection Normal or Tripped, Session charging, Stripe Tax and Cloudflare Queue Enabled or Disabled. Each row states its current impact and is grouped as follows:
- **Active override:** HQ has thrown it.
- **Runtime safety system:** HQ can throw it, or the platform sets it.
- **Deployment configuration:** set at deploy.

The read-only Feature flags list below keeps its registry words. Its tests pin them (D202, D203).

**Key levels** (finding 3). `KEY_LEVEL` levels the fourteen managed keys. Recommended: `gcip`, `github`, `telegram`, `stripe`. Optional: the ten integration keys. None is Required (question B). The Integration keys panel shows each key's level. An unset Optional key raises nothing.

**Broadcast** (finding 11). `channelState` maps a channel to Unbound, Disabled, Error, Verified recently (tested within 24 h, with no error on the row), Verification stale or Configured. The detail list keeps its "Ready" chip (pinned by D202), and adds a "Verification:" line under it.

**Capability coverage** (finding 10) comes from the switch registry's counts (market sources, diligence connectors), the integrations read, the broadcast read and the deployments registry. A read that did not answer shows Not recorded, never 0.

**Operational activity** lists events with a stored time from the reads above: webhook deliveries and refusals, the catalogue mirror's last write, failed jobs, HQ switch throws and releases, key saves, failed ticket mirrors and branches not answering. Deployments of HQ itself and detected incidents are not recorded, and the feed says so.

**Detailed consoles** (finding 12, PL-07) are grouped as Infrastructure, Payments, AI, Integrations, Controls and Product. Where a console's signal is not Healthy, its link carries that signal's state. Event syndication sits only under **Preview / Planned** (finding 17).

**GitHub Sync** (finding 8) shows the target, its 24 h, the last successful and last failed sync among the latest attempts, and the rest behind "View sync history →".

### Where the build departs from the canvas, and why

- **No Refresh button.** The page has no handler (the `hq_content_platform_h6` guard). The Unreadable cards keep their Try again buttons, and reloading the page re-reads everything.
- **Telemetry unavailable is Medium, not Critical.** This follows the canvas (PL-01, Medium) over the brief's list. Health still reads Degraded.
- **AI block.** "Monthly AI budget used" and "model routing health" are not read by this page, so they say Not recorded and link to AI usage. Eadwyn, budget protection and question ranking come from the switch registry.
- **Integrations.** There is no per-provider table with last success and last failure (PL-04). The integrations read counts connections by provider and status only. Rotation, expiry, last use and verification freshness are not stored for any managed key, so none is drawn.
- **Webhook strip.** There is no 14-day delivery strip on the overview. PAY5 on Payments draws the hourly strip.
- **Version and incidents.** Deployed at shows Not recorded (see above). Detected incidents show Not recorded until #4854.
- **Controls.** There is no Maintenance mode row and no payments or broadcast kill switch. The platform has none (`services/platformSwitches.ts`), and the canvas's sample rows are not invented.
- **Consoles.** The groups use the consoles the app routes. There is no "Data sources" group, because there is no market-sources or connectors console. Their counts are under Capability coverage.
- **Phone (PL-08 B).** Core systems become disclosure rows. Controls, coverage and consoles stack rather than collapsing into three rows.

### Emergency control plane (needs the owner's call, question A)

The one operator kill switch is Eadwyn's (`eadwyn_off`), and it lives in D1 (`platform_switches`, migration 283). During a D1 incident:
- A **read** keeps the last good reading per isolate. With none, it fails **open**, so Eadwyn keeps answering.
- A **throw** cannot be written.
- The break-glass is the deploy var `ADVISOR_DISABLED`. It is read first, with no database, but changing it needs a deploy.

So the switch is not available in the failure domain it would most likely be thrown in.

- **A. Keep D1, and document it.** This is the status quo, plus this note and a line on Switches saying a D1 incident needs the deploy var.
- **B. Mirror operator switches into the KV namespace that is already bound.** On a throw, write KV first and D1 second, for the audit trail. The gate reads KV before D1. No new binding is needed and `wrangler.toml` is untouched. The cost is KV's eventual consistency, up to about 60 s across locations, which is about the existing per-isolate TTL.
- **C. A dedicated control-plane binding.** A new KV namespace or Durable Object, scoped to emergency switches only. It needs a `wrangler.toml` change and an owner-created resource.

**Pick: B.** It removes the D1 dependency from both the throw and the read, at no new infrastructure, and D1 keeps the audit row. This PR builds none of the three. It is `Part of #4860`, and the issue stays open for this call.

### Key lifecycle

The platform records, per managed key, where it lives (Worker secret, database, unset or unknown) and when the console last saved it (`last_set_at` with its basis). That is all the overview shows. Last rotated beyond that save, expiry, last successful and failed use, verification freshness and rotation due are **not recorded** anywhere, so the overview draws none of them and never reads a key's value. Warning before a provider expiry needs a store for the provider-reported expiry. That waits on the owner (question D).

### Built (part 2), 2026-11-08: the Eadwyn rail on the operations consoles (finding 14, PL-12)

Part of #4860. Part 1's "Not done" said finding 14 was a shared-component change. Part 2 makes it opt-in, so the change stays within this issue:

- `WorkerRail` takes `operations`. On a rail with it, the rail starts as its spine on every visit. Clicking the spine opens it for that visit, and an open rail offers **Pin open on this page**. A pinned page opens with the rail open. `ui/railOperations.js` holds the rule (`railCollapsed`) and the pins (`worker_rail_pinned_pages`, a list of paths in this browser's storage, read through `lib/storage`).
- An operations rail never reads or writes the shared collapse preference (`worker_rail_collapsed`, D406). Collapsing Platform's rail does not collapse a founder desk's, and a member who keeps the rail open elsewhere still sees Platform collapsed. A detail sheet still takes the spine (D1317).
- The hosts are the Operations group of finding 12: Overview (`/admin/platform`), Monitoring (both tiers), Topology and Switches. Every other rail is unchanged, and the pin is drawn only on an operations rail.

**Where it departs from PL-12:**
- **No insight opens the rail.** The canvas opens it for an actionable operational insight, a correlation of deploy and webhook logs. No store records such an insight, and the platform does not record HQ's own deploys (part 1's "Operational activity"). So the rail opens only when asked or pinned. When an insight source exists, it can open the rail through the same `visitOpen` state.
- **The spine is the rail's existing spine** (mark, AI dot, spend, toggle), not the canvas's "E · Ask" glyph. The toggle's title on an operations rail reads "Open Eadwyn: Ask about <workspace>".
- **Per browser, not per account.** The pin is a per-viewer convenience in local storage, like the shared preference it sits beside. It is not stored on the Worker.

The suite was mutation-checked: making `railCollapsed` ignore `operations` fails 4 of the 7. `sheet_d1317.test.mjs` now pins the sheet rule through `railCollapsed` instead of the old one-line expression.
