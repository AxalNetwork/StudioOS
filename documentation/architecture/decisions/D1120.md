## D1120 — Program grants: send a grant's schedule for signature and record its signing (#3000)

- **Status:** built by S08, 2026-10-25 (#3000, part of #2376, under D982 §4 and §6).
- **Migration:** none. Migration 553's `schedule_contract_uid` and `schedule_signed_at` gain their writers.
- **Why:** D982 §4 left `schedule_signed_at` with no writer, so no grant under the signed-schedule rule (on by default, D981 §5) could ever come into force. #2778 (part 6) draws GR3's Send schedule and the Schedule column, and it is frontend-only, so the Worker part is this one.

### 1. The send

- **The route** is `POST /api/admin/program-grants/licences/:uid/:program/schedule/send`. It sits beside the other grant writes, under the same `/licences/:uid/:program` path. The issue's `:licenceUid/:key/schedule/send` is the same address, without `licences/`.
- **Who may send.** `requireSuperAdminWriteBar` (a TOTP session, a fresh step-up, the elevation) and a reason of at least 10 characters, as every grant write takes.
- **What it leaves:**
  - one `licence_events` `program_changed` row, `{ action: 'schedule_sent', program_key, changed, reason }`, like the other grant writes;
  - one admin audit row, `program_grant_schedule_sent` (`logAdminAction`);
  - a push of the licence to its branch (D984).
- **What it refuses, in this order:**
  1. A suspended, terminated or draft licence answers `licence_frozen`. Sending a schedule is a change, so the D981 §2 freeze applies.
  2. A program that is not granted, or has ended, answers `wrong_state`.
  3. A grant whose rule is recorded as off answers `schedule_not_required`.
  4. A schedule that is already signed answers `already_signed`.
  5. A schedule that is already out answers `already_sent`. A new send is allowed only once that envelope is `void` or `rejected`.
  6. A licence with no active administrator account answers `no_signer`.

### 2. The document

- **Built from the grant's terms at the send** (`services/programSchedule.ts`, `scheduleDocument`). It holds the program, the licence reference and studio, every term that applies to the program, and HQ's note. It is sent as that rendered text (`createAndSendEnvelope`'s `renderedBody`), so the studio signs exactly those terms.
- **A term nobody set prints "Not recorded"** (D797). A recorded zero prints as a zero.
- **No merge slot survives.** Any `{{` in the studio's names or the note is broken up, so the send's unfilled-field check never fires on the studio's own text.
- **The document type is `program_schedule`.** No template is stored in the library: the text is the grant's terms, not a counsel-drafted instrument.

### 3. The signer

- **The licence's principal administrator** signs, the same way the licence contract's send chooses (`licence_admins`, principal first, active accounts only). The licence's `signatory_name` is the recipient name only when the account has no name. `territory_licences` holds no signatory email, so the account is the only address there is.

### 4. What is stored

- **`schedule_contract_uid` holds the envelope's `envelope_uuid`.** The envelope is the schedule's contract.
- **No `licence_contracts` row is written.** That table is the licence's master agreement, and instantiating a new contract there supersedes every current row of the licence.
- **The envelope's `deal_id` is the grant's id.** This keeps two programs' schedules for one signer from collapsing into one pending send under D410's key. The licence contract send does the same with its contract id.
- **The name is written only while the schedule is unsigned** (`... WHERE id = ? AND schedule_signed_at IS NULL`), so a send that races a signature never replaces the signed schedule.

### 5. The signing

- **`onProgramScheduleEnvelopeCompleted(env, envelopeUuid)`** sets `schedule_signed_at` on the grant naming that envelope, only while it is null. Once it changes, it leaves one `program_changed` event (`action: 'schedule_signed'`, no actor) and pushes the licence to its branch. A second completion changes nothing.
- **Two places call it:**
  - the in-house signing route's completion block in `routes/esign.ts`, inside the `wonRace` branch, for `program_schedule` envelopes only, in its own try, so the signature never fails;
  - the DocuSign poller in `integrations/providers/docusign.ts`, beside `satisfyObligationFromEnvelope`, because that path does not share the in-house block. The send always uses the in-house flow (`viaProvider: 'native'`), so this second call is a safeguard.

### 6. The read

- **Each grant's `schedule` now carries a `status`:**
  - `not_sent`: no envelope named;
  - `sent`: an envelope named, not signed;
  - `signed`: `schedule_signed_at` set.
- `contract_uid` and `signed_at` are unchanged. `waiting_on` still lists `schedule_signed` until the signature, and a grant waiting on nothing else then reads `in_force: true`.

### 7. Left open

- **A change of terms after the signature** does not clear the schedule today (D982's PATCH is unchanged). Whether new terms need a new schedule is the owner's call.
- **A voided envelope** leaves the grant reading `sent` until HQ sends again. The read does not look up the envelope's state.

### UNVERIFIED until the owner's live check

- That the schedule email reaches a real licence administrator, and that the in-house signing page renders the schedule's text.
- That the completion block runs the hook on production D1, with `program_grants` present: migration 553 is applied there.
- That the branch push after the signature lands on a deployed branch.

### Tests

- **`cloudflare-worker/test/program_schedule_d1120.test.ts`** has six tests:
  - The send refuses a plain admin, a stale step-up and a missing or short reason, and writes nothing.
  - The schedule carries the terms and goes to the principal administrator, and its envelope is named on the grant. The send leaves both the events and the audit row.
  - Every refusal in §1, and the resend after a void.
  - The signature is recorded once. A second completion keeps the first time, and the grant comes into force.
  - Both completion paths call the hook.
  - The text rules and the status words.
- **Mutation check:** 17 mutations were tried, among them dropping the `IS NULL` guards, each refusal, the audit row, the not-recorded line, the `{{` guard, either status branch and either hook call. Every one was caught.
