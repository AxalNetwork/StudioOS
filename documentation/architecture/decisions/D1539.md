## D1539 — A deck version as a list of card hashes: card blobs, pruning, and the renditions contract (deck v2 B13)

- **Status:** built by S24, 2026-11-02 (#3789, deck v2 B13, part of #3716), from S1's final canvas revision on #3789. Renditions are specified and NOT switched on (§4).
- **Basis:** `origin/main` at `d13839c4a3`.
- **Builds on:**
  - D1500 (#3750): a card's stable `uid` and model v2;
  - D1501 (#3751, A9): the working copy and `If-Match` on the save;
  - #3748 (N3): the atomic, stale-base save (`insertVersion`'s one batch);
  - D1556: owner decision L = A, Cloudflare spend approved item by item with its monthly cost stated first.
- **Migration:** 730 (`730_pitch_card_blobs.sql`), the number #3789 gives. It sits below the highest migration on `main` (763); the deploy applies every migration its ledger has not recorded, so the order does not matter here (it creates one table and adds one column).

### Why

Every saved version copied every card into `pitch_decks.slides`. A 60-card deck saved ten times held 600 cards, though most were the same card ten times. Keeping each distinct card once, and a version as the list of its cards, stores a save as the cards it changed.

### What was decided

1. **Card blobs, content-addressed.** `pitch_card_blobs (hash PRIMARY KEY, json, created_at)`.
   - `hash` is the lowercase hex SHA-256 of the card's **canonical JSON**: keys sorted at every depth, no whitespace (`services/decks/cardBlobs.ts` `canonicalJson`, `cardHash`). The same card hashes the same whatever its key order.
   - `json` is the card as first written (`JSON.stringify` of the stored card), so a version reassembled from blobs reads as it was saved.
2. **A version is a list of hashes.** `pitch_decks.card_hashes` holds the version's hashes in card order, as a JSON array.
   - **The writer.** `insertVersion` (every save, restore, New deck's keep, the check's fix and the Google Slides import) hashes the stored cards and writes, in the one D1 batch #3748 made atomic: the stale-base `UPDATE`, the version `INSERT` with its `card_hashes`, then one `INSERT OR IGNORE INTO pitch_card_blobs … FROM json_each(?) WHERE changes() = 1`. A card already stored adds no row. A stale save writes neither the version nor a blob.
   - **The transition.** `pitch_decks.slides` is still written in full on every save. Every reader other than `getDeckRow` (the share link, the print page, the exports, the engagement read) still reads it, unchanged.
   - **The reader.** `getDeckRow`, and so `GET /api/decks/:id` and `POST /api/decks/:id/restore`'s source and reply, plus `versionConflictBody`'s `server` copy, reassemble the slides from the hashes (`hydrateSlides`: one `SELECT … WHERE hash IN (SELECT value FROM json_each(?))`). A version written before 730 (`card_hashes` NULL) reads its own `slides`, as does one whose blobs are not all there.
   - **Two writers stay legacy.** The advisor's first-deck seed (`services/advisor/writeRouter.ts`) and the deck import (`routes/imports.ts`) insert `pitch_decks` rows directly, with no hashes; those rows read from `slides` like any pre-730 version, and their next save writes hashes.
   - **The reply shapes are unchanged.** `GET /api/decks/by-project/:pid` reads no slides and answers exactly as before, `proposal` included (N1 #3878). `POST /api/decks/:id/restore` takes no body and answers `rowToDeck` as before; `card_hashes` never leaves the Worker. The test compares the restore reply byte for byte with the version's own `slides` read. No frontend file changes.
3. **The prune rule.** A blob is deleted when **no `pitch_decks` row names its hash** and it was **first written more than an hour ago** (`PRUNE_MIN_AGE_MINUTES`), at most 500 a run (`pruneCardBlobs`, daily at 05:50 UTC in the scheduled handler).
   - **Safe against a save in flight.** The prune is one `DELETE` statement and a save is one batch, and D1 runs each whole, so the prune sees a save's version and blobs together or neither. The hour's grace also covers any future writer that puts a blob in ahead of its version.
   - **What frees a blob.** Only deleting a version does, and only a project's hard delete (`services/projectTrash.ts`) deletes versions. **Versions are never pruned**: every version stays readable and restorable, as before.
4. **Renditions: the contract, not switched on.** A card's picture at 256 and 1024 px wide (`RENDITION_SIZES`), keyed by card hash in the existing `FILES` bucket at `deck-cards/renditions/<size>/<hash>.png` (`renditionKey`), so an unchanged card is rendered once across versions.
   - **The seam.** `GET /api/decks/:id/cards/:hash/rendition/:size`, for the version's readers (`projectOwned`), for a hash the version names. While `RENDITIONS_SWITCHED_ON` is false it answers 404 `rendition_not_available` with `switched_on: false`; a size other than 256 or 1024 is 400 `invalid_rendition_size`.
   - **Why not on.** Producing a rendition means rendering the card in a browser (the existing `BROWSER` binding, the way the PDF and PowerPoint exports photograph the print page) and storing the PNGs in R2. That is new spend on existing bindings, which D1556 says the owner approves first. No bucket, binding or `wrangler.toml` line is added. Nothing renders, writes or fakes a rendition.
   - **Estimated monthly cost, for the owner's approval.** Cloudflare's list prices as known when this was written (confirm on Cloudflare's pricing pages before approving): Browser Rendering $0.09 per browser-hour after the 10 hours a month Workers Paid includes; R2 Standard $0.015 per GB-month after 10 GB, Class A (writes) $4.50 per million after 1 million, Class B (reads) $0.36 per million after 10 million. Assumed: about 3 s of browser time per changed card (both sizes from one page load), 225 KB per card (25 KB at 256 px, 200 KB at 1024 px), two writes per card and 50 thumbnail reads per card. Before the free allowances, which the exports may already use:

     | Changed cards a month | Browser | R2 writes | R2 reads | New storage | Month one |
     | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
     | 2,000 | 1.7 h ≈ $0.15 | 4,000 ≈ $0.02 | 100,000 ≈ $0.04 | 0.45 GB ≈ $0.01 | ≈ $0.21 |
     | 20,000 | 16.7 h ≈ $1.50 | 40,000 ≈ $0.18 | 1 M ≈ $0.36 | 4.5 GB ≈ $0.07 | ≈ $2.11 |
     | 100,000 | 83 h ≈ $7.50 | 200,000 ≈ $0.90 | 5 M ≈ $1.80 | 22.5 GB ≈ $0.34 | ≈ $10.54 |

     Storage accumulates: each month's new storage is billed again every later month, until the renderer also deletes the renditions of pruned blobs. Renders run one at a time from the job queue, so they add no concurrent browsers beyond the included ten. **Ask:** approve the renderer at the volume the owner expects; it is then one constant and the renderer behind it.
   - **The card blobs themselves** use the existing D1 database only: one row written per new card, about 4 KB of hashes per version, one extra read of at most 60 rows per `getDeckRow`. At 20,000 changed cards a month of about 2 KB each that is 40 MB and 20,000 rows written a month, inside D1's included 5 GB and 50 million rows written; beyond the allowance it would be under $0.10 a month. During the transition D1 storage grows by the blobs, because `slides` is still written.
5. **The bootstrap.** `ensureCardBlobSchema` creates migration 730's table and column where it has not run, declared exactly as the migration declares them (D235), and `ensureSchema` awaits it. Where it cannot, a version is written and read the old way.

### What stays not built

- Ending the transition: stopping the full `slides` write, moving the other readers to `hydrateSlides`, and backfilling hashes for pre-730 versions. Each is its own task.
- The renderer (§4) until the owner approves its cost; `CardsStrip.jsx` thumbnails reading renditions (optional, #3789's revision).

### Readings for the owner

1. "Snapshot pruning" is read as pruning **unreferenced card blobs**, never versions. If it was meant to also drop old versions (for example, keep the last N), that is a change to what the versions list shows and needs its own decision.
2. The renditions are not switched on; §4's table is the figure to approve.

### Verified

**`cloudflare-worker/test/deck_card_blobs_d1539.test.ts`** (6 tests; the baseline and every post-cutoff migration, 730 included, then the runtime bootstrap on top):
- hash stability: the same card in any key order hashes the same, the hash is SHA-256 of the canonical JSON, the blob keeps the JSON as written, a repeated card is one blob;
- a 60-card deck's tenth version adds exactly its two changed cards (69 blob rows for ten versions), the 58 unchanged cards keep their hashes, `slides` is still written in full, and an unchanged save adds none;
- restore round-trips from a hashed and from a legacy version, the reply is byte for byte the version's own `slides` read, and the versions list keeps its keys, `proposal` included;
- a legacy version reads from `slides`; a hashed version with its `slides` blanked reads from its blobs, and one with a blob missing falls back to `slides`;
- pruning deletes only old unreferenced blobs, never a referenced one nor one inside its grace window, frees a deleted version's own blob, honours its limit, and a stale save writes no blob;
- the renditions seam answers 404 with `switched_on: false`, 400 for another size, 404 for a hash the version does not name, 403 for another member.

**Existing tests run:** the deck save, restore, proposal, notes, router, slide model, forms, gaps, worker, draft, New deck, check, Google Slides, hard delete, model v2, critic, investor links, grid, share and comments suites pass.
