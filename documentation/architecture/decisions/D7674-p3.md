## D7674 part 3 — Reviewed merge, stable redirects, organisation aliases and review decisions (#7674)

**Status:** implemented for review, 2026-11-27. One additive migration (four new tables), one new service, five
routes on the existing router, read changes in `services/networkDirectory.ts`, synthetic tests. No production data is
changed by this PR. This is the item D7674 and D7674 part 2 list as "reviewed merge, redirect, alias and
conflict-resolution actions"; those files are left as they were, and this file supersedes that bullet.

### Why

#7674 requires that "source removal, correction, suppression and reviewed merge preserve independent evidence,
existing requests, private overlays and stable redirects", and that "quarantined entity kinds, competing person/org
identifiers, organization aliases [...] remain inspectable review items". Part 1 made review items readable; nothing
could act on them. This part adds the actions and nothing that needs a new owner policy: no person is published, no
contact field is projected, and nothing is uploaded (#7739 Q3 B stays binding).

### Routes

All under `/api/network/directory`. The read is `requireSuperAdmin`; every write is `requireSuperAdminWriteBar` (a TOTP
session, a recent step-up, then Super Admin) and then refuses a support session (`support_session_refused`), because
it changes a shared identity. Each write takes an `idempotency_key` (8 to 120 of `A-Za-z0-9_.:-`) and a `reason`
(3 to 500 characters); a retried key answers its first result with `200` and `replayed: true` and writes nothing,
and a key reused for a different change is `409 idempotency_key_reused`. A first write answers `201` and writes one
`admin_audit_log` row with ids only.

| Route | Does |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /admin/entries/:uid` | The entry's state, readiness, a yes/no for a verified account or company link (never the account), merges, `merged_into`, `merged_from`, every observation of its source rows and of those of entries merged into it (reasons, disposition, date; no claim or raw cell), the review items naming them with any decision, aliases of every status, and the counts of what it keeps |
| `POST /admin/entries/:uid/merge` | `{ into_uid, review_uid? }`: merge `:uid` away into `into_uid` |
| `POST /admin/reviews/:reviewUid/decision` | `{ decision: kept_separate \| dismissed }` |
| `POST /admin/entries/:uid/aliases` | `{ alias }`: add an organisation alias |
| `POST /admin/aliases/:aliasUid/withdraw` | Withdraw an alias |

### A merge rewrites no evidence

A merge inserts one `network_directory_merges` row, a one-hop `network_directory_redirects` row from the merged-away
entry to the canonical one, and archives the merged-away entry (`publication_status = 'archived'`, which members never
read and the #7672 importer never refreshes). It does **not** move or edit the merged-away entry's source
observations, private source rows, affiliations, members' overlays, introduction requests (#7673), book links or
Research links. The answer reports how many of each the merged-away entry keeps (counted, never selected). Readers
follow the redirect instead:

- `GET /entries/:old` answers the canonical entry with `redirected_from: ':old'`, only while the canonical entry is
  itself visible; otherwise `404`, so a redirect never reveals a hidden entry.
- An entry's associations are its own and those of every entry merged into it, each to the other side's canonical
  entry (which must be visible). The same claim held twice (same other entry, relation, title, currentness and dates)
  is listed once.
- A member's overlay routes on `:old` act on the canonical person. A `GET` returns the caller's own overlay and, as
  `merged_overlays`, the caller's own overlays written on entries merged into it, left where they were written. A
  `DELETE` clears the caller's own overlays on the person and on entries merged into it; another member's row is never
  touched. Two members' notes are never combined.
- Readiness (D7674) reads the merged entity: every source row behind the entry or an entry merged into it. A later
  hold on the merged-away entry's source row blocks publishing the canonical one (the 2026-11-19T14:27Z note). The
  checked observation is the entry's own where it has one.

**Refused, writing nothing:** `merge_into_self` (400); `kind_mismatch` (a person and an organisation are never merged,
so "same LinkedIn on person and organisation" stays a review item); `merge_source_linked` (an entry with a verified
account or company link is never merged away, so linked entries stay protected; merge the other way);
`already_merged`; `merge_target_merged` (names `canonical_uid`); `merge_target_archived`; `entry_not_found` and
`merge_target_not_found` (404). The merge row is inserted only while both entries are still in the checked state, and
every other statement in the batch runs only when that row exists; one entry is merged away at most once (a unique
index), so a concurrent second merge writes nothing and answers `409 entry_changed`.

**Redirects stay one hop.** When a canonical entry is later merged into another, the redirects that pointed at it are
moved to the new canonical entry in the same batch; the merges table keeps the history. A merge into an entry that was
itself merged away is refused rather than chained.

**Organisation names.** Merging organisations adds the merged-away name as an active alias of the canonical entry
(`origin = 'merge'`), so a search by the old name still finds it. People get no alias.

**Reversal is not built.** `from_publication_status` is recorded so a later reviewed un-merge can restore what members
saw; until then a wrong merge is corrected by suppressing the canonical entry (D7674) while a fix is reviewed.

### Review decisions

A review item is decided once, by a new `network_directory_review_decisions` row, and its status moves from `pending`.
The review and both observations it names stay, and `GET /admin/reviews?status=all` still lists it.
`kept_separate` and `dismissed` come from the decision route; `resolved` only from a merge that names the review
(`review_uid`), which must be pending (`review_not_pending`) and must name a source row of one of the two entries
(`review_not_about_entries`). A decided review no longer counts as `open_review` in readiness. A quarantined-kind
review that is dismissed stays a `review` observation with no entry: deciding it creates nothing.

### Organisation aliases

An alias is another name an operator has reviewed (`origin = 'operator'`), or a merged-away name (`'merge'`).
Organisations only (`alias_organisation_only`): this PR keeps no alternative names for people. An alias is trimmed and
single-spaced, 1 to 300 characters (`invalid_alias`), and refused when it reads as an address, a link or a phone
number (`alias_not_a_name`). Its comparison key lower-cases ASCII letters only, as SQLite's `lower()` does, so the SQL
and the service agree. Refused: the organisation's own name (`alias_is_name`), an alias it already has, directly or
through an entry merged into it (`alias_exists`), and a name another unarchived organisation carries as its name or as
an active alias (`alias_conflict`, naming the competing canonical uids for review: merge or keep separate first). An
alias on a merged-away entry is refused (`alias_on_merged_entry`, naming the canonical entry). Members see an
organisation's active aliases, and those of organisations merged into it, in its detail (`aliases`), and a search
matches them. A withdrawal keeps the row with who, why and when, and the alias no longer matches or competes.

### What a member read now carries

`GET /entries/:uid` adds `aliases` (organisation names; `[]` for a person) and `redirected_from` (`null` unless a
merged-away uid was asked for). Overlay reads add `merged_overlays`. Nothing else in the projection changes: still no
account link, source row id, review item, duplicate candidate or private source cell.

### Not built here, and why

- **The people path** (person records, affiliations, Folk-derived organisations, people publication). The owner's
  #7739 Q3 B excludes person records and contact fields from upload, and the 2026-11-21 note says that does not
  authorise the people import. Building it needs an owner policy: which person fields members may read, at what
  visibility, and whether a person can be published at all. That question is on #7674, not guessed here.
- **Restricted-contact projections.** No visibility policy permits one.
- **Un-merge, and source removal of a whole snapshot.** Recorded for later (above); suppression covers a wrong entry.
- **Introduction requests (#7673) following a redirect.** A request keeps the uid it was made on; the member detail
  redirect gives a client the canonical uid for a new request. Changing #7673's reads is its own change.
- **Any UI**, including #7679's review and coverage screens.

### Rollback

Revert the PR. The four tables are additive and can remain; with the code reverted, an archived merged-away entry
stays archived (members do not read it) and its redirect is unused. To bring one back, suppress or republish through
the D7674 routes after a reviewed fix. No forward fix is needed: the migration adds no trigger, drop, rename, data
rewrite or index on an existing table.
