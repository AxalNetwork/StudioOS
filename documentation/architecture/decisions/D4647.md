## D4647 — A reporting default is the period that has ended, a page is a page, and a total is over the population (#4647)

- **Status:** Built for #4647, 2026-11-09. Worker only. Package 5 of 5 of the
  Portfolio audit of 2026-11-06.
- **Builds on:** #4689's `services/listPage` (`pageWindow` / `pageFacts`, the
  house shape for a page and the facts beside it); D1057 (`periodCounts`);
  D463 (the quarter-end `as_of` read on positions).
- **No migration.**
- **Scope.** The Worker half S1 asked for on 2026-11-09: quarter-end and as-of
  semantics, stable history pagination, support totals over the full
  authorized population, and the per-company latest-update read. Summary
  labels, units and the overview presentation wait for the #4817 canvas; the
  sidebar stays with #4473.

### 1. A default period is the one that has ENDED

`GET /api/positions/kpi-compliance` resolved its period with `currentPeriod` —
the period `as_of` falls **inside**. On 2 October that is a Q4 one day old.
Nobody can have reported on it, so every company in the book read as not
reported and `compliance_pct` was near zero by construction: a number about the
calendar, presented as a number about the portfolio.

`completedPeriod` is now the default. It is a function and not a subtraction
because the year transition is the case that gets this wrong: January's
completed month is the previous **December**, and Q1's completed quarter is the
previous year's **Q4**.

The response also states what it measured, because the label alone left a
reader to infer all of it:

- `period_from` / `period_until` — the window, from `periodBounds`, which reads
  the last day of a month off the calendar rather than a table of month lengths
  (February, and leap Februaries, are why);
- `period_complete` / `period_in_progress` — whether the figure is final;
- `period_default: 'last_completed'` when nothing was asked for, and
  `period_now` so the period in progress is still available;
- `timezone: 'UTC'`, which is the zone these are resolved in;
- `population: { basis: 'companies_held_in_book', period }` — the numerator and
  the denominator are over one population and one period, named so the ratio
  can be checked rather than trusted.

An explicit `period=` still asks for any period, the one in progress included,
and a malformed one is refused (400) rather than quietly resolved to something
else.

**One existing test changed.** `reporting_fixes_d1057.test.ts` asserted
`period === '2026-Q3'` for `as_of=2026-09-15`, taking Q3 from the old default.
That case is about `periodCounts` — that a stored `2026-08` counts for Q3 — so
it now names `period=2026-Q3` and keeps every assertion it had, plus two lines
recording that the default is Q2 and that Q3 is still running. Nothing D1057
pinned is weakened; the figures it checks are identical.

### 2. The support ledger's totals were the page's totals

`GET /api/portfolio-support` read `LIMIT 400` and then accumulated **every
figure from those 400 rows**: `delivered`, `promised`, `withdrawn`,
`hours_recorded`, `entries_without_hours`, `companies_supported` and every
per-company count. Past 400 entries each of them described the newest 400 while
reading as the ledger's own totals — and entries beyond them could not be
reached at all, because there was no offset.

- The figures come from their own `GROUP BY` over the whole authorized
  population. The per-company counts therefore do not move when the reader
  turns a page, which they did.
- `totals_basis: 'all_entries_in_scope'` says what they are over.
- The page is a page: `?limit=&offset=` and `page: { offset, limit, total,
  next_offset }`, with the ordering ending in `s.id`.
- `?state=` narrows the **page** so an outstanding promise stays reachable
  however deep the history is. It is deliberately **not** applied to the
  figures: a page of outstanding promises must not change what the ledger says
  it holds. An unrecognised state is ignored rather than answering nothing.
- A failed figures read answers `totals: null` with
  `totals_reason: 'Unreadable — …'` and `page_state: 'unreadable'`. The old
  `.catch(() => ({ results: [] }))` turned a failed read into an empty ledger,
  which is a claim that no support has been given.

### 3. The update history is pageable, and a company's latest is readable

`GET /api/portfolio-updates` stopped at `LIMIT 500` with no offset, ordered by
`COALESCE(submitted_at, updated_at) DESC` alone.

- `?limit=&offset=` with a `total` from its own `COUNT(*)`, so update 501 is
  reachable.
- The order ends in `id`. `COALESCE(submitted_at, updated_at)` is not unique,
  so two updates sharing a timestamp could repeat across pages or fall between
  them.

`GET /api/portfolio-updates/latest` is new: the newest update this caller may
read for each company, or for one with `?project_id=`. There was no way to get
it except by paging a history ordered by time across every company — so a
company whose last update sat behind another company's long run had none, as
far as any caller could tell. The row is picked per company by the database
with `ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY project_id …)`, so the answer does not
depend on where that row sits in the whole history. A company with nothing
readable is **absent**, not present with a null, and `companies` states how
many answered.

**The table is not aliased, on purpose.** `INVESTOR_GRANT` and `TEAM_GRANT` are
written against `portfolio_updates.id` and `portfolio_updates.audience_json` by
name. Aliasing the table would put those columns out of scope and break this
read for exactly the non-admin readers it exists for — while still compiling.
The predicate is used as it stands and bound once. Both routes take it from one
helper, `readableUpdates`, so neither can drift from the other's idea of who
may see what.

### Tests

`cloudflare-worker/test/portfolio_periods_pages_4647.test.ts`:

- the completed quarter for ten dates across every quarter and both year
  edges, and the completed month for five, January included; an unreadable
  date resolves to nothing rather than a guess;
- `periodBounds` for quarters, months, December and a leap February, and the
  three malformed shapes it refuses; `periodComplete` on the exact boundary
  day and the day before;
- 450 entries over two companies: the totals are over all 450 and are
  identical on page one and page two, and so are the per-company counts;
- a full walk in pages of 100 reaches all 450 exactly once;
- `?state=promised` returns only promises and leaves the figures unfiltered;
- a dropped table answers Unreadable rather than an empty ledger;
- the ordering invariant, pinned in the SQL — case 5 cannot prove it, because
  SQLite returns equal-key rows in rowid order for a scan that simple and the
  walk passes with the tiebreaker removed, so what holds under another plan or
  D1's own engine is pinned where it is guaranteed;
- 60 updates over two companies where one company's run is entirely newer:
  `/latest` answers both companies while a page of five reaches only one;
- the compliance wiring, by source, between the rules above and the response.

Mutation-checked both ways: the default back to `currentPeriod`, the year
transition dropped, the month end guessed at 31 days, the totals back to the
page, the tiebreaker removed from each paged read, the history's offset
removed, an unreadable read treated as zero, and `/latest`'s partition
defeated. Each fails a case. The eleven existing suites over these routes
(`partner_access_d889`, `round_calls_d653`, `reporting_reader_d1061`,
`portfolio_support_scope`, `share_update_d654`, `update_lifecycle_d4636`,
`report_figures_d1059`, `publish_sources_d1333`, `investorLifecycle.authz`,
`fill_profile_forms_d1595`, and `reporting_fixes_d1057` as described above)
pass.
