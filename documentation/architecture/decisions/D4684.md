## D4684 — A sent work product keeps the person it was sent to (#4684)

- **Status:** built by S13, 2026-11-06 (#4684, Practice audit 1/6, P0).
- **Migration:** `202611062152_advisor_deliverable_recipients.sql`, named by its UTC minute (D4106).

### The defect

An advisor's sent work products were readable by, and receipted by, the engagement's *current* client (`advisor_engagements.founder_user_id`). Every client route joined through the engagement:

- `GET /api/advisors/received/deliverables`
- `POST …/:uid/open`
- `POST …/:uid/accept`

So relinking an engagement to another account moved everything it had already delivered. The first client lost it. The new client saw it, with the first client's open and sign-off stamps on it. Relinking a work product to another engagement did the same.

### The rule

1. **The recipient is fixed at send.** `POST /me/deliverables/:id/versions/:version/send` writes `recipient_user_id` in the same `UPDATE` as `sent_at`, guarded by `sent_at IS NULL`, so two sends of one version are one send to one recipient.
2. **The client's routes authorise against the recipient alone.** The received list shows a work product with only the versions sent to the caller. Open and accept find the version by `uid` and `recipient_user_id`, and their `UPDATE` repeats the recipient in its `WHERE`. Anyone else's version is still 404, never 403, so the routes cannot be used to enumerate another advisor's or another client's work.
3. **Receipts keep their author.** Open writes `opened_by_user_id` and accept writes `signed_off_by_user_id`, each with its stamp. An accept on an unopened version records the same account as the opener.
4. **Write-once, in the store.** Three triggers refuse to change `recipient_user_id`, `opened_by_user_id` or `signed_off_by_user_id` once set. A relink racing an open cannot move a version or re-attribute a receipt, whatever code runs next.
5. **New work follows the current client.** A version sent after a relink goes to the account linked then, and only that version.
6. **No implicit transfer.** Moving already-sent work to another client is not supported. If it is added later, it is an explicit operation that lists the versions it moves, names both people, asks for confirmation and writes an audit row. It never rewrites a receipt.

### The advisor's side

- **Who each version went to.** `GET /me/deliverables` names each sent version's recipient (`recipient_user_id`, `recipient_name`), and the version trail reads "sent to <name>".
- **Nudges.** `addressable` counts unread rows whose latest sent version's recipient has an address. A note about a work product (`services/mail/moduleRecipients.ts`) goes to that recipient, and to the engagement's client only before anything is sent.
- **Unlinking after a send.** A row unlinked after it was sent stays addressable through its recipient, but shows no client account: nothing new can be sent to it. `advisor_deliverables_scope.test.ts` asserted the old rule for this case, and now asserts this one.
- **The client-account picker** says that changing the account never moves work already sent.

### Versions sent before the migration

They are pinned to the engagement's client as it stood when the migration ran, which is the only record of who they went to. Their receipt actors stay null: who opened them was never recorded, and a guess is not a record.

### Tests

`cloudflare-worker/test/advisor_deliverable_recipients_4684.test.ts` uses synthetic users only. It covers:

- send to A, relink to B: A keeps the version, B inherits nothing;
- v2 after the relink goes to B only;
- A's open and sign-off stay A's, and B is 404 on both;
- a sign-off on an unopened version records the opener;
- unrelated clients and other advisors are refused;
- a relink racing a receipt changes neither, and the triggers refuse to;
- a send racing another send: the loser is 409 and the winner keeps its recipient;
- the migration's backfill.
