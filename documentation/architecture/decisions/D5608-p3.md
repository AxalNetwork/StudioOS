### 4. Overhead: the medians on record do not cover the landed tracer

**What the record holds.** D5608-p2 cites +1.887% frontend (534.134 / 544.213 s) and +2.891% Worker (388.681 / 399.919 s). The previous holder measured these on 2026-11-13 from local paired full-part runs at local head `028b7809`.

**Why they do not cover the landed tracer.** That tracer observed module resolution with a synchronous `module.registerHooks` resolve hook (5cf1f8e4c, `test-tracer.mjs` line 132). #6904 landed a different mechanism (a9d5731ac5, squashed into af019ea02):
- an off-thread `module.register` hook (`test-tracer-hooks.mjs`) that posts each module over a MessagePort, drained at exit;
- `test-tracer-sqlite.mjs`, which wraps `node:sqlite`.

No paired measurement of this code exists.

**What existing hosted runs show.** The test-step durations of a PR's last successful `pull_request` CI run (untraced) were paired with its `merge_group` run (traced).
- The step is `node scripts/test-drift-parts.mjs <part>`. Its time excludes install, build and the trace upload.
- A pair was kept only when every suite part succeeded in both runs and no part was delegated to the MacBook.
- Parts were summed per family. The queue plans with equal weights (D7108-p2) and a PR packs from timings, so single parts are not comparable.
- The same method was then applied to a window before #6904, when neither run traced, to measure the queue-versus-PR gap with no tracer.

Traced window: 60 merged PRs, queue runs from 2026-11-24 22:24Z to 2026-11-25 22:53Z, each a median of 1.7 h after its PR run. The 95% intervals are bootstrap intervals on the paired median.

**What this can show.**
- Hosted traced queue parts are not measurably slower than the untraced PR parts of the same change.
- No family shows a regression larger than about 8%.
- The interquartile range of single pairs is about ±13%.

**What it cannot show.**
- With no tracer in either run, the queue-versus-PR gap was +5.7% (frontend) and +4.5% (Worker).
- With tracing on, the gap is +0.75% and −5.75%. It moved by 5 and 10 points.
- That shift is the size of the 5% limit and has other causes:
  - queue load and runner contention;
  - the PR-to-queue lag;
  - the plan change of 2026-11-18;
  - the other candidates ahead in the queue.
- The comparison therefore cannot separate the tracer from those causes, and no figure from it can stand as the tracer's overhead.

**Verdict.** Done-when item 2 ("measured overhead is 5% wall or less, with medians in D5608") is **not met** for the landed tracer. The 11-13 medians are evidence for the synchronous-hook tracer only.

**The exact gap.** Paired runs on one commit at or after af019ea02, where `TEST_TRACE_DIR` is the only change.
- Command: `node scripts/test-drift-parts.mjs <part> --plan-file <one shared plan>`, with `TEST_TRACE_DIR` set for one run of each pair and empty for the other.
- Parts: `frontend-1-of-2`, `frontend-2-of-2` and the six `worker-*` parts.
- At least three interleaved pairs on one runner class, or on one idle local machine as on 11-13.
- Record the per-family medians and their ratio in a D5608 part 4.

Session 39 did not run this. Its machine was shared with other sessions (load above 5 on 4 cores), and dispatching hosted runs is outside this task. Note that `runner-benchmark.yml` cannot be used unchanged: its trace env is false on a `bench/runners-*` push (D5608-p2).
