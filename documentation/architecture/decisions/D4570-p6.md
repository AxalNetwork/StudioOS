## D4570 part 6 — Money (#4570)

#4570 slice 6, after slices 1 to 4 in `D4570.md` and slice 5 in `D4570-p5.md`. Ten entries move from `unreviewed` to `verified`. Each is covered by a negative tenant and role test in `cloudflare-worker/test/authz_money_d4570.test.ts`. No product code changes.

### Why this family

The slices so far took the ledger's high-risk kinds in turn: share links (slice 3), writes to a record (slice 4), then exports, downloads and signed links (slice 5). The issue names three more kinds: admin and role changes, money movement, and session issuance and invitations.

Admin and role changes are not in the ledger: those routes sit behind an admin or Super Admin gate the inventory can see, and slice 2's sweep refuses a founder and a non-Super admin on each of them. Session issuance is the `sign_in` and `oauth_callback` kinds: the sweep and the sign-in suites cover their own gates, and what is left is review of public-by-design doors. Invitations are a handful of single deletes.

That leaves money. A gap here does more than show a record. It can point a wire at the wrong account, charge a member, settle an invoice that was not paid, remove a member's card, or bind an LP's capital with a signature that is not theirs. The ledger held one set of such routes that one PR can cover end to end, from the same few fixtures: a venture with an open closing, a booked session, a partner engagement, an order, a saved card and a fund.

### The cases

The suite asks each route through `worker.fetch` on the migrated schema. Every person, company, account number, card and amount is synthetic. The receiving account is stored as the route stores it, encrypted with a fixture key. Stripe is a fake that records every call. Charging is switched on in the test environment (`ADVISOR_CHARGING_ENABLED`, a test flag here, never a deployment's), so a charge that got past the gate would reach the billing checks and the payment provider. In production it is off.

For every refusal:

- the status is the gate's, and each route gives one answer to everyone it refuses;
- where the route answers not-found, a non-party's answer for a real record equals its answer for an id that does not exist;
- the body and the `Content-Disposition` header name nothing: no company, payee, bank, account or routing number, invoice, order or LP;
- nothing changes. The suite compares before and after: the receiving accounts and the count of reveal rows, the bookings and their slots, the engagements and invoices, the orders, the LPs and the fund, the commitments, each user's Stripe customer, the audit rows, the activity rows a route writes, and the notifications;
- no payment call is made. The card route reads the card from Stripe only for a caller who holds a customer, and sends no detach.

The last case runs the rightful party on every route. The founder and two investors read the masked instructions, and the signed investor reveals the number, with the reveal row written first. The booking's founder reaches the billing state on both mounts. Both sides read the invoice and the partner marks it paid. The buyer gets the PDF. The card's holder detaches it, and the detach reaches Stripe. The LP signs their own LPA, and no other LP in the fund changes. The retired withdraw still changes nothing. That proves each refusal is the gate and not a missing record. It also proves that a rightful act does write, call or reveal, so the "nothing changed" checks above mean something.

**Mutation checks.** 22 of 22 counted mutations were caught. Each was seen red, then restored:

- `requireAuth` replaced by a fixed party, on the receiving-account read and on the order invoice, one at a time;
- `getCurrentUser`'s `is_active` refusal removed;
- the receiving-account scope's investor clause unbound from the caller, its withdrawn test removed, its primary-admin test removed, its founder clause widened to every founder, and an admin admitted, one at a time;
- the reveal's signed-line insert unbound from the caller, and its signed-recipient test removed, one at a time;
- the receiving-account router's support-session refusal removed;
- the charge's `founder_user_id` test removed;
- the invoice read's partner side widened to any partner, and its founder-side match removed, one at a time;
- the mark-paid partner test removed, and its `partner_id` match removed, one at a time;
- the order invoice's buyer test removed;
- the card detach's customer comparison removed;
- the LPA's owner test removed;
- `requireApprovedKyc` approving every investor;
- the retired withdraw made to withdraw, and its investor role gate replaced by `requireAuth`, one at a time.

One mutation was found equivalent and is not counted: removing the reveal's `role !== 'investor'` test. A founder who passes it still holds no line, so the signed-line insert refuses them. That insert is the authorization point (D1217); the role test is a second wall.

### Hardening items, for the owner

None is exploitable as the code stands, and none is changed here. The suite asserts the routes as they are, so a fix to any of them edits one assertion.

1. **Two refusals tell a non-party the record exists.** `POST /api/engagements/:id/invoice/paid` and `POST /api/funds/lps/:lpId/sign-lpa` answer a non-party 403 and a missing id 404. Both ids are sequential integers, so this tells a member which engagements and LP records exist. Nothing else is named. The invoice read beside the first already answers a non-party with the missing record's 404.
   - Remediation class: answer a non-party as a missing record, as slice 4's calendar fix did. The engagement's own founder may keep the 403, since they already know the engagement exists.
2. **An admin may record any LP's LPA signature.** The route lets an admin through on purpose, and the activity row names the admin. The LP row itself records only that it is signed and when, and its status moves from committed to active. A signature that binds capital would normally be the LP's own act, or be recorded as an attestation by the person who made it.
   - Remediation class: record the signer on the LP row, and limit the admin path to the fund's GP or to an attestation with a reason.
3. **Support sessions are not refused alike across money routes.** The receiving-account routes refuse a support session, and so do token purchases and bank connections. The card detach and the session charge do not. Inside a member's account, a support session can remove the member's saved card, and it reaches the charge's billing checks. Charging is switched off in production. A support session is opened by an admin and recorded. This was seen in a scratch run, and the suite does not pin it.
   - Remediation class: one support-session rule for every route that moves money or removes a member's payment instrument.

### Coverage gaps

- **After the gate.** The charge's billing and payout checks and its Stripe calls are covered by `advisor_charge_route.test.ts`. The reveal's outcome rows, the currency check and the audit-failure paths are covered by D1217's suite, `company_receiving_accounts_d1217.test.ts`. They are not repeated here.
- **The card's owner is Stripe's answer.** The detach compares the card's `customer`, as Stripe reports it, with the caller's. The fake reports it faithfully. A real Stripe error answers 502.
- **Admin reach is by design.** An admin signing an LPA is not exercised: the suite neither asserts it nor questions it (hardening item 2).
- **Writes beside these.** `PUT /api/closings/company-receiving-accounts/:projectId` is not in the ledger. Its gate is visible to the inventory, and D1217's suite refuses every non-owner on it.
- **Money routes of other kinds, left for a later slice.** The secondary listings' proceeds, right of first refusal and matches; quote accept, reject and withdraw; syndicate join and close; a service offering's engage; and referral-code use.
