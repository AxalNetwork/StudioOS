### 1. Re-pointing is a forward re-pin, never a backward move

#5581's head moves only by compare-and-swap to a revision whose parent is the current head. Pointing a line back at
an earlier manifest would break that, and every replay and stale-webhook guarantee part 1 rests on. So **re-pointing
to an earlier manifest is a NEW manifest revision that re-pins the earlier manifest's component revisions**:

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| `repinRelease(line, target)` | cuts a manifest on the current head (its parent), whose body copies the target's components by revision and digest and records `repin: <target>` |
| Its digest | differs from the target's (the body names a new base and the re-pin), so every decision binds the re-pin itself |
| Approval | the earlier approvals bound the earlier digest and do not count; the re-pin needs a fresh publish approval per profile |
| Attestations | the re-pinned revisions are the same revisions at the same digests, so their attestations still hold |
| Publication | `publishRelease` from the current head; the CAS moves the pointer forward (`head_seq` + 1) |

The working heads of the components are not touched: re-pinning the v1 deck does not move the deck's head back from
v2. Only the line's publication pointer changes.

A re-pin is refused for: a manifest the line never published (`not_published`); the current head (`is_head`); one a
takedown covers (`taken_down`); a tombstoned piece (`tombstoned`); a piece the line's audience may no longer see,
read as the asset is classed now (`audience_forbidden`); another tenant (`wrong_tenant`). A replay by its key returns
the first re-pin; the same key for another target is `idempotency_conflict`.

**The #5581 extension.** `createRevision` and `advanceHead` refuse a composite whose component edge is not at its
asset's head (`composite_incompatible`). Both now take an optional `repinnedComponents`: the earlier component
revisions the caller names on purpose. It applies only to a composite's `component` edges. The CAS still requires
every other edge to be at its head, and adds one clause for every caller: no pinned revision may be tombstoned. That
clause changes nothing for an ordinary composite, whose components are heads and so cannot be tombstoned. Without the
option both behave exactly as before; a test shows the CAS refusing the same re-pin when the option is left out.

**Staleness of a re-pin.** `evaluate` exempts only revisions the target manifest pinned. They show the reason
`repinned`, not `superseded` or `input_changed`: the release restores what was published then, as it was built then.
A tombstone or a takedown is never exempt.

### 2. Takedown and retention

`takedownRelease` takes down either:

- **one release**: a manifest, named by its uid and digest; or
- **one component revision** at its digest, wherever it is pinned.

| What | Rule |
|---|---|
| Who | a person (`person_required`), not revoked (`revoked_author`), holding an authority that approves one of the manifest's profiles, or `counsel` or `security_officer`, who may always take content down (`no_authority`) |
| About what | the release identity they saw: the manifest digest, and the component's digest for a component takedown (`stale_approval`); a component the manifest pins (`unknown_reference`) |
| Why | a reason code (`rights_withdrawn`, `consent_withdrawn`, `legal_order`, `security`, `inaccurate`, `policy`) and an evidence ref (ids only) |
| Audit | every takedown, and every refusal once the line and manifest are found (another tenant's included, recorded against the release's own tenant), is a row of `work_graph_release_takedowns` with the actor, the authority, the D1 cost and the outcome |
| Replay | the same key returns the first takedown; the same key for another takedown is `idempotency_conflict`; taking down what is already down changes nothing |

**Retention.** A takedown is permanent and deletes nothing. The manifest, its pins, decisions, attestations, the
component revisions and both audit trails stay. `releaseStatus` and `publishedRelease` keep returning them, with the
takedown listed (`takedowns`) and each covered component marked `taken_down`. That is the historical evidence.

**What stops.**

- `servedRelease` serves nothing while a takedown covers the published manifest or any piece it pins, or a pinned
  piece is tombstoned, and gives the reason. It never falls back to an older manifest on its own; a person re-pins one.
- `publishRelease` refuses a manifest a takedown covers (`taken_down`, audited as part 1's refusals are). A component
  takedown blocks every manifest that pins that revision. A release takedown blocks that manifest only: a new manifest
  of the same pieces is a new release, which needs its own approval.
- `repinRelease` refuses it (§1).
- A delivery handler withholds a queued publish effect of it (§3).
- **Retraction.** The takedown's batch queues one `release_retract` effect for every confirmed delivery of the content
  it covers: the release's, or for a component every release in the company that pins it. Each is guarded on the
  takedown row of that batch. One retraction per manifest, target and ref, whoever queues it. The tenant's outbox room
  is checked first (`queue_full`).

### 3. Delivery handlers

`releaseEffectHandlers(adapters)` returns the handlers for the five `release_<target>` effects and for
`release_retract`, for #5581's `deliverEffect` / `drainEffects`. Each target is a `ReleaseDeliveryAdapter`:

- `lookup(request)`: what the provider already holds under the effect's idempotency key;
- `send(request)`: the write, which must itself be conditional on that key.

No real adapter exists yet; the tests use recording fakes.

**Checked on every attempt, before any provider call (publish).** A failed check consumes the effect with a delivery
row and calls nothing:

| Check | Row |
|---|---|
| the manifest exists, in the effect's tenant and company, on its line | `withheld` / `unknown_reference`, `wrong_tenant` |
| the manifest digest is the effect's | `withheld` / `stale_digest` |
| the line's pointer still names this manifest | `superseded` / `not_head` |
| the effect's audience is the line's | `withheld` / `audience_forbidden` |
| no takedown covers it; no pinned piece is tombstoned | `withheld` / `taken_down`, `tombstoned` |

So a late or replayed effect of an older release never overwrites a newer one at a provider, and a forged effect
naming another tenant's manifest delivers nothing (the "stale webhook cannot publish" criterion, at delivery).

**Ambiguous delivery is reconciled, never repeated.** Each attempt calls `lookup` first:

| `lookup` says | Then |
|---|---|
| `confirmed` | record the delivery (`reconciled = 1`); no send |
| `accepted` | retry later through the outbox's backoff (`awaiting_confirmation`); no send |
| `absent` | `send`; `confirmed` is recorded, `accepted` retries, `absent` is `provider_rejected` (a retraction of nothing is `nothing_to_retract`) |

An adapter error (a timeout after the provider took the write) is retried; the next attempt's `lookup` finds the
write. Only a confirmed delivery is recorded as delivered (D5458). After the outbox's attempts the effect is dead,
kept for a person to read. A missing adapter is `no_adapter`.

**A takedown that races a send.** The delivery row commits in the effect's `applied` batch together with a
`release_retract` insert guarded on a takedown covering the manifest. D1 runs batches one at a time:

- a takedown that committed first is seen by that guard;
- one that commits later sees the confirmed delivery row and queues the retraction itself.

**Cost telemetry.** Each `work_graph_release_deliveries` row carries the D1 statements the handler issued and its
provider calls. Each takedown row carries its D1 statements. Every result carries `cost`, and the outbox's audit rows
carry their own (D5581-p3).

**Not scheduled.** `drainEffects` fails an effect whose kind has no handler, so these handlers must be merged into the
one map a scheduled drain is given. No drain is scheduled, and nothing calls these handlers outside tests.

  `takedownRelease`, and a re-point into `repinRelease` plus `publishRelease`.

### 5. Rollback

Every function is unreachable without a route.

- **Undo the code.** Revert the two new services, the part 1 changes and the `repinnedComponents` option.
  `createRevision` and `advanceHead` without the option behave as before, except that the CAS's new tombstone clause
  also goes. The clause never refused an ordinary composite.
- **Drop the tables.** Dropping `work_graph_release_takedowns` and `work_graph_release_deliveries` loses the takedown
  and delivery records. A re-pin manifest already published stays a valid manifest of its line: its body and edges are
  part 1's and #5581's. Queued `release_retract` effects have no handler once the code is reverted, so nothing is
  delivered.
