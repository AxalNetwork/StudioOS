## D4692 — The slot list says what the booking would say (#4692)

Follow-up to D4695, which enforces the advisor's availability rules at the
booking itself. #4692 asked that what a founder is *shown* agree with what the
booking will *do*; D4695 left the list as it was.

### The failure

`GET /api/advisors/:uid/slots` listed every slot that was not cancelled or
blocked. A slot inside the advisor's minimum notice, inside a blackout, too
close to a session the advisor already holds, or in a week already at the
paid cap was offered, and the founder learned otherwise only from the 409 at
the click.

### The rule

The list asks the same `ruleRefusal` the booking asks, for every slot in one
pass (rules read once, the advisor's held sessions read once, the week's paid
sessions read once). Each item gains two fields:

- `unavailable_code`: `null`, or `min_notice` / `blackout` / `buffer`. These
  hold for any session type, so they are stated per slot.
- `paid_cap_reached`: `true` when a paid session cannot be booked in that
  slot's week. A free or included session can still be booked, so this is a
  separate flag and not a refusal of the slot.

The slot stays in the list: a founder can see that a time exists and why it is
not offered. The booking remains the decision; the list is advice, read at one
instant, and a slot that reads open can still be refused by the INSERT.

### Not done, in that PR

- The frontend does not yet grey out or explain these slots (no screen in this
  PR reads the new fields).
- Recording who confirmed, completed, cancelled or no-showed a booking, and
  the sessions zone's reload offer on a 409, are not in this PR; they are the
  rest of #4692 and stay open there.

### Part 2 (2026-11-09) — who closed the session, and two screens that read what the Worker already answers

- **Status:** Built for #4692, 2026-11-09. It closes the three items left open
  above.
- **Builds on:** the part above (`unavailable_code`, `paid_cap_reached`);
  D4703 (the conditional lifecycle UPDATE and its 409); D159
  (`logAdminAction`); D637 (`logActorAct`).
- **No migration.** Both audit stores already exist.

#### 1. Every lifecycle change records the hand, not only the outcome

`POST /api/advisors/bookings/:id/{confirm,complete,cancel,no-show}` share one
`transition` helper. The row it wrote carried the *outcome* — `status`,
`cancelled_by`, `advisor_decision`, `updated_at` — and named no account. So a
**completed** session, which is the one the advisor is paid on, and a cancel
could not be traced to a person; and an admin's cancel on someone else's
booking reached **no audit store at all**, which is the gap D159 exists to
close.

`transition` now records one line per change:

- an **admin's** press goes to `logAdminAction` — `activity_logs` plus
  `admin_audit_log`, which is the arm HQ's governance feed reads unfiltered —
  with the founder as `target_user_id`, so the feed names whose booking it was;
- the **advisor's** and the **client's** own go to `logActorAct`
  (`activity_logs`, hashed actor).

Three things decide the shape, and each is a defect avoided rather than a
preference:

1. **`adm` alone picks the branch**, exactly as it already picks
   `cancelled_by`. Reading the role twice, differently, would let one press be
   an admin to one store and the advisor to the other.
2. **It is recorded after the conditional UPDATE reports a changed row.** The
   write is conditional on the status the caller read (D4703), so a press lost
   to a race changes nothing and answers 409 — and must leave no record of an
   act that did not happen.
3. **Ids and states only.** The cancel reason is free text the person typed. It
   stays on the booking, because it is what the other side is told, and reaches
   neither audit store — `logActorAct`'s own contract.

Both writers are best effort: the session has already changed, and a lost line
is better than a recorded act undone by its own record.

#### 2. A 409 in Run your sessions is not "that could not be recorded"

`SessionLifecyclePanel` answered every failure with *"That could not be
recorded. The session is unchanged."* After D4703 a 409 means the opposite: the
session **moved** — the founder cancelled it, or a second press got there first
— and this press was refused against a status that no longer holds. The message
was wrong twice over, and it left the advisor pressing the same button against
the same stale list.

A 409 now says the session changed somewhere else, that nothing here was
recorded, and offers **Reload these sessions**, which re-reads through the
panel's existing `onChanged`. Every other failure keeps the old message, which
is true of it: a 500 means the write never happened, and a reload would show
the advisor what they already have.

#### 3. The founder's list reads the verdicts the Worker sends

`AdvisorDetail` (`AdvisorsPage.jsx`) filtered on `isBookableSlot` only, so the
part above's two fields changed nothing a founder saw: the Book button was
still offered on hours the booking was certain to refuse.

- `slotView` carries `unavailableCode` and `paidCapReached`. **Absent reads as
  no verdict**, never as a refusal, so an older response or the FastAPI dev
  backend does not cost a founder a bookable hour.
- `slotUnavailableReason(slot, offer)` turns a verdict into one sentence. The
  paid cap applies only when the chosen offer is priced (`pricing === 'price'`),
  since a free or included session can still take that hour; with no type
  chosen it is not yet that hour's answer. An unrecognised code returns null
  rather than a guess.
- Refused hours are **still listed**, under their reason and marked "Not
  bookable". A time the founder can see and cannot take is worth more than a
  gap, as long as it says why.
- With hours read but none bookable, the list says so. It no longer prints "No
  open slots — check back later", which was a claim about this advisor's
  availability contradicted by the hours in hand.
