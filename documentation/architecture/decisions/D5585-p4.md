### 1. Who the release owner is

A release line has no owner column. Part 1's `work_graph_release_lines` records `created_by_user_id`: the person who
registered the line, or the user an agent acted for. **The release owner is the line's creator.** The owner never
changes with later manifests, so every release of a line goes to the same person.

The owner can sign only while all of these hold. Otherwise the effect is refused `signer_unavailable` and nothing is
sent:

- the account is active (`users.is_active = 1`) and has an email;
- the account is linked to the release's company (`user_company_links`);
- that company is not archived (D1137).

WorkGraph's tenant has no table of its own today. D5579 resolves it through the company readers, so membership of the
release's company is membership of its tenant. Part 2's handler has already checked that the manifest, the line and
the effect share one tenant and company before the adapter runs.

### 2. One envelope per release effect, through the native e-sign service

| Step | What it does |
|---|---|
| Credentials | The native service mails the signing link through Gmail. Without `GMAIL_CLIENT_ID`, `GMAIL_CLIENT_SECRET` and `GMAIL_REFRESH_TOKEN` the adapter refuses `target_not_configured` and calls nothing. |
| Body | `releaseSignBody`: a fixed heading and sentence, then the release line, the manifest uid, `sha256:<digest>`, the effect's ref and its idempotency key. Ids only, no content and no source locators. |
| Send | `createAndSendEnvelope` with `renderedBody`, `viaProvider: 'native'`, document type `work_graph_release`, the owner as sender, subject and the one recipient. The format is `unilateral` with no Axal party. |
| Lookup | The body is built from the effect alone, and the service stores its SHA-256 as `body_sha256`. So the body's hash is the key's identity. `lookup` reads the owner's `work_graph_release` envelopes with that hash. |
| Retract | `voidEnvelopeForResend` on every live envelope of the manifest and ref. |

**What counts as delivered.** An envelope the owner has: one whose signing mail is recorded (`email_sent` in
`esign_audit_events`) while it is out for signature, or one already signed or declined. A void, voided or cancelled
envelope delivered nothing.

**An ambiguous delivery is reconciled, never repeated.**

- A send whose answer was lost is found by the next `lookup`, and the delivery is recorded as reconciled. Nothing is
  sent again.
- If Gmail refuses the mail, the service records `email_failed`. The adapter voids that envelope through the service
  and refuses `provider_unavailable`. The next attempt sends a fresh envelope over the same body, so the owner is
  asked once, by a link that reached them.
- If an envelope of the key exists but no mail is recorded (the worker stopped between the two), `lookup` says
  `absent`. `send` voids that envelope first, then sends. If it cannot be voided because the owner is signing it,
  the owner has it, and it counts as delivered.

**Retraction.** A takedown's `release_retract` effect voids every envelope of the manifest and ref that is still out
for signature. Envelopes of other releases are untouched. If the owner is signing at that moment, the void is
refused (`signature_in_progress`) and retried. A signature already given stays as evidence, the way a takedown keeps a
release's decisions and audit. The retraction is done once nothing of the manifest is left to sign, and it mails
nobody.

**Cost.** Every e-sign service call (a lookup read, a create, a void) counts once in the drain's `ProviderMeter`, under
`signed_document`. Part 2's delivery rows still carry each delivery's D1 statements and adapter calls.

### 3. One release out for signature per owner at a time

The e-sign service keeps one pending envelope per document type, sender and recipient (D410). Release envelopes all
share the `work_graph_release` type and the owner is both sender and recipient. So while one release waits on its
owner, a second release's envelope is not created: the service answers `already_pending` with the first envelope. The
adapter sees that the envelope's hash is not its own and refuses `signer_has_pending_release`. The outbox retries
with backoff, and the second release is sent once the first is signed, declined or voided. After the outbox's
attempts it is dead-lettered for a person to read.

This keeps D410's rule intact rather than giving each release its own document type. If the owner wants several
releases out for signature at once, that is a change to the service's dedupe key and belongs to a later part.

### 4. Known limits, for the route part

- **The signing mail's words.** The native service sends its existing agreement mail
  (`sendAgreementAssignedEmail`). It names the sender (`StudioOS releases`) and the document title (`StudioOS release
  sign-off`), but its fixed wording ("has reviewed and verified your partner profile", "Closing Binder") was written
  for partner agreements and is wrong for a release. A release-specific mail is a change to `services/email.ts`. It
  must land before the part that turns delivery on.
- **DocuSign.** The adapter sends through the native provider only. The DocuSign path needs the sender's own
  integration, and a release has no sender who connected one.

### 7. Rollback

Point `signed_document` back at `unconfiguredAdapter()` in `releaseAdapters` and delete the new service. Nothing else
calls it. Leaving `WORK_GRAPH_RELEASE_DELIVERY` unset keeps the drain off without a revert. Any envelope sent while it
was on can be voided by its owner from the e-sign pages.
