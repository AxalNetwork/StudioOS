## D624 — An IC re-vote or recusal never silently rewrites or deletes a graded journal entry

**An IC vote that touches a decision-journal entry whose outcome is recorded follows D618's rule. A re-vote that changes the thesis or the decision stamps `edited_after_outcome_at`. A recusal re-marks the entry `other` and stamps it, and never deletes it. The vote itself is never blocked.** Issue #1345. No migration: it writes the column migration 415 added.

**What was true.** `POST /api/ic/:uid/vote` keeps one auto-drafted journal entry per voter and decision (`routes/ic.ts`, the journal block of the vote handler). That path ignored the outcome:
- **A re-vote** rewrote the entry's `decision`, and its `thesis` too when the vote carried a rationale.
- **A recusal** deleted the entry when its thesis was still the auto-generated "IC vote (…)" line, and otherwise re-marked it `other`.

Both happened even after the outcome was recorded. A graded thesis or decision could therefore change, or the entry disappear, with no trace. That is what D618 exists to prevent.

**What counts.** A vote can move only two graded fields: the thesis and the decision. The stamp (`gradedStamp`) is D618's PUT rule narrowed to those two:
- the stamp is `now` when `outcome_recorded_at` is set and either field differs from what the entry holds;
- otherwise the existing stamp is kept, or NULL if there is none.

So:
- a re-vote that changes nothing does not stamp, and does not erase an earlier stamp;
- before the outcome nothing is stamped.

The thesis is compared after the vote's own rule is applied: a rationale shorter than three characters keeps the entry's thesis, so such a vote can only stamp by changing the decision.

**The recusal.**
- **Graded entry:** a recusal never deletes it, even one still carrying the fallback thesis. It re-marks the decision `other`, which stamps it, and keeps the thesis.
- **Ungraded entry:** before the outcome nothing changes. The fallback draft is deleted, and a hand-written one becomes `other`, unstamped.

**What it does not do.**
- It does not block, delay or refuse the vote. The vote stays the source of truth, and the journal stays best-effort (a journal failure never fails the vote).
- It does not keep the previous thesis or decision. Like D618, the column records the time of the latest graded change after the outcome.

**Tests.** `cloudflare-worker/test/ic_vote_graded_journal_d624.test.ts` runs the real `ic` router against real SQLite, with the journal table built from the baseline plus migration 415. It covers:
- a re-vote before the outcome, which stamps nothing;
- a re-vote after the outcome, by decision and by thesis;
- an identical re-vote, which neither stamps nor erases an earlier stamp;
- a short rationale, which keeps a hand-edited thesis;
- a recusal after the outcome, which keeps the entry, re-marks it and stamps it;
- a recusal before the outcome, which behaves as before;
- the rule itself.

The three older IC fixtures (`ic_conditions_minutes`, `ic_recusal_final_d468`, `ic_company_scope`) gain the two columns production has, because the vote path now reads them.
