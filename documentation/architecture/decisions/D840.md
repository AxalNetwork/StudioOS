## D840 — Mercury, signed in through Mercury's MCP server, feeds the founder's Raise and Grow modules and the investor's sections, read-only

- **Status:** decided by the owner on 2026-10-21 and filed by S1 as #2345, the tracking issue. Part 1 (#2346) wrote this file; each later part adds its own section to §3.
- **Basis:** `origin/main` at `7a4d957`. Worker paths are relative to `cloudflare-worker/src/`.
- **Stacks on:** D710 (the read-only Mercury client), D713 (a member's own Mercury), D752 (the investor's proof of funds, the founder's spend), D753 (the connector page) and D655 (the `connector_fed_items` ledger).

### 1. The decision

The owner, 2026-10-21, on `https://axal.vc/account/connectors/mercury`: "Enable users to sign in to Mercury using MCP, to use data to populate modules across Grow and Raise sections in Founders profiles. Read-only. And for Investor profiles to populate across the different sections and modules as well." The owner also picked "Sign in with Mercury".

Mercury hosts an official MCP server at `https://mcp.mercury.com/mcp` (Streamable HTTP, beta):
- a member signs in with OAuth 2.0 (authorization code with PKCE and dynamic client registration), asking for `read` and `offline_access`;
- Mercury says the server cannot start a transaction or change account data.

It is separate from Mercury's banking-API OAuth program, which is approval-based and which this decision does not use.

### 2. Rules every part keeps

- **Read-only, enforced twice.**
  - **The scope.** The sign-in asks for `read offline_access` only. A grant that reports any other scope is revoked and refused, at the callback and at every refresh (`scopePermission`, `oauth.ts`).
  - **The tools.** `MERCURY_READ_TOOLS` (`services/mercury/mcp.ts`) is the frozen list of tools the Worker may call. `callTool` refuses any other name before a request is made, and also refuses a listed name the server's own `tools/list` does not offer. `cloudflare-worker/test/mercury_mcp_d840.test.ts` pins the list, and holds that it names no send, request, transfer, approval, create, edit or update tool. The server lists such tools; none is ever called, whatever it offers.
  - The MCP client sends POST only: no DELETE, no redirect followed.
- **One interface.** Parts 2–4 read Mercury through `services/mercury/reads.ts` alone, and only `reads.ts` imports `mcp.ts` (test 7).
  - `openMercuryReads` gives one typed function per allowlisted tool.
  - `MercuryBankReader` is what D713's and D752's feeds read, the same on both ways in: `tokenBankReader` for a pasted token, `mcpBankReader` for a sign-in.
- **Facts are written; judgement is proposed** (D782).
  - Balances, transactions and statements are facts. They land through the `connector_fed_items` ledger (D655), so a re-sync updates a row and a row the member deleted stays deleted.
  - Matching a wire to an investor, a payment to a capital call, or a cost to a use-of-funds line is a proposal the member accepts.
- **Only the member's own data, on their own pages.**
  - A balance is never shown to anyone else.
  - An investor's proof of funds stays D752's dated "at least" statement.
  - Account numbers show their last four only. `parse.ts` and the parsers in `reads.ts` keep named fields alone: no account or routing number, EIN, email, address or download link.
  - No Mercury figure is ever written to a log, an error or a comment.
- **The page claims only what is built** (D753).
  - `integrations/mercuryFeeds.ts` lists each use: role, module (or none), the MCP tools it calls, and its decision. Each part adds its landings there.
  - The registry's personal `feeds` and the connector page's tools are held to that list by `cloudflare-worker/test/mercury_feeds_d840.test.ts`.
- **Mercury is stubbed in every test.** `cloudflare-worker/test/_mercury_mcp_fake.mjs` fakes the MCP server and its OAuth server: `initialize`, `tools/list`, `tools/call`, the metadata, registration, token and revocation. The live check is the owner's: one real sign-in on production, recorded as the page's `verified_on`.
- **Tokens.**
  - The access and refresh tokens live only in the row's `credentials_enc` (`integrations/secrets.ts`, AES-GCM under `KEK_PII`, bound to the row's uid).
  - Nothing logs, returns or throws a token, a code, a verifier or a response body. An OAuth error is a `MercuryOauthError` with a code alone, and a callback failure redirects with a fixed reason word (`CALLBACK_REASONS`).

### 3. What each part built

**Part 1, #2346: Sign in with Mercury.**

- **The MCP client** (`services/mercury/mcp.ts`).
  - The session is `initialize` (protocol `2025-06-18`), then the server's `Mcp-Session-Id` on every later request, then `notifications/initialized` and every page of `tools/list`. After that it sends `tools/call`.
  - JSON and event-stream answers are both read.
  - Each POST has a 10-second deadline, a session is capped at 24 POSTs (the Worker's subrequests), and a redirect is refused.
  - Errors keep D710's `MercuryClientError` words, so every existing caller handles them unchanged:
    - a tool off the list, or not offered: `path_not_allowed`;
    - 401 or 403: `unauthorized`;
    - 429, or a spent budget: `rate_limited`;
    - a JSON-RPC error, or `isError`: `http_error`;
    - not JSON-RPC: `bad_json`.
- **The allowlist:** `getOrganization`, `getAccounts`, `getAccount`, `listTransactions`, `getTransactionById`, `getCashflowSummary`, `getTreasury`, `getAccountStatements`, `listInvoices`, `listCustomers`, `getRecipients`, `getSafeRequests`, `listCategories`.
  - Left off although the server lists them: `getUsers`, `getAttachment`, the card tools and `getCurrentDate`.
  - Never on it: `createCategory`, `editCategory`, `updateTransaction` and the money-request tools.
- **OAuth** (`services/mercury/oauth.ts`, `routes/mercury_oauth.ts` mounted at `/api/integrations/mercury/oauth` ahead of the integrations router).
  - **Discovery.** It reads RFC 9728's protected-resource metadata (the path form, then the host's), then RFC 8414's authorization-server metadata (then OpenID's). It refuses if:
    - an endpoint is not https on `mercury.com` or a subdomain;
    - the issuer differs from the one named;
    - S256 is not offered.
  - **Dynamic registration (RFC 7591)** happens once per environment and redirect URI, stored in `platform_settings` under `mercury_mcp_client:<redirect uri>`. The client is public (`token_endpoint_auth_method: none`) and named "Axal VC" (Mercury refuses "Mercury" in a client name). A secret, if one is ever issued, is stored encrypted and sent as Basic auth.
  - **The redirect URI is the host the sign-in started on**, `https://<host>/api/integrations/mercury/oauth/callback`, so the CSRF cookie and the session cookie are on the same host as the callback.
  - **Start** (`GET /start`) answers `{ authorize_url }`. Who may start:
    - a founder, a partner, or an investor on HQ, exactly who may paste a token;
    - a founder needs the Growth plan, as the token path does;
    - a support session gets 403, an investor off HQ gets 403 `purpose_not_allowed`, and anyone else gets 404.

    The authorize URL asks for `read offline_access`, with PKCE S256, `state` and `resource=https://mcp.mercury.com/mcp` (RFC 8707).
  - **State and CSRF.**
    - `state` is #1's signed, single-use, ten-minute token, HMAC-bound to the user and to `mercury_mcp` (`integrations/oauth.ts`). It is consumed before anything else in the callback, so it can never be replayed.
    - The start also sets `__Host-axal_mercury_oauth` (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax, ten minutes). Its SHA-256 rides in the state row, and the callback refuses without the same cookie (`csrf_mismatch`).
  - **Callback** (`GET /callback`), in order:
    1. It exchanges the code with the verifier.
    2. It reads the organization and accounts through the MCP server.
    3. It checks the scope, then D713's decision 3 (not an Axal entity's organization).
    4. Only then does it write the member's one `mercury` row: `auth_type = 'oauth_mcp'`, `scopes_json` the granted scopes, `config_json` `{ read_only: 'reported', via: 'oauth_mcp' }`.

    A refused grant is revoked and stores nothing. It redirects to `/account/connectors/mercury?oauth=ok|error&provider=mercury&reason=<word>`.
  - **Refresh** happens a minute before expiry, or after a 401 at session open, behind #1903's lease. A refused refresh marks the row `error` / `token_rejected`, and the member reads "Sign in with Mercury again."
  - **Disconnect** is the generic `DELETE /api/integrations/:uid`. It calls the provider's new `disconnect`, which revokes the refresh and access tokens (RFC 7009, best effort) before the row is deleted.
  - Pasting a token over a sign-in revokes the sign-in first. The row and its uid stay, and `auth_type` returns to `api_key`.
- **The interface for parts 2–4.**
  - `mercuryAccessFor(env, userId)` (`oauth.ts`) is the access a route uses for the signed-in member's own row, refreshed when needed. It returns null for no row, a pasted token, or a row that needs attention. Its docblock documents the row's shape.
  - `MercuryReads.statementPdf(accountId, statementId)` returns `{ bytes, content_type }`, or null when the account has no such statement. The statement's link (`downloadUrl`, UNVERIFIED) is read inside the call and never returned, and the bytes are never logged. Each request is:
    - a GET, with the member's bearer token;
    - to https on `mercury.com` or a subdomain only (no credentials, no port), checked before each request (`isStatementHost`, the SSRF guard);
    - allowed at most two redirects, each re-checked against those hosts;
    - accepted only as `application/pdf`, at most 15 MB, whether or not the length is declared.
- **The existing feeds on both ways in.** `services/mercuryPersonal.ts`' `personalConnection` now holds a `MercuryBankReader` (`services/mercury/connection.ts`, `bankReaderFor`) in place of the raw token. Its readers paginate by cursor (`transactionsPage`) instead of offset. The founder's credits and spend, the partner's matches and the investor's proof of funds read the same organization, accounts and lines whichever way the member connected (`cloudflare-worker/test/mercury_oauth_d840.test.ts`, test 2). The D713 and D752 suites pass unchanged on the token path.
- **The feed list** (`integrations/mercuryFeeds.ts`). Part 1 adds no landing. Its entries are D713's and D752's existing uses, and parts 2–4 append theirs.
- **The page** (`integrations/connectorPages.ts`).
  - Each member variant's `sign_in` is `oauth_or_key`, "Sign in with Mercury, or a Read Only API token", and `endpoints.sign_in` is the MCP server.
  - Each read tool names both its API path and the MCP tool a sign-in calls.
  - A `sign_in_with_mercury` account tool names the registration and the revocation.
  - The entity page is unchanged. `connector_pages_d753.test.ts` now scans the MCP client, the reads and the OAuth module as Mercury's sources.
- **The frontend.**
  - On a member's Mercury page, the band's action is "Sign in with Mercury" (`mercuryPersonalApi.oauthStart`). The chevron's "Use a Read Only API token" opens the token form.
  - The return's notice says what happened in the member's words (`SIGN_IN_REASONS`, `mercuryModel.js`). The entity row ignores any return.
  - Manage holds "Sign in again" and "Use a Read Only API token" (or "Replace token").
  - "How it reads" says how the row connected (`viaRow`).

**No migration and no new secret.** The tokens use `KEK_PII`, the state uses `JWT_SECRET`, the lease uses `RATE_LIMITS`, and the client is stored in `platform_settings` (migration 241). All of them exist today.

**UNVERIFIED, for the owner's first live sign-in.** Mercury's documentation (`docs.mercury.com`, `mercury.com/auth.md`) and `mcp.mercury.com` could not be reached from the building session: the container's network policy refused them on 2026-10-21. The tool names are the ones Mercury's server listed to an MCP client that day. The OAuth details follow the MCP authorization spec and secondary sources. The first sign-in must confirm:

1. that `https://mcp.mercury.com/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource/mcp` (or the host's root form) names the authorization server, and that its RFC 8414 metadata offers S256 and a `registration_endpoint` on `mercury.com`;
2. that open registration accepts a public client named "Axal VC" with this host's callback, and whether it issues a secret;
3. that the token endpoint grants `read offline_access` and reports `scope` (if it reports none, the requested scope is assumed, per RFC 6749 §5.1);
4. that a refresh token is issued and rotates, and whether revocation is offered;
5. the protocol version the server accepts, whether it answers JSON or an event stream, and that it issues `Mcp-Session-Id`;
6. that a statement's link is `downloadUrl` and is served from a `mercury.com` host with the MCP bearer token (a pre-signed link on another host is refused);
7. that a tool's data is in `structuredContent` or JSON in the first text content, and the field names: accounts carry `accountNumber` (masked or full: only the last four is kept) and `kind`; transactions carry `accountId` (`getTransactionById` is matched to an account by it); `page.nextPage` is the cursor; and each of `reads.ts`' other parsers.

Until then the page's `verified_on` stays null.

**Part 3, #2348: Mercury feeds the founder's Grow modules.**

The founder's own Mercury, signed in through MCP, fills five Grow modules. Product improvement and Market expansion read nothing from it.

- **The read** (`services/mercury/growFeeds.ts`, `readGrow`). It reads only through part 1's `openMercuryReads`:
  - accounts;
  - lines posted in the last 12 full months, at most three 1,000-line pages (a fourth page marks the read capped);
  - treasury;
  - invoices, with their customers' names.

  A pasted token cannot read invoices, customers or treasury, so Grow asks the founder to sign in with Mercury (409 `mercury_sign_in_required`).
- **The sync** is the founder's, on demand: `POST /api/cycles/grow/mercury/sync` ("Sync from Mercury" on each fed module). Nothing reads a founder's bank on a schedule.
  - Only the venture's founder may sync, never a co-founder, an admin, a partner or a support session. A refused sync reaches no Mercury.
  - The answer and the activity line carry counts, never a figure. A failed read is 502 `mercury_read_failed` and writes nothing.
- **Every landing goes through `connector_fed_items`.** A re-sync updates the row its ledger row links. A row the team deleted keeps its ledger row with no `local_id`, and no later sync re-creates it.

| Module | From Mercury | Lands as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue and traction | operating money in and net burn per full month (D752's arithmetic, financing excluded); invoices; credits | **Fact.** One `project_metrics` row per full month, `source = 'mercury'`, dated the month's last day, with `cash_in_cents` (migration 521, integer cents) and `net_burn`. Cash on hand is never written there: it stays D752's confirmed save, because admins and partners read the table. A credit that matches exactly one paid invoice (same amount, posted on or after the invoice date and at most 10 days after it was due) lands as a D713 revenue entry with the invoice's customer, under D713's refusals and its index (kind `credit`, so D713's pick lists it as imported). Every other credit stays a pick. Invoices paid, open and overdue are counted (team only). |
| Durable company-building | cash, treasury, runway | **Fact**, team only, each "From Mercury, as of <date>". The checklist gains "Twelve months of runway" (`DURABILITY_ITEMS`, `mercury: 'runway'`): Mercury ticks it at 12 months or more, and the checklist shows the tick and its date, never the figure. It is in the list and its total only for a founder with an active Mercury connection, or once ticked by hand, so a founder without Mercury loses no score. |
| Team expansion | payroll by month (a payroll category, or a payroll provider's debits) | **Fact**, team only. "Hires affordable at current runway" is computed when read, never stored, and labelled "a computed figure, not a plan": the people at last month's payroll cost per head that fit while keeping 12 months of runway. |

| Partner network | payments to a payee whose normalised name is exactly one active Axal partner's company | **Proposal:** "You paid <partner> $N since <date>: link to this partnership?". Only the founder accepts or dismisses. Accept links the named `growth_partners` row, or a new one under the partner's company, through the ledger (kind `partner_payment`). Nothing is written before Accept, and a dismissed proposal stays dismissed. |

- **Only the founder's company sees a figure.** `parts.from_mercury` on each fed module's read goes to the founder and the members linked to the venture's company. Anyone else who reads the venture (an admin, a partner, an advisor, an investor) gets `team_only` and the sentence, never a figure. A part that cannot be read is `unreadable`, and the rest of the module still loads.
- **What is stored** (migration 521):
  - `project_metrics.cash_in_cents`;
  - `mercury_grow_facts`, the team-only figures by kind and period (a channel's spend by its uid). No counterparty name, account number or transaction id is stored.
  - `mercury_partner_proposals`, which keeps the partner's company and the paid total.
- **The page** lists exactly what is built:
  - five `grow_*` entries in `MERCURY_FEEDS`, with `GROW_FEED_KINDS` naming the ledger kinds each module receives;
  - the founder's registry `feeds`;
  - the founder page's `list_invoices`, `list_customers`, `read_treasury`, `payroll_by_month`, `marketing_spend` and `partner_payments`.

  `mercury_grow_d840.test.ts` holds the list against what a sync lands, against part 1's fake MCP server. Partner network, Durable company-building, Team expansion and Distribution also gain labels and doors in `moduleLabels.ts`.
- **UNVERIFIED**, beyond part 1's list:
  - Mercury's invoice statuses (`paid`; `open`, `unpaid`, `sent`, `pending`, `outstanding`; `overdue`, `past_due`);
  - whether an invoice carries a paid date (Grow uses the due date);
  - the treasury accounts' `availableBalance`.
