### Decisions

#### 1. Three balances, never mixed

| | AI top-up balance (this decision) | Perk credits (D412, D540) | Introduction credits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Store | `ai_topup_accounts` (419) and the new `ai_credit_ledger` (486) | `perk_credit_ledger` (`186_perks.sql:118-134`) | `intro_credit_ledger` (`services/introductions.ts:66-80`) |
| Routes | `/api/ai-budget/*`, `/api/orders/*`, `/api/admin/catalog/token-packs*`, `/api/public/plans-and-packs` | `/api/perks/*` | `/api/introductions/*` |
| What a member reads | "Top-up balance", "tokens", "Token packs", "Add tokens" | "Perk credits" (`PerksPage.jsx:48-51`, `:566`) | "Introduction credits" |
| How it is bought | a Stripe token pack, and nothing else | never: earned or granted | no longer sold (§12) |

- **Kept apart.** There is no transfer, conversion or shared code path between the three (`D629.md:3`, `:33`). No D755 file reads `perk_credit_ledger` or `intro_credit_ledger`.
- **Never "credits" on screen.** The internal table is `ai_credit_ledger`, but a member never sees the AI balance called "credits". On screen it is the top-up balance, in dollars.
- **The Perks page clause.** The Perks page sentence (`PerksPage.jsx:1750`, `:1777`) gains "…and from your AI top-up balance."

#### 2. The unit

- **USD.** The balance is stored in integer micro-USD (`AI_USD_SCALE`, `services/aiBudget.ts:6`). Stripe amounts are integer cents. $1 is 100 cents, or 1,000,000 micro-USD (`D629.md:15`).
- **How amounts are shown.** Balances and caps use `microMoney` (exact). Spend uses `usageMoney`, to the cent (D750 decision 5).
- **Packs are named by face value.** A pack is "$25 of tokens", never a count of tokens: `/account/usage` already uses "tokens" for model token counts (`AccountUsage.jsx:260-264`).
- **The price rule** stays byte for byte: "$1 of tokens buys $1 of AI use. Tokens never expire." (`PricingPhone.jsx:17`).

#### 3. Token packs

- **Four packs at launch: $10, $25, $50 and $100.** Each is a one-time USD Stripe price, credited at face value: $10 credits 10,000,000 micro-USD.
  - $10 keeps Stripe's fixed card fee tolerable while AI is billed at cost.
  - Four steps cover a light month up to heavy use.
  - The sandbox's existing $10 "AI top-up" price fits as the first pack (dated data, 2026-10-17).
- **No bonus at launch.** The store allows a bonus of up to 10% of face value, set by the Super Admin.
  - A bonus is always its own `bonus` ledger entry, never folded into the purchase.
  - While members pay Cloudflare's cost (`AI_BILLING` unset: AIRail (#1874), its plan decision and its billed-metering task, A3), a bonus is a straight loss. So all four packs launch with no bonus.
- **The store: D1 table `ai_token_packs`** (migration 486). Columns:
  - `uid`, `price_id` (UNIQUE) and `product_id`;
  - `face_cents`, from 500 to 50,000;
  - `bonus_microusd`, from 0 to `face_cents` × 1,000;
  - `on_sale`, `sort` and `origin` (`created` or `adopted`);
  - `created_by`, the timestamps and `archived_at`.

  The label is derived from `face_cents` and is never typed.
- **The price stays Stripe's.** A pack is on sale only when its price, read from the D1 catalog mirror, passes D750's `judge` (active, `one_time`, `usd`, a whole amount) and equals `face_cents`. A pack that fails is not shown to members, and HQ sees why.
- **`STRIPE_PRICE_AI_TOPUP` is kept as a fallback.** If it is set and not already a pack row, it is listed as one more pack (origin `environment`, no bonus) under D750's rules. HQ can **Adopt** it into the store, and the variable can then be removed.
- **The catalog kind.** `ProductKind` gains `token_pack` (`services/catalog.ts:20-27`), so kind lists can tell packs apart.
  - The list of packs is `ai_token_packs`, not Stripe metadata. Any Admin and the dashboard can edit metadata, and a metadata marker would let them mint AI balance.
  - The Add-ons grid (`ProductsPage.jsx:499-502`) leaves out every pack price.

#### 4. Who creates a pack: the Super Admin, through the Worker

The Super Admin presses **New pack** on HQ › AI allowances › Token packs, which calls `POST /api/admin/catalog/token-packs {amount_usd, idempotency_key}`. Using its own `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`, the Worker then:

1. creates the product "$N of tokens", with metadata `{kind: token_pack, decision: D755, seller: management}` and `Idempotency-Key: token_pack:<key>:product`;
2. creates one one-time USD price of N × 100 cents, with `Idempotency-Key: token_pack:<key>:price`;
3. mirrors it, inserts the pack row and writes `admin_audit_log`.

Three more routes complete the set:
- **`POST …/token-packs/adopt {price_id}`** records an existing price, after judging it. That covers a price the owner made in the dashboard, or the fallback.
- **`PATCH …/token-packs/:uid`** sets `on_sale`, `bonus_usd` and `sort`.
- **`POST …/token-packs/:uid/archive`** sets `on_sale` to 0 and archives the Stripe price.

An amount never changes: a new amount is a new pack.

**Why the Super Admin and not the owner in the Stripe dashboard:**
- **The Worker checks the pack's shape** when it is created: one-time, USD, whole dollars from $5 to $500, and a bounded bonus. A product typed by hand can be sold and charged without crediting, because a price that is not the pack is charged as an ordinary line (`services/orders.ts:498-516`).
- **The price is made in the key's own Stripe mode**, so D750's `mode_mismatch` cannot happen.
- **Each create is idempotent and audited**, and needs no deploy and no environment change.
- **No agent creates a Stripe object.** A pack is created only when the Super Admin, a person, presses Create. The owner keeps the live account, the keys, the webhook endpoint and its events (owner actions).

**What the existing routes need first:**
- **The Super Admin gate.** The admin catalog writes accept any Admin (`routes/catalog.ts:94`, `:125`, `:172`, `:222`, `:236`, `:284`, `:314`). The token-pack routes call `requireSuperAdmin`.
- **No pack edits through the generic writes.** They refuse kind `token_pack`, and refuse to edit or archive a pack's product or price, with `token_pack_managed_on_hq`.
- **Idempotency keys.** Today Stripe is called with no idempotency key (`services/catalog.ts:772`, `:835`). `createProduct` and `createPrice` take one.
- **Cool-off.** `/api/admin/catalog` joins `COOL_OFF_PREFIXES` (`index.ts:606-638`), in the same class as promos.

#### 5. Buying

**The flow:**
1. **Add tokens**, on the rail, the Assist card, Usage or the phone pricing screen, opens `/plans-and-pricing#token-packs`.
2. **Buy** on a pack opens `/checkout?token_pack=<uid>`.
3. `POST /api/orders/intent` is sent with that one line.
4. Stripe Elements confirms the existing PaymentIntent (`metadata.kind=cart_order`, money kind `marketplace`, `routes/orders.ts:184-207`).
5. `/checkout/confirmation` waits for the webhook, then says "Tokens added".

**The rules the Worker enforces in `POST /api/orders/intent`** when a line is a pack price:
- **Alone.** The pack is the only line, quantity 1 (`token_pack_alone`). A pack checkout never touches the shared cart.
- **No promo code** (`promo_not_for_token_packs`). What a member pays before VAT is the face value, so the free path (`routes/orders.ts:141-174`) can never apply to a pack.
- **On sale.** The pack is on sale, and its mirrored price equals `face_cents` (`token_pack_not_on_sale`).
- **Within the limits in §16.**

**The purchase row.** `ai_topup_purchases` gains `pack_uid`, `bonus_microusd`, `reversed_microusd` and `dispute_state` (migration 486). It records the credit (face × 10,000) and the pack's bonus at that moment. A later change to the bonus never rewrites it (as `D629.md:31`).

**Retired.** `?ai_topup=1` stays only as an alias that redirects to `/plans-and-pricing#token-packs`, and `useAiTopupCart` is retired.

**Card details** stay in Stripe Elements. The Worker holds only IDs and the client secret (`routes/payments.ts:31-34`).

#### 6. The credit: the payment webhook only

- **Only the webhook credits.** The verified `payment_intent.succeeded` webhook is the only thing that credits a purchase (`routes/billing.ts:1852-1861`). `/orders/confirm`, `fulfilOrder`, the free path, HQ and every other route never do.
- **One D1 batch, idempotent on the PaymentIntent ID.** It writes:
  - the account row;
  - a `purchase` entry (+face), with `source_ref = pi:<id>`, only `WHERE credited_at IS NULL`;
  - a `bonus` entry (+bonus), with the same `source_ref`, only when the bonus is above 0;
  - the balance increase (purchase plus bonus), only if the purchase entry was written;
  - the `credited_at` stamp and the `ai_topup_credited` activity log.
- **Every existing check stays.** The purchase row must exist and the webhook secret must be set. The PaymentIntent must be `succeeded`, in the right currency, with `amount_received` equal to `expected_total_cents`, and with the right `order_ref` and `user_id` (`services/aiTopups.ts:105-113`).
- **The notice.** Once the batch commits, and only once, an in-app notice is sent: "Tokens added: $25.00", linking to `/account/usage#top-ups`. The introduction grant does the same (`services/introductions.ts:307-319`).
- **Fulfilment.** `fulfilOrder` still runs after the credit (`routes/billing.ts:1856-1859`). It marks the order paid, renders the invoice and emails it, and writes no `user_products` row for a pack line (`services/orders.ts:498-516`): tokens are a balance, not a licence.
- **Retries.** A webhook that cannot credit returns non-2xx, and Stripe retries it, as now.

#### 7. The ledger: `ai_credit_ledger`

**What a row holds.** The ledger is append-only, with one row for every movement of the top-up balance:
- `uid`, `user_id`, `kind`, `delta_microusd` (a signed integer) and `source_ref`;
- where relevant, `payment_intent_id`, `reservation_uid`, `pack_uid` and `shortfall_microusd`;
- `actor`, `reason` and `created_at`.

`UNIQUE(kind, source_ref)` is its idempotency key, the same pattern as `perk_credit_ledger`. UPDATE and DELETE abort, through a trigger.

| kind | sign | written by | source_ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| `purchase` | + | the payment webhook | `pi:<id>` |
| `bonus` | + | the payment webhook, in the same batch | `pi:<id>` |
| `hold` | − | a trigger on an `ai_budget_reservations` INSERT with `topup_microusd > 0` | `res:<uid>` |
| `release` | + (the whole hold) | a trigger when a reservation leaves `pending` or `review` | `res:<uid>` |
| `spend` | − (`actual_topup_microusd`) | the same trigger, when it is above 0 | `res:<uid>` |
| `refund` | − or 0 | the `charge.refunded` webhook | `rf:<charge>:<amount_refunded>` |
| `dispute` | − or 0 | `charge.dispute.created` | `dp:<dispute>` |
| `dispute_won` | + | `charge.dispute.closed` (won, or warning closed) | `dw:<dispute>` |
| `adjustment` | ± | the Super Admin, with a reason of 10 or more characters | `adj:<idempotency key>` |

- **The invariant.** For every user, `SUM(delta_microusd)` equals `ai_topup_accounts.balance_microusd`. Tests assert it after every scenario, and HQ's reconcile read lists every user for whom it fails.
- **Holds by trigger.** Holds, releases and spends come from triggers, so the hold, settle and review code stays exactly as it is (`services/aiBudget.ts:98-130`, `:222-253`). That code belongs to AIRail (#1874), its billed-metering task (A3). D755 removes only the legacy `draw` INSERT (`services/aiBudget.ts:114-116`), which the trigger replaces.
- **Migration 486 (`486_ai_credits_token_packs.sql`):**
  - creates `ai_token_packs`, and `ai_credit_ledger` with its triggers;
  - copies every `ai_topup_ledger` row (`credit` becomes `purchase`, `draw` becomes `spend`);
  - writes a `hold` row for every pending or review reservation with `topup_microusd > 0`;
  - adds a trigger on `ai_topup_ledger` INSERT that mirrors into the new ledger, so the old Worker still running between the migration and the deploy loses nothing (the predeploy hook applies migrations first: CLAUDE.md fact 1);
  - adds the four columns to `ai_topup_purchases`.

  Migration 419 is immutable (`scripts/migration-immutability-gate.mjs`), so `ai_topup_ledger` is kept, unwritten, for a later cleanup.
- **Never below zero.** The CHECK at `419:14` stays. Every negative movement takes at most the balance and records the rest as `shortfall_microusd`.
- **Naming the run.** A `spend` row names its reservation. The run it paid for can be named once AIRail's migration (493–495), in AIRail (#1874)'s billed-metering task (A3), adds `reservation_uid` to `ai_usage_logs`.

#### 8. Refunds and disputes reverse the credit

New webhook cases go in the switch (`routes/billing.ts:1465-1893`), after signature verification (`:1371-1401`). They act only on charges whose PaymentIntent has an `ai_topup_purchases` row; every other charge is untouched.

- **`charge.refunded`.**
  - The target reversal is `ceil((purchase + bonus) × amount_refunded / amount_captured)`, so VAT shares pro rata, minus what was already reversed for that PaymentIntent.
  - The Worker takes `min(target, balance)` as a `refund` entry and records the rest as shortfall.
  - The `source_ref` is `rf:<charge>:<cumulative amount_refunded>`, so a replay is ignored and a second partial refund is its own row.
- **`charge.dispute.created`.** The same calculation for the disputed amount, written as a `dispute` entry. The purchase's `dispute_state` becomes `open`.
- **`charge.dispute.closed`.**
  - If the dispute is won, or the warning is closed, a `dispute_won` entry returns what the dispute took.
  - If it is lost, nothing more moves, and `dispute_state` becomes `lost`.
- **The member is told.** After each reversal, an in-app notice says, for example, "Refunded: $X was taken off your top-up balance."
- **Where refunds are made.** Refunds are issued in the Stripe dashboard, or through the admin refund route. The webhook is the only path that reverses a credit.
- **The purchase block.** While a member has an open dispute, or has recorded a shortfall in the last 90 days, they cannot buy packs (`token_packs_on_hold`).
- **Owner confirmation.** `D629.md:33` makes this the owner's call. D755 picks "take back the unspent part" (S1's earlier pick for AIRail too). The reversal task merges only after the owner confirms on #1008.
- **2026-10-18: the owner confirmed "take back"** (relayed by S1 on #1920). Only take back is built (CR3, `services/aiCreditReversals.ts`); there is no record-only mode.

### CR7 checkout implementation (#1985)

The token-pack URL selects one advertised CR2 pack by its uid (or the advertised
price id for an environment fallback with no uid). The order intent contains
only that price and quantity 1; no shared cart, promo or saved-card choice is
used. The ordinary cart checkout keeps its existing behavior. The existing
checkout layout and country/VAT calculation are reused.

Confirmation is a read of the member's own order. Only an explicit
`ai_topup.state = credited` can show “Tokens added”; the current balance must
come from `ai_topup.balance_microusd`, never a client-side sum. Refunded and
disputed are terminal. Waiting and overdue are polled with delays of 1, 2, 4,
8, 15, 30, 30 and 30 seconds (nine reads including the initial read). An unmount
cancels its timer and ignores late replies. After the bound, the page points
to Usage; it calls a payment recorded only when the order is paid.

A `token_pack` navigation hint can keep a missing order projection waiting;
it cannot prove credit. The owner authorized the necessary shared dependencies
under #1985: `getOrderForUser` checks ownership before adding `ai_topup`,
and `orderTopUp` reads the scoped purchase state and current account balance.
It never credits or fulfils the order. Missing purchases or failed reads stay
unreadable; ordinary orders keep their existing shape.

The Token packs section reads the existing public plans-and-packs endpoint,
shows one Buy link per advertised pack and keeps those prices out of Add-ons
and the ordinary product slide-over. The shared Add tokens destination and the
retired checkout redirect lead to that section. Current CR2 packs take precedence
over the deprecated product switch. CR5's plan allowances and visitor routing
remain separate.

Verification is in `frontend/test/checkout_token_pack_d755.test.mjs`: 17
scenarios and 40 embedded mutation checks, each followed by the restored
scenario. The shared-model test adds three mutations. Worker receipt coverage
has three scenarios and nine mutations. Existing cart persistence/addition
coverage is retained in `ai_topup_cart_d629.test.mjs`; its obsolete top-up-hook
cases move to the isolated checkout tests. Providers are stubbed or forbidden.
The managed local executor was unavailable; CI is the build, full-suite and
guard evidence.

### CR7 historical receipts follow-up (#1985)

A recorded top-up purchase, scoped to both the owner and order reference, is
the authority for the optional order projection. A price that HQ later adopts
as a pack cannot change an older ordinary or mixed receipt. Ordinary orders
use one bounded purchase lookup rather than a catalogue lookup for every line;
a database predating the purchase table still returns its ordinary receipts.
An explicitly recorded token-pack line with no purchase keeps the base receipt
and exposes only `ai_topup.state = unreadable`, which confirmation names as
Unreadable and never credits. Other failed reads still fail as unreadable.
No read writes, fulfils an order or contacts Stripe.

`checkout_order_topup_d755.test.ts` covers historical mixed receipts, missing
purchase/table, genuine read failures, the lookup bound and the existing
owner/state/balance rules: four scenarios and thirteen embedded mutations.
The frontend checkout suite adds a missing-purchase marker assertion and its
mutation, retaining its existing 17 scenarios. Each mutant must break the
same scenario, with the restored implementation passing on both sides.
