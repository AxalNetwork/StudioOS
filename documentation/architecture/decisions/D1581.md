### 1. The decision on live transcription (spec item 2)

The streaming transcription with turn detection powers **Rehearse only**: its live words a minute and its notes checklist. That is #3827's part 2, not this issue.

**Present gets no live microphone.** Present runs in front of investors, so recording there would need a consent step that no board draws. Present gets the per-card budgets and the remote.

### 2. Per-card budgets in Present

The presenter view (`PitchPresent.jsx`) now has a **Pitch length** choice of 2, 3 or 5 minutes (3 by default). Under the notes it shows the current card's time against its share, "0:42 of 0:30", amber when over.

- **One split.** The share is Rehearse's own split, `cardBudgetsMs`: the pitch length across the cards in whole seconds, with the first cards taking the leftover seconds. Present reads it through `presentTimingModel.presentBudget` and never computes a second one.
- **Time per card.** Each time a card comes up, Present records a mark, as Rehearse does (`spentByCard`). A card's time is then the sum of its stretches on screen, so going back to a card adds to its time.

The existing view (current card, next card, notes, timer, count) is extended, not replaced.

### 3. `DeckRemote`: one class, two kinds of instance

`durable-objects/deck-remote.ts` is bound as `DECK_REMOTE` in all three `wrangler.toml` tables under the migration tag `v7-deck-remote`. It is reached only through `doNamespace` (D264).

- **`deck:<version id>`, the room.** It holds Present's sockets and the pairing.
  - The sockets are Hibernatable WebSockets, at most 8 per room, so an idle Present costs no duration.
  - A step from the paired remote is sent to every Present socket at once.
- **`code:<six digits>`, the pointer.** It records which deck a live code belongs to, and answers once, because the join takes it. A code already in use by a live pairing is never issued again: the pair route draws another (up to five tries).

There is no D1 table: the pairing lives only in the Durable Objects.

### 4. Pairing, and who may do what

- **Pair.** The presenter pairs and gets a six-digit code, good for ten minutes and for one join. A new code ends the remote paired before.
- **Join.** The phone joins with the code and gets a `remote_id`, good for four hours. Unpair ends it.
- **Steps.** A step is one of Present's own keys (`next`, `prev`, `first`, `last`) or `{ card: n }`. Anything else is refused with `invalid_step`. Esc stays the presenter's alone. Present ignores remote steps while Rehearse is open, because Rehearse owns the keys then.
- **Who.** Every route needs a signed-in member who may read the deck (`projectOwned`), the phone included.
  - A member who cannot read the deck gets the same answer as a wrong code (404 `code_not_found`), so a code alone opens nothing.
  - Pair and join sit under fail-closed buckets, `deck_remote_pair` and `deck_remote_join`, at 10 a minute per member. At that rate, guessing a million codes takes far longer than a code lives.
- **No binding.** Without the `DECK_REMOTE` binding, every route answers 503 `remote_off`.

### 5. The 300 ms test

`cloudflare-worker/test/deck_remote_d1581.test.ts` runs a step through the real route and room, from the phone's request to the frame on Present's socket, and requires under 300 ms. Locally that is a few milliseconds.

The issue's test is on staging. `scripts/remote-latency.mjs` pairs a deck, joins as the phone, opens Present's socket and times each step on a deployed Worker. It exits 1 at 300 ms or more. It needs a member's token, so the owner runs it. The result goes on #3828.
