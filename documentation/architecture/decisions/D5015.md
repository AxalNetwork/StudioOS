### The engine

`recommend(model, profile, { allowProvisional })` is pure: no database, no clock, no randomness. It returns:
- the three normalized factors, the weighted index, the tier and the recommended fees;
- `complete`, `available`, `missing`, `provisional` and an `unavailable_reason`;
- a trace that explains every number.

The v1 formula weights purchasing power at 4000 bps, the startup ecosystem at 3500 and market size at 2500. Raw values are never multiplied together. Each input becomes a bounded factor first:

| Input | Transform (from the model's `normalization`) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Purchasing power | `PPP / reference`, then floor and cap | A rich market pays more, up to a cap; a poorer one never falls toward zero. |
| Startup ecosystem | `sqrt(score / reference)` (or `log10` with a slope), then floor and cap | The US is about 7× the next ecosystem. A square root plus a cap stops that outlier from pushing every other market toward the floor. Each market's factor depends only on its own score. |
| Market size | `1 + slope × log10(population / reference)` (or configured bands), then floor and cap | Diminishing returns: doubling the population adds about 0.075, not 1.0. |

Then:
- `index = Σ weight × factor / 10000`;
- each fee is `round_to_increment(reference fee × index)`;
- the minimum royalty is that ratio of the **rounded** annual fee (the figure HQ quotes), rounded to the increment;
- the royalty is the model's default unless the tier entry sets its own;
- the regulatory surcharge comes from the model's map for the profile's class.

### The decisions

1. **Integers only, including the nonlinear steps.**
   - Factors and the index are millionths, weights and rates are basis points, and money is minor units.
   - `log10` is an integer binary-logarithm algorithm on BigInt: the integer part by doubling and halving, then 48 fractional bits by repeated squaring, scaled by log10(2) to 18 places.
   - `sqrt` is an integer Newton square root.
   - No `Math.log` or `Math.sqrt` touches a contractual figure, so a result is the same on every runtime.
2. **Rounding is explicit and happens in two places.**
   - The index rounds once, half away from zero, from the exact weighted sum.
   - Each fee rounds once, half up, from its exact value in micro-cents to `fee_rounding_cents` (EUR 2,500 in v1).
   - The square root floors to the ppm.
   - The trace keeps the unrounded micro-cent figures.
3. **Missing is never low.**
   - A `not_recorded` input, or one with no value, makes `available: false`, names the input, and leaves the index and fees `null`.
   - A `provisional` input is used only with `allowProvisional: true`. The result is then `complete: false` and lists it.
   - A missing regulatory class leaves the surcharge `null`: unknown, not free. It does not block the recommendation, because the class is HQ's optional input.
4. **The engine never invents configuration.**
   - It refuses a model whose weights do not sum to 10000, whose normalization lacks any transform it needs, or whose tiers do not start at index 0. It raises `PricingEngineError('model_invalid', problems)`.
   - `V1_MODEL_DRAFT` holds #5013's stated values as the configuration a v1 draft is published with. It is not a fallback.
5. **A digest of everything the calculation used.**
   - `sourceDigest` is the SHA-256 of canonical JSON (keys sorted at every level). It covers the model's identity and calculation fields, every input with its status, source and as-of date, and the provisional permission.
   - The same inputs always give the same digest, whatever the key order.
6. **Only frozen models are snapshotted.**
   - `createSnapshot` refuses a draft (`model_not_frozen`), because a draft can still be edited and a snapshot naming it could stop reproducing. `previewRecommendation` works with any model and writes nothing.
   - An unavailable recommendation is still snapshotted (`complete = 0`, missing inputs listed, no figures), so "this market could not be priced that day" is on record.
7. **A snapshot reproduces from itself.**
   - `verifySnapshot` recalculates from the snapshot's own columns and the model it names. It never reads the live profile, which may have changed.
   - It compares the digest, the index, the tier and every figure.

### Tests

`cloudflare-worker/test/licence_pricing_engine_5015.test.ts` (13 tests) covers every case the issue lists:
- the reference market comes out at index 1.0 and pays exactly the reference fees;
- a high-PPP, tiny market hits the PPP cap and is pulled down by size;
- a huge, lower-PPP market hits the PPP floor and gains only with diminishing returns;
- a US-scale startup outlier is capped and leaves other markets unchanged;
- each NULL input leaves no index or fee and is named;
- a provisional input is refused, or allowed and flagged;
- weight sums other than 10000 and missing normalization are refused;
- floors and caps bound every factor, and the index ranges from 0.35 to 1.645;
- commercial rounding is exact (EUR 48,750 rounds to 50,000, EUR 48,749.25 to 47,500), and the trace keeps the micro-cents;
- the integer `log10`, `isqrt` and `divRound` are exact at their boundaries;
- the digest is deterministic, independent of key order, and changes when any input changes;
- a snapshot is unchanged by unrelated database changes and verifies from its own columns after its country's profile changes;
- an unavailable market is snapshotted as such, and a draft model is refused.
