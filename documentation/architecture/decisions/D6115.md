## D6115 — Remove a Grow partner, market or obligation added by mistake (#6115)

A writer on a venture can now remove a Partner network partner, a Market
expansion market or a Durable company-building obligation that was added by
mistake. Before this a partner could only be Paused or Ended, a market set to
Not now and an obligation marked Done, so a mistaken row stayed in the table,
the card and the counts.

### Hard delete, not soft delete

The row is deleted. No `deleted_at` column was added and no migration ships.

- **The readers.** `growth_partners`, `markets` and `compliance_events` are
  read in more than twenty places outside the Grow writers: the module reads
  and cards (`routes/cycle_grow.ts`, `services/growCycle.ts`), the phone focus
  (`services/cycleFocus.ts`), report figures (`services/reportFigures.ts`),
  publish sources (`services/publishSources.ts`,
  `integrations/publishSources.ts`), venture scoring and thesis fit, the
  company sheet, the Mercury and Google feeds, the mail context pack, update
  asks (`routes/portfolio_updates.ts`) and, for `compliance_events`, the whole
  compliance and calendar surface. A soft delete is correct only once every
  one of them filters the flag, and one that is missed counts the row again.
- **What there is to keep.** Removal is refused for a record that holds
  history (below). What is left is a row with no history, which nothing needs
  to read back, and its removal is kept in activity.
- **Precedent.** `DELETE /api/compliance/events/:id` and
  `DELETE /api/cycles/grow/channels/content/:uid` were already hard deletes.

### When removal is refused

Each refusal is a 409 whose `detail` says what to do instead. The check is
part of the `DELETE` statement itself (`AND NOT EXISTS …`), so there is no
window between a check and the delete: a delete of a row the venture owns that
takes nothing is the refusal.

| Record | Refused when | Sentence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Partner | It has a `partner_revenue` month, a `growth_partner_deals` deal, or an accepted `mercury_partner_proposals` row linked to it (Mercury payments) | This partner has revenue or deals recorded, so it stays as history. Set its stage to Ended instead. |
| Obligation | It has an `obligation_occurrences` row (it has been held) | This obligation has been held, so its past meetings stay as history. Mark it done instead. |
| Market | Never | — |

A market holds plans and judgements only: its scores, its decision, its entry
steps and its localisation checks. Its entry steps and localisation rows are
deleted with it, by the tables' `ON DELETE CASCADE` (D1 enforces foreign
keys; the test database turns them on). Investors' replies to an update ask about it stay on the
update in `update_ask_replies`; only their link to the market is gone.

An obligation's agenda items are its plan for the next meeting, not a record
of one, so they are deleted with it, by the same cascade. Holding the meeting writes the
occurrence, and from then on the obligation is kept.

### The existing compliance delete now refuses too

Obligations are `compliance_events` rows, and `DELETE /api/compliance/events/:id`
(used by `api.complianceDelete` on the Compliance page and the Calendar's
compliance sheet) deleted the row and checked nothing. D1 enforces foreign
keys, so that delete also cascaded the obligation's `obligation_agenda_items`
and `obligation_occurrences` (`462_grow_record_stores.sql`, `ON DELETE
CASCADE`): a held board meeting lost its past meetings, and the R2 objects of
their minutes were left behind with no row pointing at them. It logged no
activity.

The route keeps its own gate (`checkWrite`) and now runs the same
`removeObligation` as Grow's Remove. So a held obligation is refused there with
the same sentence (the Compliance page now shows it, the Calendar sheet
already showed a refusal), a fresh one takes its agenda with it, and the
removal is logged. `compliance_task_events` (a company task's timeline) and
`scope_tasks.compliance_ref` keep their foreign-key behaviour, cascade and set
null, as before.

### Where it runs

- **Writers** (`services/growWriters.ts`): `removePartner`, `removeMarket` and
  `removeObligation`. Each proves the id is the venture's (a foreign id is a
  404), applies the refusal, deletes in one batch, and unlinks a connector's
  `connector_fed_items` row (`local_id = NULL`, as `deleteSheetFact` does) so a
  Calendar-proposed obligation is not proposed again.
- **Record action** (`services/records/grow.ts`): `remove`, a danger write
  with a confirmation, on each of the three records, for writers only (the
  records' `writeGate`). The page re-reads the record after a write; the 404
  sends it back to the list.
- **Routes** (`routes/cycle_grow.ts`): `DELETE /partners/:uid`,
  `DELETE /markets/:uid` and `DELETE /obligations/:id`, each `{ project }`
  through the same `write` gate as every other Grow writer. No client method
  was added: the page removes through the record action.

### Activity keeps the removal

Each removal writes one `activity_logs` row through `logGrowWrite`:
`grow.partner-network` / `grow_partner_removed`, `grow.market-expansion` /
`grow_market_removed` and `grow.durable-company-building` /
`grow_obligation_removed`, with the record's id, the venture and the writer.
The summary names the record (`Partner removed: <name>`), as the table already
shows that name to every reader; no customer or contact is named.

### Verified

`cloudflare-worker/test/grow_record_remove_6115.test.ts`: a fresh partner,
market and obligation are removed by the record action and by their DELETE
routes and leave the module's rows, total and card; their children go; the
ledger row is unlinked; activity holds the removal. A partner with revenue, a
deal or accepted Mercury payments and a held obligation are refused with their
sentence, and nothing is deleted or logged. The compliance route refuses a held
obligation and logs a fresh one's removal. An advisor and another venture's
founder are refused, and a foreign id is a 404.
