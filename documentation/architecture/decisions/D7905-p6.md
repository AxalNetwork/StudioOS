## D7905 part 6 — Interruptible engine, whole-phase export bound, create reconciliation (#7905)

Part 5 left four of S32's findings open in the executor. This part closes their
executor side. It raises no timeout, cap or lease and adds no deletion beyond
one exact container: every change makes a phase shorter or stoppable.

- **Interruptible engine calls (F6).** Every docker and setfacl call goes
  through `boundedClient`, which is asynchronous. It uses no shell and a
  scrubbed environment, and caps captured output at 1 MiB. Each call takes a
  timeout and an abort signal, and is killed with SIGKILL when either fires.
  An interrupted call rejects with `interrupted: true`: killing a client does
  not undo what the daemon may already have done, so callers that change
  engine state reconcile rather than assume. The non-interruptible
  `runCandidate` is removed, so candidate work runs only through
  `runCandidateInterruptible`. The run's cancellation stops staging, create,
  start, verification, re-admission, install, the recheck, the test and the
  export. Removal is never cancellable.
- **Whole lifecycle inside the bound (F1/F3).** The bound is the earlier of
  the receipt and the Run step wrapper. Every mandatory phase has a deadline
  derived from it:
  - Staging, create, start, verification, re-admission, install, the recheck
    and the test end `FINISH_RESERVE_MS` before the bound.
  - The export ends `REMOVAL_RESERVE_MS` before the bound.
  - Inspect, rm and the absence check end by the bound.

  Each engine call gets the smaller of 30 s and what is left before its phase
  deadline (`callBudget`); with nothing left, it is not made. The supervisor
  also stops waiting on re-admission, the recheck and the export at their
  deadlines (`withinPhase`), even if the callee ignores its signal. The
  plan-check `python3` and `git` probes are bounded at 30 s each.
- **Whole-phase export bound (E1).** One deadline and the cancel signal bound
  the entire export:
  - Every file operation is asynchronous, and trace files are written only
    after the stream completes. When the deadline or an abort fires, the stage
    is closed at once and the export reports failed.
  - The final live check and the rename run together in one synchronous step,
    so nothing can publish afterwards.
  - A stop after the rename reports `offer: 'published_unsynced'`.
  - The terminal fsync after the rename is checked too (S32's recheck of f13
    found it unchecked). If that fsync returns after the deadline or after an
    abort, the export reports failed with `offer: 'published'`, never complete.
    A late check cannot bound I/O that is blocked; the whole-phase stop above
    does that.
  - An fsync already handed to the kernel cannot be withdrawn. It may finish
    after the report, inside `pending/` only. That host state is unknown, not
    clean.
- **Ambiguous create (C3).** A create intent (owner, image, absolute deadline)
  is written durably before `docker create` is asked. If no ID comes back,
  the supervisor inspects the one fixed name, `studioos-job-<owner>`, and
  never uses a pattern or a pool:
  - Only an exact identity match is adopted and removed by its ID: name, owner
    label, image, entrypoint, command and deadline. It is never started.
  - A foreign object under the name, an absent name or an unreadable engine
    is not complete cleanup. The intent is kept.
  - The cleanup step and the next prepare reconcile the intent again. An
    absent name, or an exact container verified gone by ID and by name, drops
    the intent; a conflict or an unreadable engine keeps it.
  - An intent that cannot be written means no create at all.

- **Durable tail (S32's recheck of `48730285`).**
  - **The gap:** the recovery-store writes after a verified removal are synchronous local fsyncs that sit outside every bound. In S32's model, removal ends 1 ms before the bound, then 60 s of store work follows, and the run was still reported as success.
  - **Why not race it:** abandoning a durable write would leave the record's state unknown, so these writes are not raced.
  - **The fix:** whether the run overran is now measured after that tail. A run that reaches its outcome past the lifecycle bound reports `deadline_overrun` (infrastructure, exit 1), never success. A real test failure, cancellation or timeout keeps its own result and exit.
  - **Still not bounded:** the fsync itself; how long it takes natively is unknown.
- **Run-mode pre-work** (receipt read, Python and git probes, plan check) runs before `withCancellation` installs its handlers.
  - Nothing owned exists then: no container, no intent and no record.
  - A SIGTERM at that point ends the process by default, with nothing to clean.
  - Each step is bounded: the receipt read is aborted at 60 s, and the Python and git probes are killed at 30 s each.
  - Its time counts towards the absolute Run-step bound, because that bound is measured from process start.

**Still unproven (not settled by this part).** Every result here comes from a
fake engine and real local processes:
- How dockerd reacts to a killed client.
- Whether `docker rm --force` removes a paused container on the pinned
  runtime.
- Whether a create can still land after both absence observations.
- How long fsync takes on the host.

Native qualification on the pinned runtime is still open. A fixed reserve or a
transition check is accounting, not a measurement. R1 (sticky negative test
truth after a phase-hook error) and the final authenticated mapping and
delivery are S39's, and this part does not change them.
