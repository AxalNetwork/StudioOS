### 1. What a render approval authorizes

A `render` decision of `approve` on a release manifest now states `render_ceiling_microusd`. This is the most the
render it authorizes may spend, in micro-USD, at most `maxRenderCeilingMicrousd` (USD 100,000).

`decideRelease` enforces the pairing:

| Input | Answer |
|---|---|
| a render approval without a ceiling | `approval_incomplete` (`…, 'render_ceiling'`) |
| a ceiling on anything else (a publish decision, a reject, a withdrawal) | `malformed` |
| a ceiling that is not a positive whole number, or is over the maximum | `malformed` |

A trigger enforces the same pairing on insert, so no other writer can store an approval without a ceiling. Every
other rule of a render approval is unchanged (D5585 §5, D5585-p12 §1): a person, not revoked, not the author, with an
authority the profile accepts, on this digest, and for the line's audience.

### 2. Which approval admission reads: `renderApprovalFor(env, ctx, { assetUid, baseRevisionUid })`

The render builds on a base revision of an asset. A manifest of the venture authorizes that render when:

- it pins exactly that revision of that asset;
- no applied takedown covers the manifest or the piece;
- every policy profile of that piece has at least one live render decision that counts under `decisionNotCounted`.
  That is the publish rule: each reviewer's latest row per profile, re-read against the context's authorities and
  revocations (D5458).

The ceiling works like this:

- A profile authorizes the most that any of its counted approvers allowed.
- The manifest authorizes the least across its profiles, so every profile has agreed to the spend.
- The newest manifest that authorizes wins. At most `RENDER_APPROVAL_LIMITS.manifestsMax` (10) manifests are read,
  newest first.

The answer is `{ approval: { manifest_uid, ceiling_microusd } | null, reason }`, where `reason` is one of `no_base`,
`not_pinned`, `taken_down` or `unapproved`.

A render with no base revision cannot be authorized. A manifest pins revisions, never an asset's future, so a first
render needs a revision (a draft, a storyboard) pinned and approved first.

### 3. What admission does with it

`VenturePolicy.render_approval_required === true` makes every `video_render` and `brand_image` ticket of the venture
need an approval. Any other value, or no value, leaves admission as before. The caller passes
`SwarmFacts.render_approval`, the same way it passes the head revision it read. After the stale-revision check and
before budget and storage:

| `render_approval` | Code |
|---|---|
| not passed (`undefined`): not read | `render_approval_unverified` (503) |
| `null`: none | `render_unapproved` (403) |
| present but not a manifest uid and a positive ceiling | `render_approval_unverified` (503) |
| this ticket's reserve plus what the venture already committed to the piece exceeds the ceiling | `render_over_approved` (409) |

What the venture "already committed to the piece" is `renderApprovalCommitted`: the reserves of its waiting and live
tickets on the same asset, base and modality, plus the charges of its settled ones. A waiting ticket counts here,
unlike in the budget, because it is already a claim on the approval.

So one approval bounds the total spend on rendering one piece, retries included. A new base revision is a new piece
and needs its own approval. The admitted ticket records `render_manifest_uid`. Each refusal is audited with its own
code.

### 4. The audit table, rebuilt

`swarm_admission_events` (D5586-p2) lists its accepted codes in a CHECK, and SQLite cannot widen a CHECK in place. The
second migration rebuilds the table:

1. create it under a new name;
2. copy every row;
3. drop the old table;
4. rename the new one;
5. recreate its index and both append-only triggers.

Every other column, CHECK and the key are unchanged. The owner approved the drop.

The two rules for a drop and a rename (SCHEMA_DRIFT.md §4) hold here:

- **The drop:** there is nothing to export or bookmark, because the table is empty.
- **The rename:** it lands on the same name with the same columns, so the Worker one version back reads and writes
  the rebuilt table unchanged. The rebuild itself runs through Miniflare's D1 in the two `.mjs` tests. The table holds no rows in
production, since nothing writes it yet. `SWARM_AUDIT_CODES` and the CHECK stay pinned together by the D5586-p2
vocabulary test, which now reads the rebuild.

### 5. Recording one

The reviewer route `POST /api/work-graph/releases/manifests/:manifest/decisions` (D5585-p10) takes `purpose`
(`publish` by default, or `render`) and `render_ceiling_microusd`. It is behind the same flag and step-up as before.
The admin log records the purpose.

### 6. A fix on the way

`decideRelease` re-ran itself after a failed insert, so it could answer a concurrent writer's row as a replay. A second
failure recursed again, without end. The new trigger made that reachable: a store that refuses a row would loop
forever. The retry now happens once, and a second failure throws. `attestComponent` has the same shape, and no trigger
of this part touches its table.
