## D867 — Telegram calls by forwarding audio to Axal

#2513, part of #2508. S20 · Codex, continuing S28's preserved groundwork.
Migration 528. Built behind `TELEGRAM_CAPTURE`, default **off** in development,
preview and production. No production operation or real Telegram recording
was performed. This PR is **Part of #2513** as S1 requested; rollout and live
checks remain with the owner and #2514.

### 1. Calls study

Official sources read on 2026-10-23:

- The [Bot API method catalogue](https://core.telegram.org/bots/api) exposes
  messaging and files, with no call/media-session method found. The selected
  path is an existing recording sent to the bot, not a bot joining a call.
- **One-to-one call recording: UNVERIFIED in the client.** The
  [Telegram FAQ](https://telegram.org/faq#q-voice-video-and-group-calls)
  describes calling but provides no recording procedure. Its silence does
  not prove that every current client lacks recording. Axal depends on no
  such feature; the founder must supply a recording made with consent.
- Admins can record group video chats/livestreams. Telegram's
  [group-call documentation](https://core.telegram.org/api/group-calls)
  says the recording goes to the admin's Saved Messages. The
  [recording method](https://core.telegram.org/method/phone.toggleGroupCallRecord)
  is user-only, so it is not Axal's Bot API capture path.
- The [official client announcement](https://telegram.org/blog/chat-themes-interactive-emoji-read-receipts)
  describes audio-only and audio/video recording. Saved recordings can be
  shared as files; the Bot API's forwarding contract excludes protected
  content and service messages. Live forwarding from a current client is
  **UNVERIFIED** here. Axal admits audio only, at most 5 MiB per file; a video
  recording needs its audio track exported separately (D865).
- D864's owner ruling stands: no MTProto user-session capture, login code,
  API hash or session string is accepted. This agrees with D860's fallback
  of a consented recording forwarded to the platform. It does not enable
  live WhatsApp or Telegram calls.

### 2. Entry and consent

The existing secret-checked `/api/channels/telegram/webhook` already receives
private `message` updates. No webhook subscription or public route is added.
Normalisation preserves `/start`, `/confirm` and the manage-chat command;
other private media/answers use `telegram_capture`. A sender must be a human
in their own private chat, with matching sender/chat IDs. Group and bot
messages are ignored. Forwarded origin, name and username never identify the
member. The proven D684 integration is the only mapping to an Axal user.

With capture off, no capture, file request or reply is made. With capture on,
the existing Telegram rollout, active-member and connection-rate gates still
apply. An unlinked sender receives bounded linking instructions; no media
reference is stored for them. A Telegram Business connection is not required
for this private-forward path, but the existing Axal/Telegram link is.

A voice note, audio file or admitted audio document joins the current waiting
capture: up to 20 items within 30 minutes, using the same limits as WhatsApp.
Declared files over 5 MiB are refused immediately. Round videos and other
unsupported types get an audio-only explanation. Nothing is downloaded or
transcribed while waiting.

Axal asks once, with the same all-party attestation wording as WhatsApp:
**Yes, out loud**, **Yes, in writing**, **No**. These are ordinary text buttons
on a one-time reply keyboard, not callback buttons. The
[Bot API keyboard contract](https://core.telegram.org/bots/api#replykeyboardmarkup)
supports this in the bot's private chat; nothing is sent on behalf of a
Business account.

A reply to the exact saved question selects its capture. A bare keyboard
answer is accepted only when one waiting capture is unambiguous. A YES
writes `channel_consents` (`provider=telegram`, `source=member_attested`,
`method=verbal|written`, `capture_id`) and changes waiting to consented in one
D1 batch. A replay creates no second consent. A queue failure after that
batch is recoverable from webhook redelivery. **No** deletes a waiting
capture without a file fetch; it cannot delete a YES's consent in a race.
An already imported recording is deleted through its normal session controls.

### 3. Store and identity

`528_telegram_captures.sql` creates the table and its member/expiry indexes.
It contains Axal IDs, status/times, count, a pinned venture, consent/session
references, the import lease/error, and encrypted media references.
`created_at` is the opening time. `answered_at` and `imported_at` record the
later transitions; unavailable facts stay null.

`media_refs_enc` is columnCipher data bound to the capture UID. Each item
contains its file reference, declared size/type and hashed message reference.
The capture also pins a hash of the proven sender identity. Relinking the
same integration to another Telegram account cannot attest, join or import
that older capture. Successful import forgets the media-reference ciphertext.
No content, name, provider ID, credential or transcript enters a log or queue
payload. Queue jobs carry only Axal capture IDs and retry counts.

Telegram and WhatsApp have independent integer capture IDs. Every capture
consent lookup/deletion includes its provider. Purging or declining one
provider's capture cannot delete or authorize the other's consent.

The venture is the member's most recently updated owned, undeleted venture
at arrival, pinned on the capture. A later venture selection does not move
it. Import goes through `createInterview`, `writableInterview` and
`openSession`, which re-check ownership and the existing interview cap.
No venture means a refusal before download; no venture is invented.

### 4. Import, accounting and failures

The existing `channel_followup` dispatcher sends Telegram capture jobs to
`telegramCapture.ts`; WhatsApp keeps its own handler. A lease permits one
importer; a stale lease can be recovered after 30 minutes. The run re-checks
the feature switch, account/link, pinned sender, consent, expiry and lease.
It re-checks after an awaited download before storing its result.

A new draft interview holds an `in_person` session, `submitted_via=telegram`,
with the member's attestation. Files are fetched sequentially through
`fetchTelegramVoice`: declared size, `getFile` size, bounded stream, container
sniff and audio admission. At most one 5 MiB file is in memory. Telegram's
own 20 MB download limit is not Axal's admission limit. `putChunk` uses the
item's sequence, and a retry skips already stored chunks on the same session.

`finishSession` queues the existing recorder pipeline. Speech-to-text uses
`aiRouter` and its AI-token reservation/settlement. Translation and Validate
proposals remain the shared D857/D859 engine's work, under its processing
preferences; proposals still need founder acceptance. No model is called by
the capture service. “Saved” means the recording is stored and queued, not a
promise that transcription has already finished.

Temporary provider failures get up to three queued attempts. Permanent
refusals and retry exhaustion clean up partial recorder audio/drafts and
retain an authored failure code. The founder gets an at-most-once, safe
refusal sentence, including the size or missing-venture reason. Successful
imports get one Saved reply. No raw provider error is reflected. Interrupted
queue submission resumes the same session without a second download.

### 5. Retention and release

Seven days is Axal's capture-retention bound, **not a claimed Telegram
file-ID expiry**. The channel purge removes expired waiting/unimported
captures and provider-scoped consent. It removes abandoned partial audio
and drafts first. An active lease is not purged underneath its job; expiry denies its next
write, or stale-lease cleanup follows. Imported sessions and their consent/audio remain
until normal session deletion.

Deployment must apply migration 528 before code reaches its purge query,
even while the feature flag is off. There is no new secret or binding:
existing channel bot credentials and the existing queue, D1 and recorder R2
are reused. All three `TELEGRAM_CAPTURE` vars stay off. Rollback is to keep
or set capture off; the additive table can remain. Business chats keep their
separate interviewee-YES rule and are unaffected by private attestation.

Owner preview/live follow-up before enablement: prove linking and one-time
keyboard replies on a real client; forward consented synthetic voice/audio
and an audio-only call recording; verify hold before YES, No, one saved
session and transcription, oversize refusal, relinking and expiry. Record
counts/states only. Do not use customer content in the report. #2514 owns
rollout readiness and the runbook; no task enables capture as a side effect.

### 6. Verification

`telegram_capture_d867.test.ts` drives the real webhook, SQLite consent and
capture stores, recorder R2 and queue with synthetic Telegram/AI. It covers
both YES methods, No, encrypted row binding, unlinked/invalid senders,
item/window bounds, ambiguous/context replies, canary/off gates, provider
consent isolation, concurrent/replayed imports, safe failures, interrupted
queue work, revocation during download and partial-audio expiry/exhaustion.
The recorder test checks its transcription and settled AI-token reservation.
WhatsApp capture and broader channel suites remain regression gates.
Isolated source mutations exercise these behaviors without any provider call.
These are integration tests, not evidence of a completed real-device check.
