## D8333 — Sizing model and scale findings for the CI capacity pool (#8333)

The CI capacity pool gets a tested sizing model, `scripts/ci/capacity-scale-model.mjs`. It computes what a
target number of concurrently running jobs needs in slots, machines, coordinator API calls and queue wait, and
names the enforced limit that stops it first. It changes no limit, workflow, coordinator or executor file, and
it approves no machine, spend or activation. Its planning rows are arithmetic over recorded inputs.

The record cannot drift from the source. `frontend/test/ci_capacity_scale_8333.test.mjs` probes each recorded
ceiling against the validator that owns it, measures the per-pass cost on the real `CiCapacity` loop, and
reproduces receipt admission waiting behind a pass. Raising a limit or changing the loop fails that test until
the record is updated. Every provider fact in the test is synthetic; nothing here is native or live evidence.

### 3. Findings

Each names its owner. "Blocks" says what it holds up; none blocked the source landing of #8234 as a disabled change.

1. **No hosted execution exists.** `hostedReaders()` returns `{}` (`scripts/ci/runners/executor-isolation.mjs:33`,
   the same blob on main and `8c11`), so every `hosted` and `github-8` admission refuses and no hosted execution
   job is composed. The first live milestone needs four hosted Worker shards. Owner S09 with S39. Blocks the
   initial pilot.
2. **The prepared route moves no existing work.** `ci.yml` submits only on `push` (line 226) and the pilot
   runtime refuses any other event (`ciCapacityRuntime.ts:102`). No Worker shard ran on a push in the window:
   the suite on main is skipped when the merge queue already verified it. The pilot therefore adds shards, and
   its result is a separate check run, outside `landing gate`. Moving real work means routing `merge_group`
   (50% of Worker shards) and feeding its aggregate into the check that gates landing, with hosted fallback.
   Pull-request shards (50%) stay hosted: `selectPools` sends untrusted source to hosted, a deliberate control.
   Owner S39. Blocks any benefit to ordinary CI.
3. **Polling exceeds the API budget at four active requests.** At 3,600 to 3,840 calls an hour per request, a
   15,000-an-hour budget holds three; the policy admits eight. Corrections: observe from signed
   `workflow_job` events with `reconcileChild`, read children in list pages of 100, send conditional requests,
   and slow the cadence when nothing is due. Owner S39. Blocks expansion past three requests.
4. **A pass is one sequential chain, and admission waits behind it.** `tick`, `submit` and `receipt` share one
   ordered queue (`ciCapacity.ts`); the test shows a receipt lookup held until the pass ends. At 150 ms a call
   (assumed until measured at deployment), eight requests make a 19-second pass and 38 make one longer than the
   90-second external start window, so executors could not be admitted in time. Corrections: cap calls per
   pass with a cursor, do network reads outside the ordered section, or give each request its own object.
   Owner S39. Blocks expansion.
5. **There is no backlog.** The ninth active request is refused `capacity_active_bound` and not retained
   (`ciCapacity.ts:167`). Today the outcome is defined: explicit rejection, and the ordinary hosted suite runs
   anyway, so GitHub's own job queue is the durable queue. Once routed shards replace hosted ones, a refused
   request needs retained work or a bounded retry. Owner S39. Blocks ordinary-CI routing at volume.
6. **The request shape is literal.** Six parts named `worker-N-of-6` (`ciCapacityPolicy.ts:157`, `:188`), at most
   two external parts a request (`:165`), one included part in flight (`:167`), eight active requests (`:168`).
   That is 48 logical assignments and 17 jobs running off standard hosted runners. Only Worker shards are
   eligible. Correction: reviewed configuration with validated upper bounds, and shard names taken from the plan.
   Owner S39. Blocks expansion.
7. **The external lease is shorter than the median shard.** `externalExecutionMs` is 25 minutes; the measured
   hosted Worker shard is 26.0 minutes at the median and 43.1 at p95, on a 4-vCPU runner, and the job container
   has 2 CPUs. Most external attempts would be cancelled and re-run on hosted, adding work. Correction: a
   12-part plan for delegated runs, or a lease set from the p95 measured on the qualified machine. Owners S39 and
   S31. Blocks a useful pilot result.
8. **Container memory is unmeasured against the workload.** `--memory` is 4 GiB and the 4 GiB `/work` tmpfs,
   where installs and tests both write, is charged to the same limit (`job-container.mjs:178`). Hosted runners
   have 16 GiB. Native qualification must record peak memory including tmpfs. Owners S09 and S31. Blocks native
   qualification sign-off.
9. **The fleet policy ends at 50 single-slot nodes.** `validateScaleConfig` accepts stages 1, 5, 10 and 50, and
   a `FleetNode` holds one runner. The inventory reader reads at most three pages of 100 runners
   (`ciCapacityGithub.ts:63`). Above 50 slots needs new stages; above 300 registrations needs a wider read.
   Owners S39 and S38. Blocks expansion past 50 slots.
10. **The request store fills in about two days at the current cap.** It holds 1,000 requests and prunes only
    those completed more than seven days ago (`ciCapacity.ts`, `CAPACITY_REQUEST_LIMIT`, `CAPACITY_RETENTION_MS`).
    Eight active requests of 25 minutes are 19 an hour, 1,000 in 52 hours; at 120 running jobs it is 21 hours.
    New submissions are then refused `capacity_storage_bound`. Correction: retain by count as well as age.
    Owner S21 with S39 review, by S39's assignment (#8339, D8339). Blocks sustained operation at the
    full-suite policy.

S34's authentication and continuation review is a separate enablement gate and is not assessed here.

### 4. Planning scenarios

`node scripts/ci/capacity-scale-model.mjs`. Assumptions, all parameters: six shards a request, the measured
26.0-minute shard, slots filled to 80%, four 2-CPU, 4-GiB slots a machine plus host overhead, one spare
machine, 150 ms an API call, a 15,000-an-hour budget.

| Running jobs | Slots | Machines (10 CPU, 20 GiB) | Active requests | Completions an hour | API calls an hour today | Needed per request per pass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 120 | 150 | 39 | 20 | 277 | 24,000, the loop's ceiling | 3 or fewer |
| 240 | 300 | 76 | 40 | 554 | 24,000 | 1 |
| 480 | 600 | 151 | 80 | 1,108 | 24,000 | 1, at a 20-second cadence |

Every row is stopped today by findings 3, 4, 6 and 9. With slots filled to 80%, Erlang C gives a mean wait
under a second at all three sizes; wait comes from too few slots, not from pool size.

Demand is the other bound, and hosted capacity is the reason for the wait. All of ci.yml never ran more than 60
jobs at once, and held 56 for half an hour, while up to 365 jobs waited. The suite shards alone would have run
192 at once, and 88 for half an hour, had each started when it was created. The work the prepared route may move
is smaller: merge-queue Worker shards ran 44 at once at most and 24 for half an hour. A pool of 120 slots covers
today's whole suite at its half-hour level; for merge-queue work alone it would sit mostly idle. Machines cost
money that hosted runners on this public repository do not, so the return is the 62.6-minute p95 wait, not
spend. Cost is machine-hours times the provider's rate, read from the provider's billing export with its lag;
neither the provider nor the rate is recorded yet.

### 5. Staged path

1. The existing first milestone (#7888): two qualified Linux machines, four hosted shards, six outcomes.
   Needs finding 1 fixed and S31's native qualification.
2. Twelve running jobs, two active requests: fits every enforced limit and the API budget.
3. Three active requests, the most the API budget holds, until finding 3 is fixed.
4. Stage 50 after findings 3, 4, 7 and 10; then new fleet stages and a reviewed request shape for 120 and above.

Each stage records useful running jobs, wait, dispatch delay, completions an hour, resource peaks, retries,
retained reservations, cleanup and cost before the next begins. Stop when retained reservations grow, the API
budget passes 80%, or cleanup leaves residue; scale down by draining, then removing eligibility, then deleting.
