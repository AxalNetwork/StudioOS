### 1. The routes

Mounted at `/api/work-graph/releases`. Nothing else is mounted under `/api/work-graph`. The file is
`release_reviews.ts`, beside `release_sites_public.ts`: a name sorting after `votes.ts` would take the last registry
slot, which `route_registry_4105` keeps for `votes.ts`.

| Route | Gate | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /pending?tenant_id&company_id[&limit]` | `requireAuth` | the manifests awaiting the caller's decision in that company (§4) |
| `POST /manifests/:manifest/decisions` | `requireStepUp` | `{ tenant_id, company_id, digest, audience, profile, authority, decision, approval_ref?, citations?, disclosure_ref?, rights_ref?, idempotency_key }`, `decision` one of `approve`, `reject`, `request_changes`, `withdraw` |
| `POST /attestations` | `requireStepUp` | `{ tenant_id, company_id, revision_uid, digest, requirement, evidence_ref, authority, idempotency_key }` |

Both writes go to part 1's services unchanged: `decideRelease` with the caller as the reviewer (`purpose: 'publish'`;
render approvals wait for #5586), and `attestComponent` with the caller as the actor. Nothing here restates a rule the
services hold. `approval_ref` defaults to `release_review:<manifest>`: there is no approval-inbox item behind a decision
recorded here, and the service needs a reference.

After the session, every route checks, in this order, each refused 403 and (on a write) audited:

1. **Membership:** `resolveActiveCompany` for the named company: a `user_company_links` row, and the company not
   archived (D1137). Otherwise `not_member`.
2. **Tenant:** the company's release records (lines and grants) must live under the named tenant
   (`companyUnderOtherTenant`, part 8's own check). Otherwise `wrong_tenant`. A manifest or revision of another scope
   is also refused `wrong_tenant` by the services.
3. **Revocation:** a person part 9's resolver finds revoked (`users.is_active = 0`) is `revoked_author`. The session
   already refuses an inactive account (401); this holds even if a session outlives the flag, and the services check it
   again.

### 2. Authority

The context a reviewer acts in holds **their own authorities only**, from `resolveReleaseAuthorities` for exactly
(tenant, company, reviewer), plus the scope's revoked people. Consequences, through the services' existing checks:

- A decision counts only under an authority the person holds now that approves the profile (`no_authority`); the
  author cannot approve their own manifest (`self_approval`); a profile not in the manifest is `policy_profile`; a
  marketing or investor approval without its rights, disclosure or citations is `approval_incomplete`.
- An attestation only under an authority the requirement names and the person holds (`no_authority`). A requirement
  no authority attests (`pr_validated`, `ci_passed`) belongs to a CI run, an agent, and is refused `no_authority` here:
  the service would otherwise accept a person's attestation of it with no authority at all.
- **Being a Super Admin adds nothing.** A Super Admin not linked to the company is `not_member`; linked, they hold only
  what the store grants them. Tested.
- At publication the decision is re-checked against the whole scope's authorities, as before (part 1, D5458): a
  revoked grant or account stops an approval counting there, whatever this route accepted.

### 3. Stale writes and replays

Every write names the digest the person reviewed: another is `stale_approval`, with the manifest (or revision) and the
digest sent. A decision also names the audience it was reviewed for (`audience_forbidden`). A manifest's digest never
changes, so there is no further compare-and-swap to carry: decisions are append-only, and the live one is the
person's latest row per (manifest, purpose, profile). **A withdrawal is a later `withdraw` row by the same person**, so
it can only ever withdraw their own decision, and the approval stops counting at once (tested at publication). The
services' idempotency keys apply: the same request again is a replay (`replayed: true`), another request under the key
`idempotency_conflict`.

### 4. The pending list

`pendingReleaseDecisions` reads the newest unpublished manifests of the scope (at most 200; a line's head is
excluded) and keeps a manifest only if:

- the caller did not author it;
- a policy profile in it is one an authority the caller holds now approves (`POLICY_PROFILES`);
- the caller has no live decision for that profile there (their latest row is absent or a `withdraw`), so a
  withdrawal puts it back and a request for changes takes it off;
- no applied takedown covers it (`takedownsCovering`).

Each item is `{ manifestUid, lineAssetUid, digest, audience, profiles, authorities }` (the profiles still to decide,
and per profile the authorities the caller holds that approve it): ids, digests and names, never a body. With no
authority held, it answers an empty list after no release read at all. Two statements, plus one per manifest that
has a profile left to decide (its takedown check).

### 5. Why the same switch

`WORK_GRAPH_RELEASE_ADMIN` already turns the release workflow on: publication, takedown and the authority store. A
decision only matters to a publication, and an authority only exists once a Super Admin granted one through those
routes, so reviewer routes on with the admin routes off would record decisions nothing can use, and admin routes on with
reviewers off is part 9's state, which still works (decisions through the service). One switch keeps the owner's act
to one value. It answers its own code, `release_review_off`, before the session or D1 is read. A second flag
(`WORK_GRAPH_RELEASE_REVIEW`) can be split off later if the owner wants reviewers dark while the admin routes are live.

### 6. Why a step-up, and the audit

The house requires no step-up for a member's own writes in general. These are not general writes: a release decision is
the sign-off of a counsel, a signatory, a brand owner or a security officer that a public publication relies on, the
same class as `round_calls.ts`'s counsel approval and `trust.ts`'s sanctions review, which take `requireStepUp`. An
email-link session (lower assurance, GOTCHAS "Auth blockers") must not be enough; the SPA's 403 interceptor already
answers `step_up_required` with the step-up prompt. The read is `requireAuth` only.

Audit: the decision or attestation row is the services' own record (append-only, with the reviewer, authority, digest
and idempotency key). Every write that passes the session and step-up is also a `logAdminAction` row
(`work_graph_release_review_decide` / `_attest`, the person as actor; `admin_audit_log` and `activity_logs`), with its
outcome, refused or not, as `research_study_decisions.ts` records a member's decision. A read writes none.
