### 1. The lifecycle

```
pending ──Accept (note?)──▶ accepted ──the intro sent from the Email composer──▶ made
   ├──Not now (note?)──▶ declined
   └──the asker withdraws──▶ withdrawn
```

- **Accept and Made are two steps (D1357 §2.1).** `decide` takes `accepted` or `declined`, once. `made` is refused (400 `decision_invalid`).
- **Made is recorded on send.** The Email composer's `POST /api/mail/send` takes `intro_uid`.
  - **Before anything goes out**, the Worker checks that the caller is the teammate on that request and that it is `accepted`. Anyone else's request is 404. A pending or declined request is 409 `intro_not_accepted`, and a made one is 409 `intro_already_made`. A refused send never leaves the mailbox.
  - **After the send succeeds**, the request is `made`, `mail_sent_uid` names that `mail_sent` row, and the asker is told.
  - **The introducer chooses the recipient.** It is whoever they write to. Axal does not read the person's email from the relationship, and the shared projection has no email to read.
- **Withdraw** is the asker's, on a pending request only (409 `not_pending`). The teammate is told, and can no longer answer it.
- **Each step writes an event row** under its request (`intro_accepted`, `intro_declined`, `intro_made`, `intro_withdrawn`, with `parent_uid`), the shape 653's rows already had. It also writes an audit row and notifies the other side.

### 2. The two notes (D1357 §2.2)

- **`note`** is the reason, and the existing column. The teammate reads it.
- **`context_note`** is the context for the person, optional, at most 1,000 characters. The teammate may forward it.
- **`decision_note`** is the teammate's note on Accept or "Not now", which the asker reads.

Only the two people on a request read any of the three. No one else can read the request at all (§4).

### 3. Outcomes and follow-ups

- **An outcome is a `kind = 'outcome'` row** under the request, recorded by either side once the request is made. The value is one of `meeting_held`, `replied`, `no_reply` or `not_a_fit`, with an optional note of at most 500 characters.
  - **The latest one is what the log reads.** Until one is recorded, the log reads **"Not recorded"** (`NOT_RECORDED`), never "no reply" (D1357 §2.3).
- **A follow-up** is the caller's own row in `relationship_follow_ups`, one per member and request. It is set on an accepted or made request.
  - **Fields:** `due_on`, `done_at` and `snoozed_until`.
  - **State:** due, scheduled, snoozed or done. A new date reopens a done follow-up.
  - **Each side keeps its own.** The asker's follow-up is never shown to the teammate, nor the teammate's to the asker.
- **`follow_ups_due`** on `GET /intro-requests` counts the caller's own follow-ups that are due today, on requests still accepted or made. A snoozed follow-up counts only from the date it was snoozed to. The count ignores the log's filters, because it backs the page's "Follow-ups due · N" chip.

### 4. The access rule on every read

D1356's rule, kept:
- **A request exists only because the teammate shared** with that team when it was asked (D1356 §6).
- **Every read and every step names the caller in its `WHERE` clause.** A request between two other members is 404 "Not found.", in the same words as one that does not exist. It is in no list and no count.
- **The log's `team` filter must name one of the caller's own teams.** Any other key is 404, so the log never confirms that another team exists.
- **A teammate who never shared** is therefore in no request, no log row and no count.

**What is not added:** no contact detail. `person_key` stays the URL or email key 653 already kept, and is not in any reply. The send's `mail_sent_uid` is shown only to the introducer who sent it.

**A choice for #3572 to confirm.** When a teammate turns sharing off after a request was made, the request stays in both people's logs. RI7 says "Requests already sent stay in your inbox". The asker's log keeps it too, as their own record of their own ask. A request records a relationship that was shared when it was asked, so nothing new is revealed.

### 5. The migration

`relationship_interactions` is rebuilt with its ids kept, as 762 and 763 did, because SQLite cannot widen a `CHECK` in place.
- **Vocabulary:** `status` gains `accepted`, and `kind` gains `intro_accepted` and `intro_withdrawn`.
- **New columns:** `context_note`, `decision_note`, `outcome`, `outcome_note`, `recorded_by_user_id`, `mail_sent_uid`, `accepted_at`, `made_at` and `withdrawn_at`.
- **A new CHECK** holds that an `outcome` row has an outcome and no other row has one.
- **Old `kind = 'outcome'` rows are not copied.** No route ever wrote one (D1356 §6), and they could not satisfy the new CHECK.
- **`made_at` is backfilled** from `decided_at` for requests 653 had already marked made.

### 6. Checks

`relationships_intro_lifecycle_d4329.test.ts` uses synthetic people on the real migrated schema. `fetch` answers only the introducer's own mailbox provider. It covers:
- accept, then made on send, which is two steps;
- the decision note;
- made only from the teammate's own send for an accepted request, checked before anything leaves;
- Not now;
- withdraw, and only while pending;
- outcomes from either side once made, with "Not recorded" before;
- follow-ups (due, snoozed, done, reopened), and the due count being the caller's own;
- the log's team and status filters;
- every step refused for anyone not on the request and for a support session;
- audits by ids;
- the migration's columns and CHECKs.

1. The context note dropped on insert.
2. `decide` accepting `made`.
3. The send check passing a request that is not accepted.
4. The send check skipped.
5. Withdraw without its pending guard.
6. The due count without `user_id`.
7. A snoozed follow-up counted as due.
8. `mail_sent_uid` shown to the asker.
9. The team filter dropped from the statement.
10. The team filter accepting a team the caller is not on.
