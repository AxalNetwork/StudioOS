## D1571 — A deck's language: right to left, and capacity per script

- **Status:** Built by the S14 session (#3818, deck v2 D10, part of #3716; 2026-11-01).
- **Builds on:** A8 (`sanitizeTheme` keeps `lang` and `dir`), D1499 (the one geometry), D1517 (`designSystem` capacity and fit), D1554 (`reflow.ts` and its frontend mirror), D1546 (`keepSettings`), and D901 and D904 (the HTML and editable PowerPoint exports).
- **Migration:** none. The language lives in the version's `theme_json`, beside the aspect.
- **Owner decision:** none.

### Why

Plan §0 turns Translate into a typed instruction ("in French"), so a translated deck must read right to left when its language does, and must fit its words at that script's width. Before this change, nothing on the surfaces mirrored right-to-left text: `text-left` was hard-coded on The Grid, the cards strip and the block grid, and Present, print and the exports set no `dir`.

### What was decided

1. **The language is the theme's** (`theme.lang`, a BCP 47 tag, with an optional `dir`).
   - No language means English, left to right, and such a deck saves as it did.
   - The language is set under ⋯ › Theme › Language (`DeckSheets.ThemeSheet`), or by the typed instruction (plan §4.3). It is never a control in the header, whose row holds only presence, ✓ Saved, ⋯, Present and Share ▾.
   - A theme change keeps the language, as it keeps the compliance settings (`keepSettings`).
2. **The direction follows the script** (`designSystem.scriptOf` and `dirOf`, mirrored in `deckLanguage.js`; a test holds the two equal).
   - Arabic (ar, fa, ur) and Hebrew (he, yi) read right to left.
   - Chinese, Japanese and Korean are CJK and read left to right.
   - Every other language is Latin.
   - A theme's own `dir` wins.
3. **Capacity is per script** (`SCRIPT_EM`).
   - Latin keeps `GLYPH_EM` (0.52), so every deck saved before this change fits exactly as it did.
   - Arabic is 0.48 and Hebrew 0.5.
   - A CJK character is one full em, so an area holds about half as many characters.
   - The script is read by `textCapacity`, `capacityAt`, `fitText`, `fitProblems`, the 4:3 reflow (`reflowSlides`, and `reflowCards` on the editor's draft) and `GET /api/decks/:id/grid`. The grid reply also names `lang`, `dir` and `script`.
4. **Every surface that draws a card is direction-aware.**
   - **Where:** The Grid (`GridStage`), the cards strip, `BlockGrid`, Present (`PitchPresent`'s card), the print page (PDF, and PowerPoint's photograph of it), and the offline HTML file.
   - **How:** each puts the deck's `lang` and `dir` on the card's face, and its text aligns to the start (`text-start`, `ps-5`, `padding-inline-start`, `text-align: start`), so it mirrors with `dir`.
   - **Editable PowerPoint:** each block's paragraphs are written `rtl="1" algn="r"` (`rtlParagraph`).
   - **Not mirrored:** the boxes stay where the founder placed them on the grid ("Keeps your layout"); only the words mirror. Editor chrome (menus, tables, the AreaLightbox picker) aligns to the start but keeps the app's own direction.
5. **Fonts.** Each card names its language, so the browser's font fallback picks a face that covers the script. No font is bundled.
