### 8 and 12 parts: projection, not measurement

No run has used 8 or 12 Worker parts. Holding today's Worker work (89.8 test-step minutes at p50) and the
observed ratio of slowest part to mean part (1.31 at six parts) constant:

| Worker parts | Slowest part, min | Worker runner-min | Run |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | 19.6 measured (p50, equal); 20.9 with duration plans on 11-17/18 | 94 measured (96 billed) | 12 runs on 11-25, above |
| 8 | ~14.7 projected | ~95 + rounding, ~99 billed | none: needs a count-only push |
| 12 | ~9.8 projected | ~98 + rounding, ~104 billed | none: needs a count-only push |

More parts also take more of the 60 concurrent hosted jobs (D8421) per queue entry: 11 or 15 suite jobs
instead of 9.

### Recommendation

Keep six Worker parts and two frontend parts. Six is what every measured run used; the projection says eight
would save about five minutes of wall time on the slowest part for roughly three more runner-minutes and two
more concurrent jobs per entry, and twelve about ten minutes for roughly eight. Neither is worth a count change
on a projection. If the owner wants the queue shorter, eight is the one to measure first.

The 15% target is not reachable by packing alone on the evidence above: the best of 78 duration-planned runs
was 19%, the median 41%, and the Worker per-file times move too much between runs for one snapshot to predict.
