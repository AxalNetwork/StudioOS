## D4538 part 4 — Discovery's eligibility gate and evidence-free ranking, as a pure service, measured on queries.v1.json (#4538, #5966)

Part 1 (`D4538.md`) set the recommendation contract (`../SKILL_DISCOVERY_CONTRACT.md`) and the labelled set (`../skill-discovery/queries.v1.json`). This part builds the logic behind that contract, without a route: the hard eligibility gate, then ranking stages 1 to 3. Issue #5966 is slice 1 of #4538 Part A. The route is #5967. Inputs and evidence are #5968.

### What is built

- `cloudflare-worker/src/services/agents/skillDiscovery.ts`, which is pure. It takes:
  - the agent's facts: role, company and granted tool keys;
  - the catalogue's skill facts: each skill's published version (name, purpose, steps, roles, tools, output kind and taxonomy), its scope and company, its status, whether the caller may read it, and whether HQ's suspension covers it.

  It returns `{ outcome, candidates, blocked, clarify, abstain_reason, stages }`. It reads no store, writes nothing to D1, calls no model and attaches nothing. Attaching stays `PUT /api/teams/agents/:uid/skills`, through `attachCheck`.
- `checkDiscoveryRequest` applies the contract's bounds:
  - a task of 1 to 500 characters;
  - `output_kind` from the three kinds;
  - at most three `taxonomy_focus` slugs;
  - a `limit` from 1 to 10, default 5.
- `cloudflare-worker/test/skill_discovery_ranker_5966.test.ts` runs every scenario and measures the result. It sits beside `skill_discovery_queries_4538.test.ts`, which still holds the labels to the gate.

### The gate

`gateOf` refuses in `attachCheck`'s order (`skillLinks.ts`), using its codes, and then applies discovery's own:

| Code | When |
|---|---|
| `not_found` | Another company's skill (or a company skill with no company), or a draft the caller may not read. Listed nowhere. |
| `version_not_published` | No published version |
| `skill_retired` | Retired |
| `skill_suspended` | `skillRunRefusal` at the version, passed in by the caller (D4534) |
| `role_mismatch` | The version does not list the agent's role |
| `tool_not_approved` | The first tool not granted, as `{ key, label }`. It uses the Worker's own `missingTools` and `toolLabel`. |
| `output_mismatch` | `output_kind` was asked for, and the skill produces another |
| `input_unavailable` | **Seam.** Only when the caller passes `seams.inputs` (#4667) |
| `not_runnable` | **Seam.** Only when the caller passes `seams.runnable` (#4534) |

The two seams are not guessed. With no seam passed, neither code is ever returned, and stage 3 says that inputs were not checked.

`blocked` lists the skills that fail the gate but match the task: the same coverage floor as a candidate, best first, at most `limit` of them. It never lists `not_found`. IDF is taken only over skills the caller may read, so a hidden skill cannot move another skill's score either. The test checks this by adding and removing one.

### The relevance method: lexical BM25, in the Worker

**Chosen: BM25 over the published version's text, computed in the Worker.**

- **Terms.** The text is lower-cased and split into words. Stop words are dropped, and each word is lightly stemmed: plural, `-ing` and `-ed` off, a doubled final consonant dropped, then a final `e` dropped.
- **Fields.** Name ×3, purpose ×2, each step ×1, and the tag slugs' words ×1.
- **Constants.** k1 1.2, b 0.75. IDF is taken over the skills the caller may read.
- **Coverage.** A skill's coverage is the share of the task's IDF weight it matches. It is the floor for being a candidate, and it feeds confidence.

The other stages:

- **Stage 1, role and taxonomy fit.** The role is already required by the gate. A tag named in `taxonomy_focus` multiplies the score by 1 + 0.25 per tag. A tag whose slug words the task uses multiplies it by 1.1. Both are reported in `fit.taxonomy` and in `reasons`.
- **Stage 3, output compatibility.** An asked-for kind is a gate. A task that says "draft" or "write", with no kind asked for, multiplies a draft skill's score by 1.1. Inputs are not checked: the stage's `reason` says so (#4667).
- **Stages 4 and 5** report `available: false`. Every candidate's `evaluation` and `operational` are `{ available: false, reason }`, naming #4533 and #4536.
- **Use count** is accepted as a skill fact and never read. The test flips it and checks that the answer does not change.

**Alternatives considered:**

- **Embeddings** from Workers AI or a provider's API.
- **A model judging** relevance.

Either would send the task text, which a person writes and which may name company specifics, to a model, and would add cost, latency and an answer that changes with the model's version. Lexical matching sends nothing anywhere. It is deterministic, and each candidate's reasons can name the matched terms. A semantic method can replace stage 2 later, with its own decision, once a larger labelled set shows where lexical matching misses.

**What it sends where: nothing leaves the Worker.**

- **What the service reads:**
  - the request's task text, output kind, taxonomy focus and limit;
  - the agent's role, its company and its granted tool keys (the route will read these from the agent row, never from the client);
  - each catalogue skill's published-version text and metadata.
- **What it never reads:** company records, connector payloads, tool results, integration identifiers and secrets. It makes no network call and logs nothing.
- **Where the result goes:** to the caller only.

### Outcomes

- **`no_suitable_skill`** in two cases. The `abstain_reason` names the act, or names the best blocked skill and its missing tool, or says nothing fits.
  - **The task asks for an act no skill performs.** A task word in the closed `ACTS` list (send, email, book, pay, wire, transfer, buy, sign, submit, tweet, delete and a few others) triggers it. Matching candidates may still be returned, at `low` confidence, with an `uncertainty` saying the skill only prepares a brief or draft for a person to act on. This matches the set's `no-skill-send-email` label, where the investor-update draft is acceptable.
  - **No eligible skill reaches the coverage floor of 0.2.**
- **`ambiguous`** in two cases. Every candidate is then `low`, and `clarify` asks "Which do you mean: A (…), or B (…)?" with the first two skills' purposes.
  - The second candidate scores within 0.85 of the first.
  - The task has a single content term that at least two candidates match.
- **`recommend`** otherwise. The first candidate is `high` when it covers at least half the task's weight and the runner-up is below 0.6 of its score. Otherwise it is `medium`, and `uncertainty` says why.

**Candidates:**

- A candidate below half the top score is dropped.
- At most `limit` are returned.
- `version` is the skill's published version, which an attach would pin. A draft is never named, and an unpublished skill is gated.

### Measured on queries.v1.json

These figures are measured on the labelled set's 15 scenarios. They are not a claim about the full catalogue.

- **Set:** `queries.v1.json`, version 1.
- **Catalogue:** the 182 library skills on main at `95210ad1ab`, each as a published version 1, plus the set's inline fixtures.

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Precision@1 (relevant or acceptable), over the 10 `recommend` scenarios | 1.0 |
| Precision@3, over the candidates returned (at most 3) | 1.0. It equals precision@1, because each `recommend` scenario returned exactly one candidate. |
| Recall@3 of `relevant`, over the 10 scenarios with relevant labels | 1.0 |
| Rank of the first relevant skill | 1 in 10 of 10. None missed. |
| Ineligible-recommendation rate (`must_not_return` in `candidates`) | 0: none of the 8 labelled ones, at limits 1, 5 and 10 |
| Outcome matches | 15 of 15 |
| Abstention matches (`no_suitable_skill` where labelled) | 4 of 4, with no false abstention |
| Ambiguous scenario | `ambiguous`, both labelled skills as the first two, every candidate `low`, a question naming both |

The test pins exactly these numbers, as this block:

```json measured

  "scenarios": 15,

```

A ranker change that moves a number must change this block too. A new set (`queries.v2.json`) is measured and recorded with its own version.

**How to read these numbers:**

- **They are in-sample.** The thresholds were chosen with this set in view, so 15 of 15 shows the method can satisfy the labels, not how well it generalises.
- **Some margins are narrow.**
  - The weakest true match is `postmortem-cto`, at coverage 0.22 against the 0.2 floor. The task's "went wrong" and "yesterday" appear in no skill, so only "write" and "outage" match.
  - The strongest eligible skill on the non-act abstention (`no-skill-travel`) reaches 0.08.
  - The ambiguous pair scores 0.98 of each other.
- **The ineligible rate does not depend on ranking.** It is zero because the gate runs before ranking. It would stay zero whatever the scores.

**Known lexical limits:**

- **Stemming does not merge every word family.** "Summarise" and "summary", for example, stay apart.
- **Generic verbs can carry a match.** An out-of-set probe, "Summarise the contract with our reseller" for a CEO without the contract skill's role, came back `ambiguous` between two "summary" skills, at low confidence. That is the safe failure, but a semantic stage 2 would do better.
- **The `ACTS` list is closed.** An act it does not name is ranked like any other task.

### Departures from the contract's response

- **`stages` is added to the response.** The contract says that each stage reports what it used, and that a stage with no source says so. `stages` carries that: stages 1 and 2 are available; stage 3 is available, with a reason while inputs are unchecked; stages 4 and 5 are `available: false`.
- **`score` and `coverage` are added to each candidate,** so the order can be inspected.
- **`workflow_context` is not read.** It is reserved for #4535.
- **Taxonomy areas are inferred from tag slug words,** not from the taxonomy's labels. The labels live in D1, and the service reads no store.

### Tests

`skill_discovery_ranker_5966.test.ts`, 14 tests:

- The measurement, pinned to `MEASURED`, and to this file's block.
- Every scenario's outcome is its label's, and every relevant skill is ranked first.
- No `must_not_return` skill is a candidate at limits 1, 5 and 10. Every candidate passes `gateOf`.
- Another company's skill is in neither list. This holds for a fixture, and for a copy of the right skill under another company. The agent's own company copy is a candidate. A hidden skill moves no score.
- A role mismatch and a missing tool are in `blocked` with their codes and `tool`, and the abstention reason names the skill and `mercury.read`.
- The ambiguous scenario: `low` confidence, the labelled pair, and a question naming both. The abstentions have reasons, and offer only `acceptable` skills, at `low`.
- Versions:
  - a candidate pins the published version (3 when published at 3);
  - an unpublished skill is `version_not_published`;
  - a draft the caller may not read is named nowhere;
  - a suspended skill is `skill_suspended`.
- Evidence blocks are exactly `{ available, reason }`, false. A flipped use count changes nothing.
- The gate's order and the seams (`input_unavailable` and `not_runnable`), each only when wired and after the other codes.
- The request bounds and the limit.
- Taxonomy focus raises a score and is reported.
- The term rules.
- A source check:
  - the service has no DB, `fetch`, `env`, SQL, Hono or attach call;
  - it imports only `./skills` and `./skillLinks`;
  - `toolLabel` is its one value import from `./skillLinks`.
