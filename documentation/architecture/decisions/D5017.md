### 1. A refresh never changes a licence

`POST /api/admin/licences/:uid/pricing/snapshot` takes one D5015 country snapshot per country of the basis under the
**active** model, then appends one `licence_pricing_snapshots` row holding the combined figures. It writes no licence
term and answers `licence_changed: false`. Opening an annual review does the same and adds a review row. Licence
snapshots are append-only (two triggers), as country snapshots are.

### 2. The apply is the D4694 terms write

`POST /api/admin/licences/:uid/pricing/apply` names the snapshot and the `terms_revision` the editor read. One batch
holds these statements:

1. the decision insert;
2. the core-terms `UPDATE … WHERE terms_revision = ?`;
3. the schedule's minimum guarantee, when one is accepted (D5953);
4. the review close, when a review is named.

The decision insert is guarded by an `EXISTS` on the same revision. A stale editor therefore writes nothing: no terms
and no decision. It gets `409 terms_conflict` with the current revision and terms.

The apply also refuses a snapshot that no longer describes the licence. That is a snapshot whose territory, basis or
basis revision has changed since it was taken (`409 snapshot_stale` / `pricing_basis_stale`).

### 3. What the core terms hold, and what they do not

`territory_licences` stores two of the five figures:

- `annual_fee_cents` holds the annual fee;
- `revenue_share_bps` holds the royalty.

The apply writes those two, and sets `currency` to the recommendation's. A licence held in another currency is refused
with `409 currency_mismatch`, so a figure and its unit always move together.

The minimum royalty has no core-term column. Since D5953 (the owner's Q9 = A), the apply writes the accepted minimum
royalty into the schedule's minimum guarantee (`licence_terms.minimum_guarantee_cents`, §4.4). It does so in the same
revision-guarded batch, and creates the schedule row when there is none. An accepted minimum royalty of `null` leaves
the schedule untouched. D5953 holds the mapping and the rules.

Two figures have no term at all: the initial fee and the regulatory surcharge. They are recorded as accepted on the
decision and shown. Nothing invoices them.

### 4. Override

An override is any of these:

- an accepted figure that differs from the recommendation;
- an apply on an unavailable recommendation;
- any apply under the `hq_override` basis.

An override needs `override_reason`. Without one the apply returns `400 override_reason_required`. Every apply also
carries `reason`. The decision row records:

- the actor (`created_by`);
- the reason and the override reason;
- the recommended and accepted figures;
- the snapshot;
- the terms revision before and after;
- the effective date;
- the next review date (by default a year after the effective date, and HQ can set its own).

An unavailable recommendation is never applied as zero. `accepted_value_required` asks for the figures instead.

### 5. Multi-country basis

`licence_pricing_policies` holds one current basis per licence, with a revision. Writes to it are compare-and-swap;
`expected_revision` is 0 for the first write. The bases are:

- `primary_country`: one country of the territory prices the licence.
- `basket_weighted`: every country of the territory has a weight. The weights total exactly 10000 basis points, and the
  route and the pure rule both refuse anything else. The weights and the country snapshot each one came from are frozen
  on the licence snapshot (`components_json`).
- `hq_override`: HQ sets the figures. The named country's recommendation is shown for reference, and every apply is an
  override.

A single-country licence with no recorded basis is priced from that country (`implied: true`, policy revision 0). A
multi-country licence with no basis is refused at snapshot with `409 pricing_basis_required`.

A basket never prices from only the countries that happen to have data. One unavailable country makes the whole
recommendation unavailable. Basket fees are weighted, then re-rounded to the model's commercial increment. The unrounded
value stays in the trace.

The decision's existing foreign key (`snapshot_uid` → `country_pricing_snapshots`) names the heaviest-weighted country
snapshot. The new `licence_snapshot_uid` column names the whole basket.

### 6. The agreement and invoices

The apply never instantiates, sends or edits a contract. When the annual fee, the royalty or (D5953) the minimum
guarantee changes and the licence has a current contract, the apply needs `acknowledge_agreement_mismatch: true`; without it the answer is
`409 agreement_ack_required`.

Afterwards, the pricing read reports `agreement.state: 'mismatch'` while any decision that changed a contractual figure
is newer than the current contract. HQ clears that state through the existing replacement: it instantiates a new
contract on the Contract tab, which supersedes the old one (D4671).

Issued `licence_fee_invoices` are never read or written. Future invoices read `annual_fee_cents` under their own rules
(D802). The test compares the invoice rows before and after an apply.

### 7. The push is reported apart from the commit

The apply pushes to the branch like every terms write (D272). The answer carries:

- `committed: true`;
- the push result (`pushed`);
- `push_outcome`, which is `committed` or `partial` (D4707).

The `terms_changed` licence event records the same outcome, with `source: 'country_pricing'`. It reuses
`terms_changed` because `licence_events.event` is a CHECKed list (migration 553). Adding a value to that list would mean
rebuilding a sealed table for a label.

### 8. Annual review

`licence_pricing_reviews` holds at most one open review per licence (a partial unique index). The review is classified
against the current terms as one of:

- `unchanged`;
- `increase`;
- `decrease`;
- `incomplete` (no recommendation, or no current fee).

HQ closes it in one of four ways:

- **keep** the current terms, with a reason;
- **postpone**, with a reason and a later date;
- **apply** the recommendation, through the apply with `review_uid`;
- **override** it, the same way.

A trigger keeps a closed review closed and requires each update to move `revision` by exactly one.

The next review date comes from the first of these that applies:

1. the open review's due date;
2. otherwise, the later of the latest decision's `pricing_review_on` and the latest postponement.

**Not built here:** a scheduled sweep that opens due reviews by itself. The read reports `review_due`, and HQ opens the
review from the panel. The review queue across licences is #5020's.

### 9. Audit

Every write goes to `admin_audit_log` through `logAdminAction`, with the actor and the reason:

- basis set;
- snapshot taken;
- applied;
- review opened;
- review resolved.

The apply also writes the licence's own history (`licence_events`). All writes need the Super Admin write bar.
