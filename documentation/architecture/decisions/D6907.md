## D6907 — Ownership & rights: read a right's clause from the investor's own signed documents (#6907)

- **Status:** Worker part built 2026-11-16 (`Part of #6907`). The proposal filter, the Accept path and the rail card wait on #6868.
- **Builds on:**
  - D1058: reporting duties come from signed paper, through the closing line's envelope and its `body_sha256`;
  - D6228: Eadwyn proposes a right that is in the paper but not recorded, and never records one;
  - D6868, which files this issue.

### The problem

PRM18's rail has Eadwyn say, for example: "This Investors' Rights Agreement §3.3 grants you an observer seat; it is not recorded here. Add it?" It cites the document, the clause and the page.

D1058 maps a fixed set of template clauses to reporting duties (`INFORMATION_RIGHTS`), but nothing reads the clauses of a signed document for any right.

### What is built

**`services/signedClauses.ts`:**
- **Which documents.** The investor's own received closing lines for the company, each line's envelope, and only an envelope whose status is `completed`. This is the source D1058's duties use. Nobody else's line is read. Newest first, at most 50.
- **When the body is read.** Only when the stored body's SHA-256 equals the envelope's `body_sha256`, the hash both parties signed over.
- **When it is not.** A missing body is "The signed text is not stored with this document." A body that does not match is "The stored text does not match what was signed, so it is not read." Either way the document returns `readable: false` with its reason and no clauses, never "none found".
- **Splitting** (`splitClauses`). The body is split at clause openers: a markdown heading, or a section number as the paper writes it ("3.", "3.3", "3.3(a)", "§ 3.3", "Section 3", "Article 3"). Each clause keeps:
  - its number and heading;
  - its text, at most 2,000 characters;
  - its line;
  - its page, counted from form feeds where the body marks pages, or null where it does not. A page is never guessed.
- **Matching** (`matchRights`). Six right types, by the words the paper uses: `pro_rata`, `information_rights`, `board_seat`, `observer_seat`, `liquidation_preference`, `anti_dilution`. One clause can write more than one. A match is a candidate for a person to confirm; nothing here records a right.

**`GET /api/portfolio-clauses/:projectUid[?type=…]`** (`routes/portfolio_clauses.ts`):
- **Who:** an investor, not an admin, holding the company through a confirmed commitment (the held scope of Positions). Not held and unknown answer alike: 403 `not_held`.
- **Filter:** `type` narrows the right types; an unknown one is 400.
- **Answer:**
  - per document: its envelope uuid, title, type, signing day, `readable` and reason, and its matching clauses (text and location only);
  - `summary`: the document count, the `unreadable` count, and clauses per type. With the unreadable count, the rail can say a document could not be read.
- The body is never returned beyond the matching clauses.

**Client:** `frontend/src/lib/api/portfolioClauses.js` (`portfolioClausesApi.get`).

### Choices made here

- **Right types.** The six types are the ones D6868's "Backend required" lists for the rights table. When #6868's table lands with its own keys, the mapping is one table here.
- **Envelopes.** Only envelopes reached through the investor's own closing lines are read, not every envelope addressed to them. That is the "signed documents the investor holds" for this company that D1058 already trusts.
- **No model.** Matching is by the paper's words, which can be checked and tested. Eadwyn's wording of the proposal is the rail's job, over these matches.
