## D7674 part 4 — People-only controlled apply and the professional person projection (#7674)

**Status:** implemented for review, 2026-11-27. One new route on the existing router, one new service, a shared
refactor of the part 2 apply, a narrower member projection for people, synthetic tests. No migration. No production
apply has run, and the route refuses one. D7674, part 2 and part 3 are left as they were; this file supersedes their
"people path" bullets under "Not built here".

### Why

The owner answered the people question on #7674 (answer B, 2026-11-27): signed-in members may read a
**professional-only** projection of people: name, a `linkedin.com/in/` profile URL, geography, sectors and
affiliations. No contact field (email, phone, address, personal site) and no raw source cell reaches a member. People
are never uploaded through the organisation route, which keeps refusing them (`people_records_refused`).
Restricted-contact projections stay unbuilt. #7739 Q1 A (the Super Admin's browser to write-barred Worker routes) and
Q4 A (no real production apply while the queue alert is open) stay binding.

### The route (`network-directory-people-apply-v1`)

`POST /api/network/directory/admin/people-apply`: the same gate as the organisation apply (`requireSuperAdminWriteBar`,
then `support_session_refused`), the same 4 MiB byte-capped body, and the same envelope fields with
`contract: "network-directory-people-apply-v1"`. The envelope's shared checks run in the same order (submitted SQL,
unknown field, contract, target environment, **`production_apply_held`** from the one `PRODUCTION_APPLY_HELD` constant,
which this change does not lift, schema, mapping version, shapes). An organisation upload sent here is
`contract_unknown`; a people upload sent to the organisation route is refused there as before.

Idempotency is the organisation apply's, unchanged: the reviewed `run_uid` and the planner's per-row checkpoint keys
(`network_directory_import_rows`). A retry applies only missing batches, a completed run replays with
`replayed: true` and writes nothing, a failed batch rolls back alone and the same upload resumes it, and the run is
marked complete only after the read-back counts (now including people and affiliations, held to the plan's own
numbers) reconcile and no protected entry changed. Each call that wrote is audited as `network_directory_people_apply`
with ids and counts only.

### Which fields a person upload may carry

| Source | Accepted columns | Becomes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `airtable_angels` | `Name`, `Headquarters`, `Sector Focus`, `LinkedIn` | name, geography, sectors, profile |
| `folk_french_vcs` | `Person`, `First name`, `Last name`, `Urls`, `Industry`, `Companies`, `Job title` | name and its parts, profile, sectors, affiliations and their title |

Everything else is refused, including professional-looking columns the owner did not list (`Type`, `Investment Stage`,
`Currently investing?`, `Previous Investments`, Folk `Website`): an operator removes them before upload, as #7739 Q3 B
already requires for the organisation path. Another source is `source_not_people_mapped`. A manifest row must be
`entity_kind: "person"` (`organisation_records_refused`); it may carry the person keys (`affiliation_disposition`,
`title`, `title_disposition`). `manifest.organizations` may name only Folk organisations that a person's `Companies`
cell names, the affiliation targets, and nothing else (`organisation_records_refused`). The server's re-plan checks
again that no uploaded record became an organisation.

### Refuse, not drop

The organisation contract **refuses** a disallowed field (`contact_field_refused`, `field_not_permitted`) and writes
nothing; this contract does the same, so a reviewed upload is applied exactly as reviewed or not at all. Dropping a
cell would change the records after the operator reviewed their digest. Every offending cell is **counted before
anything is refused**, and the refusal carries `contact_fields`, `fields_not_permitted`, `invalid_linkedin_urls` and
`records_affected`, never a value or a column name. A contact field is: a column outside the mapping whose name
reads as a contact channel or a personal site (email, phone, mobile, tel, address, contact, cell, WhatsApp, Telegram,
Twitter, website, homepage, site, URL, links, blog, Instagram, Facebook, Skype, Signal), or whose value holds an email
or a phone number; an email, a phone number or a link in an accepted column; a non-LinkedIn link in the profile
column; an email anywhere in the manifest; a contact-shaped title override; an email channel review; or a personal
LinkedIn profile given as a firm's website. Contact refusals come first, then unmapped columns, then malformed
LinkedIn cells.

### LinkedIn, strictly

A profile cell is empty (no profile) or exactly `https://linkedin.com/in/<slug>` or `https://www.linkedin.com/in/<slug>`,
an optional trailing slash, the slug 3 to 100 letters, digits, hyphens or percent-escapes (`LINKEDIN_PROFILE`). Refused
as `linkedin_url_invalid`: `http:`, no scheme, a country subdomain, `/company/`, `/in/` with no or a short slug, a query,
a fragment, a further path, credentials, a port, an upper-case host, a look-alike host, a list, or text. The planner
then stores its normal form (`https://linkedin.com/in/<lower-case slug>`), and the re-plan holds every planned person
profile to the same rule and every person's website to `null`. The member projection applies the rule a third time: a
stored person link that is not a strict profile reads `null`. No employment, account or identity is inferred from a
profile, an email domain or a website.

### What a member reads about a person

`projectPerson` (used for every person in search, detail and the operator list): `uid`, `kind`, `name`, `first_name`,
`last_name`, `profile_url`, `geography`, `sectors`, `verification`, and `source` (the source's name and observation
date, so a source-only claim and its freshness read as such). Not in it, whatever the row holds: `website`, `stages`,
`source_categories`, `currently_investing`, `previous_investments_source_text`, the account or company link, any
private source cell, review item or duplicate candidate. Organisations keep the D7674 projection.

The detail's affiliations are unchanged from D7674 and part 3: each visible organisation's name and uid, the title,
the relation (`source_association` for every imported one), `current`, `former` or `unknown` from what a reviewer
recorded (an import records none, so it reads `unknown`), the dates as recorded, the verification state and the
observation date. Merged-away entries answer their canonical entry, and associations follow redirects, as part 3 says.

**Publication.** `POST /admin/entries/:uid/publish` now publishes a person through the same reviewed action and the
same readiness (latest observation, same-time conflict, open review); the `people_publication_held` refusal and the
operator list's flag of that name are gone. A person is only ever read by members after an
operator publishes it. Two people sharing a LinkedIn profile stay `review` rows with a `duplicate_profile_identity`
item, create no entry and so never appear; namesakes with different or no profiles are separate entries.

### Not built here

- Restricted-contact projections, and any contact field at any visibility: no policy permits one.
- Recording an affiliation's currentness or dates through a route (they are read as recorded; an import records none).
- The member and operator UI, including #7679's review and coverage screens.
- Production counts: none, because nothing was applied in production; `PRODUCTION_APPLY_HELD` stays `true`.

### Rollback

Revert the PR. Nothing in the schema changed. People applied in a non-production environment stay staged entries and
immutable observations; a person an operator published can be suppressed through the D7674 route.
