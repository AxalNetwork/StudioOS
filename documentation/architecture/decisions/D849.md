## D849 — MCP platform security: one limit per member across keys and grants, the limits read, no credential in a log, and the cross-tenant matrix

- **Status:** #2472, slot S14, part of #2463 (`documentation/architecture/MCP_API_PLATFORM.md` §4, §6). Built in two PRs: PR 1 (#2499, merged) is §2 and §4; PR 2 is §3 and §5.
- **Basis:** `origin/main` at `87b87a48`. Worker paths are relative to `cloudflare-worker/src/`.
- **Stacks on:** D666 and D669 (grants re-checked on every call), D841 (member API keys), D788 (the member's AI spend limit and its notices), D120 (support sessions).

### 1. The decision

Plan §4 sets two limits: 60 calls a minute per key or connection, and 120 a minute per member **across every key and grant**, both fail-closed. Until now only the first existed (`withinGrantLimit`), so ten connections were ten times one connection's limit. A member can now see the limits in force, a support session cannot buy AI tokens, and a guard keeps every credential out of the lines people read later.

### 2. What was built (PR 1)

- **The member limit.** `mcp/grants.ts` adds `USER_CALLS_PER_MINUTE = 120` and `withinUserLimit(env, userId)`. It counts under `mcp_rl:user:<id>:<minute>` in `RATE_LIMITS`, the key every grant and (with #2470) every API key shares. The grant limit and the member limit share one counter helper, `withinMinute`, which fails closed on any KV error. `callTool` checks it after the grant limit, for every call made over a connection (a grant id; with #2470, a key). An in-app call with no grant (Eadwyn, D854) is not counted: it has its own per-turn caps. Over the limit it logs `rate_limited` and answers *"Too many calls from your account across its connections. Wait a minute and try again."* The tool never runs.
- **The limits read.** `GET /api/developer/limits` (`routes/developer_limits.ts`, client `developerKeysApi.limits()`), signed-in session only:
  `{ window_seconds: 60, member: { calls_per_minute }, connection: { calls_per_minute }, keys_enabled, keys: [ { uid, name, prefix, rate_per_minute, monthly_limit_microusd | null } ] }`.
  The figures are the constants `callTool` enforces, imported, so the read cannot drift from them. Only live keys are listed; with `API_KEYS_ENABLED` off the caps still read and `keys` is empty. It never returns a secret, a hash or a key's scopes. An `axk_` bearer gets `401 session_required`.
- **A support session cannot buy AI tokens.** `POST /api/orders/intent` refuses a cart holding a token pack under an impersonation token with `403 { error: 'support_session_refused', message: 'A support session cannot buy AI tokens.' }`. It is judged before the pack's own refusals and before any Stripe call, and writes no order row. Other orders are unchanged. `/confirm` needs no check: it only fulfils a payment intent `/intent` created.
- **No credential in a log.** `scripts/check-secret-logging.mjs`, in `test:guards`, reads every `console.*` call, `logActorAct(...)` call and `activity_logs` write (the `prepare` and its `.bind`) in the Worker. It fails on an identifier that contains `bearer` or `secret`, outside strings and inside `${}`, and on the text `axk_`. An object key names a field and passes. The tree passes it with an empty allowlist.

### 3. The per-key cap and the alerts (PR 2)

PR 2 landed after #2470 (D842, keys on `/mcp`) and #2466 (D844, `meteredCall`). No migration. The code is `services/apiKeyLimits.ts`, hooked into `services/metering.ts` and `mcp/server.ts`.

- **A key's holds are its own.** A metered call through a key holds under `key:<uid>:exec:<execution>:<component>` instead of `exec:<execution>:<component>`. Migration 525's idempotency key then says whose the hold is, so a key's month spend is a sum over its own rows in `ai_budget_reservations`, the same rows D788's member limit reads:
  - pending holds count at what they hold;
  - holds in review count at the recorded cost when it is higher;
  - settled holds count at what they charged;
  - refunded holds count at nothing.
- **`key_limit_reached`.** When the key has `monthly_limit_microusd`, `meteredCall` refuses the call if the key's spend plus the quote would pass the cap. The check runs twice:
  - **before the hold**, leaving out the call's own earlier hold on a replay;
  - **again right after it**, counting the new hold. When this re-check fails, the hold is released (refunded) and the call refused, so calls racing past the cap together are refused, never both charged.

  A replay never re-checks, so it cannot release the hold its first attempt still runs under. A free call (quote 0) holds nothing and is never capped. A key or spend that cannot be read refuses (fails closed). On `/mcp` the refusal is the tool error `{ error: "This API key has reached its monthly AI token limit. …", code: "key_limit_reached" }`, with no amounts, like D844's other ledger refusals. The member reads the figures in the limits read. The usage row records `refused` / `insufficient_tokens`.
- **Alerts, on D788's path** (`notify()`, `channels: ['in_app', 'email']`, `category: 'billing'`). Each is deduplicated in `RATE_LIMITS`, and a notice that fails never fails the call:
  - **`api_key_limit`** fires after a settled charge passes 80% or 100% of a key's cap. Only the highest threshold passed is sent, once per key, month and threshold, linking to `/developer/keys`. A key's charges in a month only grow, so no lower threshold can follow a higher one.
  - **`api_key_failures`** fires when, after a failed or timed-out call through a key, the key has made at least 50 calls in the last hour and more than 20% of them failed (`outcome = 'error'`; a refusal is not a failure). It is sent once per key and hour, linking to `/developer/usage`.

  A notice names the key by its name and public prefix, and carries only the member's own figures.
- **The limits read** gains `used_microusd` for each key: the key's month spend, or `null` when it cannot be read (never 0).
- **The key rows of the matrix.** A key carrying each principal's own ventures refuses every company tool on any other venture, before it runs, and reaches its own. A key whose creator lost the venture reaches it no more. D842's own tests already cover a revoked, expired or unknown key and the per-key rate (`mcp_api_keys_d842.test.ts`).
- **Not covered by the key cap:** model runs a tool makes through `aiRouter` inside a call. They hold under their own unkeyed reservations, as every `aiRouter` run does, and the member's own D788 limit governs them. Today every tool is priced at 0 (D844 v1), so the key cap bites once a tool is priced.

### 4. Verified

- `cloudflare-worker/test/mcp_security_matrix_d849.test.ts` (14 tests): founders A and B, an investor and an admin.
  - Consent gives each only their own ventures.
  - Every registered tool that takes a company refuses every company outside the caller's grant, before it runs. Each founder's own company reaches every tool.
  - A tool with no company argument sees only the caller's own live companies, even under a grant that names one it was never given.
  - Lost ownership and a deactivated account refuse every tool.
  - The member limit spans connections, leaves another member untouched, fails closed, and does not count an in-app call.
  - Every `/api/developer/*` route: another principal's key cannot be changed, revoked or rotated (404, the row unchanged); no read lists another member's key; a key cannot name another's venture; a support session cannot write; a key and no session reach nothing.
  - The limits read, with keys on and off.
  - A tool result that carries instructions comes back as one JSON value, through the real MCP handler. Exactly one call is logged and nothing is written.
- `cloudflare-worker/test/orders_support_session_d849.test.ts` (3 tests): the refusal, the member's own purchase, and a support session's non-token order.
- `frontend/test/secret_logging_guard_d849.test.mjs` (3 tests): seven leak shapes are found; prose, field names and comments pass; the tree passes and `test:guards` runs the guard.
- Every assertion was mutation-checked both ways: 16 mutations of the limit, grant, consent, limits-read and support-session code; 3 of the order refusal; 9 of the guard. All were killed.

### 5. Verified (PR 2)

- `cloudflare-worker/test/api_key_limits_d849.test.ts` (10 tests, on the AI budget fixture with migrations 510, 523, 524 and 525):
  - **Spend.** A key's holds are tagged with it, and its month spend is its own: pending, review, settled, refunded, another member, a new month.
  - **The cap.** It refuses before the run and the hold, an exact fit passes, and another key, no key and a free call are untouched.
  - **Races.** Two calls racing past the cap are never both charged.
  - **The re-check.** A racer's hold landing between the check and the hold gets the late hold released.
  - **Replays.** A replay is not counted twice and never releases its first attempt's hold.
  - **Fails closed.** An unreadable or malformed key runs nothing.
  - **`api_key_limit`.** It fires once at 80% and once at 100%, and a jump past both sends only 100%.
  - **The limits read's spend.** It reads, and is null when it cannot be read.
  - **`api_key_failures`.** It needs more than 20% of at least 50 calls, ignores refusals and calls over an hour old, never counts another key's or member's calls, and fires once an hour.
  - **`callTool` through a key.** It returns the `key_limit_reached` result, records the usage row, and a failing call raises the failure notice.
- `mcp_security_matrix_d849.test.ts` gains the key rows (16 tests in all). The limits read's `used_microusd` is `null` there, because that fixture has no ledger.
- 23 mutations of PR 2's code, each caught both ways. They cover:
  - the hold key and both checks;
  - the replay exclusion and the replay skip;
  - the release;
  - the threshold notice, its arithmetic and its deduplication;
  - the review cost and the member scope;
  - the failure window, share, outcome and call;
  - the limits read's field and its null.

One mutation (an `AND 1` added to the failure query) is equivalent, and was replaced with a real one.


### Integration after D842 merged (PR #2499)

The shared fail-closed minute counter retains D842's configurable API-key rate
and its key-failure throttle. The member cap counts both OAuth grants and the
now-mounted API-key principal (`keyUid`), while in-app calls with neither remain
outside it. A member-limit refusal uses D842's structured `rate_limited` result
and execution id, preserving D843's usage event and rate-limit category. This
allocates no new cap, decision or migration number; per-key spending and alerts
remain the separate PR 2 work above.
