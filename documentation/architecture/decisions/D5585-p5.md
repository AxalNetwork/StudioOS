### 1. Which email an envelope gets

`signingEmailFor` chooses by the envelope's document type. A `work_graph_release` envelope gets the release sign-off
email. Every other envelope gets the agreement email (`sendAgreementAssignedEmailResult`), with its subject, words and
sender unchanged.

The release email names the release by the ids its envelope body already records. `releaseSignFacts` reads the line,
the manifest and the digest back from the stored body, so a reminder and a signer's resend send the same email as the
first send. If a release envelope's body names no release, the email is refused (recorded as a failed mail). It is
never sent the agreement email instead.

### 2. The words

| Part | Release sign-off email |
|---|---|
| From | `StudioOS releases <noreply@axal.vc>` |
| Subject | `Sign release <line uid> (sha256:<first 12 hex of the digest>)` |
| Body | The owner is asked to sign the release because they own its line. The release line, the manifest and `sha256:<digest>`. "Signing records your signature against this manifest digest in the e-signature record. You can decline instead." The signing link. |
| Footer | The agreement email's: the link is unique and expires in 7 days, and every access is logged. |

A release line has no name column, so its asset uid is its name. The caller chooses that uid, so the HTML escapes it,
the manifest uid, the greeting and the link. The subject goes through `buildRawEmail`'s header sanitizing, as every
subject does.

The email carries ids only, never a component, a source or a locator. It claims nothing the platform does not do:
signing records a signature (or a decline) on the envelope, and nothing in WorkGraph reads that signature yet.

Like the agreement email, it is transactional. It honours the suppression list and has no unsubscribe line. It has no
"reply to this email" line, because it comes from `noreply@`.
