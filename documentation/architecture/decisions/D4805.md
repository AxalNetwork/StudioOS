## D4805 — A signature proves one terms revision; program lifecycle actions commit whole (#4805)

- **Status:** built by S27, 2026-11-07 (#4805, Programs audit 2/7).
- **Number:** D4805 is this issue's number (D4106). Migration: `202611071200_program_grant_revisions.sql`.
- **The invariants (from the issue):**
  1. A signature proves a specific immutable terms revision, not merely that some schedule was once signed.
  2. A lifecycle action either commits a consistent local state or leaves its prior state intact.
  3. Remote propagation retries independently of the business transition.

### 1. What was wrong

- **Grant terms.** `PATCH /licences/:uid/:program` rewrote the terms and left `schedule_contract_uid` and `schedule_signed_at` in place. The schedule the studio had signed for the old terms therefore made the new terms read "signed" and "in force". A schedule envelope could also complete after the terms had moved on, and the completion marked the grant signed whatever it had shown.
- **Grant lifecycle.** Pause, resume, end, terms change and grant each wrote the state, then wrote the history line as a second statement, then pushed to the branch inline. A failure between them left a changed state with no history; a Worker that died before the push left the branch on an old copy with nothing to retry it.
- **Influencer lifecycle.** Admitting committed the decision, then a batch for the membership, partner row and draft terms, then a second batch for the Investor Pro grant and the history row. A failure in the third step left an active member with no entitlement and no history, and the compensating write that reopened the application did not undo the membership. A pause or end committed the membership, then took back the entitlement in a second batch. A handle takedown deleted and inserted handle history before its guarded `UPDATE` and kept them when the `UPDATE` lost.
- **Notices.** The route sent the member's notice after the commit; a failure was logged and the notice was gone.

### 2. The terms revision

- `program_grants.terms_revision` starts at 1 and goes up by one with every accepted change to the terms or the note (the two things the schedule prints), and with a re-grant. Every write names the revision it read (`... AND terms_revision = ?`), so a stale change loses.
- `termsHashOf` is the SHA-256 of the program, every term in catalogue order and the note.
- `program_grant_schedules` has one row per schedule envelope: the revision and hash it was built for, when it was sent, when it was signed, and when a newer revision replaced it. Rows are never deleted and a signature on one is never cleared.
- `program_grants.signed_revision` is the newest revision whose schedule was signed. It is never cleared, so a grant whose terms moved on reads `amendment_needed`, not "never signed".

### 3. What signs what

- **The schedule prints** "Revision N · terms fingerprint …" (`scheduleDocument`), and the send stores both on a schedule row **in the same batch** that names the envelope on the grant. That UPDATE requires `terms_revision` to be the revision it rendered from. If the terms moved while the envelope was being sent, nothing is recorded (409 `terms_changed`), and the envelope that went out names no schedule row, so it signs nothing.
- **A completion** (`onProgramScheduleEnvelopeCompleted`, from the in-house route and the DocuSign poller) finds the schedule row by envelope.
  - **Current:** the row's revision is the grant's and its hash still matches (a `legacy` row, from before this migration, is checked by revision only). The row is marked signed and the grant is marked signed, in one batch with its history line and the owed branch push.
  - **Stale:** the row is marked signed, because the studio did sign it. `signed_revision` is raised, a `schedule_signed_stale` history line says so, and the grant stays unsigned and waiting on an amendment. Nothing else changes.
- **A change of terms** clears the grant's `schedule_contract_uid` and `schedule_signed_at` (the grant is not signed at its new revision), supersedes the earlier schedule rows (their signatures stay), and **voids the superseded unsigned envelopes in e-sign** until the first signature lands, so the studio cannot sign the earlier terms and the replacement can be sent. An envelope already being signed is left alone; if it completes later it is the stale case above.
- **Presentation.** `schedule.status` can now be `amendment_needed`, and `schedule` carries `revision` and `signed_revision`. The Programs grid, the studio's terms card and the history say "Terms changed · new signature needed", and the action reads "Send amended schedule".

### 4. One batch per decision (`services/decisionBatch.ts`)

A batch is one transaction, but a `UPDATE ... WHERE state = ?` that loses is not an error, so the statements after it would run for a decision that did not happen. `decision_guard` is an empty table with `CHECK (decision_won = 1)`. A decision batch is:
1. the deciding `UPDATE`, guarded on the state and revision it read;
2. `INSERT INTO decision_guard ... changes() = 1`, which fails the CHECK, and so rolls everything back, when the `UPDATE` lost;
3. everything that depends on it, unconditionally;
4. `DELETE FROM decision_guard`.

`runDecision` returns `{ lost: true }` when the guard is what failed and rethrows any other failure. The routes then answer with the same sentences as before for a lost decision; a storage failure goes through `mapError`, which logs the text and answers a sentence.

Used by:
- **Grants:** grant, re-grant, draft grant, terms change, pause, resume, end and the schedule send. Each is the state, its history line, the superseded schedule rows, any envelope void and the owed branch push.
- **Influencers:** the application's decision (admit, decline, more), and every membership change (tier, pause, resume, end, handle takedown).
  - Admitting is the application, the partner row, the membership, the draft terms and trigger, the Investor Pro grant, the history row (naming the membership it created) and the notice.
  - A change is the membership `UPDATE`, which names the tier, status and handle it read; the handle's history; the grant or take-back; the history rows; and the notice. The compensating write that reopened an application is gone, because there is nothing to compensate.
  - `retireHandleStatements` replaces `takedownStatements`: the clear is the deciding `UPDATE`, and the history rows follow the guard.

### 5. Durable delivery

- **Branch push.** `queuePushStatement` writes the licence's `licence_push_pending` row (D272) in the same batch as the change, dated so the next retry picks it up. The inline push after the commit still runs and clears the row when it lands. A licence with no deployment writes nothing: its branch pulls on its first read. So the push is owed from the commit, even when the Worker dies, or the inline push cannot start, before it runs. `retryPendingLicencePushes` sends the licence as it is then, and repeats nothing.
- **Notices.** `noticeStatement` writes a `program_outbox` row in the decision's batch. `deliverOutbox` sends it after the commit and marks it delivered; the ten-minute cron (`retryOutbox`) sends the ones that did not land, at most five times each. `notify` answers null both when it fails and when the member switched every channel off, so null counts as undelivered and is retried (a suppressed notice is tried five times and sends nothing). A retry only sends: it does not touch the decision.
- The route no longer sends the influencer notices itself.

### 6. What this does not change

- **The program proposals, Perks and playbook publish** are #4806.
- **`admin_audit_log` lines** (`logAdminAction`) are still written by the routes after the commit. The history this issue requires, `licence_events` and `influencer_events`, is in the batch.
- **A branch push or a notice that never lands** is retried at the cron's cadence, not made atomic with the decision, which is the issue's third invariant.
