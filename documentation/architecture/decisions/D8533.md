## D8533 — Queue consumers end in one outcome under redelivery, and a cron tick records each task's outcome (#8533)

- **Status:** built 2026-11-25 (#8533, slot S35). Not deployed. One migration (`202611250106_8533_cron_task_outcomes.sql`, an added nullable column).
- **Builds on:** `src/queue-consumer.ts` (the `job_idempotency` claim), D201 and D4703 (`cron_run_history`, `partial`), `POST /api/infra/dlq/:id/retry`, D6686 (formation cases), the email pipeline (`services/email/send.ts`).
- **Tracker:** #8509 row F3. Source: the platform CI audit of 2026-11-25 (P1, queues, cron and background workflows).

### What was found

Queue delivery is at-least-once, and no test delivered an application message twice or stopped a consumer after its first durable write. The harness built here (`cloudflare-worker/test/_queue_scenarios.ts`) does both, for one message, and asserts one business outcome and a visible status after each of four scenarios: duplicate, interrupted, exhausted, replay. Run against payment fulfillment and notifications, it failed three ways:

1. **A delivery that died after its claim lost the job.** The consumer claims `job_idempotency` before running the job. If the isolate dies after the claim (and before the job finishes, or before a failure releases the claim), the redelivery finds the claim and ack-skips the message as a duplicate. A paid incorporation stayed at `paid` and an email stayed `queued`, and nothing recorded it.
2. **An email could go out twice** once (1) is fixed: a delivery that died after marking its log row `sent` left the claim without a result, and the next run sent again.
3. **A dead-lettered email read `failed`**, the same as one still being retried.

### What was decided

- **A claim has a lease.** `CLAIM_LEASE_SECONDS` = 16 minutes: a consumer invocation lives at most 15 (Cloudflare's limit) and is dispatched up to a minute late. A claim with a result is a duplicate (ack-skip, as before). A claim with no result younger than the lease is `in_flight`: the message is retried after the lease (metered `deferred`). Older than the lease, one conditional `UPDATE` takes it over and the job runs. The cost: a genuine duplicate of a running job comes back once after 16 minutes, spending one of its retries, instead of being acked at once; and a job whose best-effort result write failed can run again after the lease, which is why the next point exists.
- **The business state is the last guard.** A formation case not at `paid` does not move (already so, D6686). `deliverNow` now returns without sending when the email's log row reads `sent`. What remains: a crash between the provider accepting an email and the `sent` mark landing still sends twice; no durable write sits between them, so this is an external effect the queue cannot make atomic.
- **A dead-lettered email reads `dlq`.** `dlqConsumer` marks the log row (`markEmailDeadLettered`), leaving a `sent` row alone; a replay through the admin route sends it and marks it `sent`.
- **The harness is shared.** #8516 (signature-stage advancement) and #8525 (project moves) reuse it: a spec gives the queue, consumers, `maxRetries`, a world built by the real producer, the outcome count, the visible status, the interruption points and the recovery path. Each interruption point is its own test; the default is the consumer's first write of any kind.

### Per-task cron results

`cron_run_history` recorded the trigger, and since D4703 the names of tasks whose catches fired. A `completed` row could hold a task that ran and did nothing. The existing columns are text a person reads (`summary`, `error`), so a JSON column was added rather than packed into them: `task_outcomes`, nullable, no default (the Worker one version back writes rows without it; the INSERT names the column only when the tick reported a task).

- Each entry is `{task, outcome, error_class?, note?}`. `outcome` is `done`, `skipped` or `failed`. `error_class` is the error's `name`, else a code the call site passed (`calendar_copy_retry_failed`), else `unclassified` (free text or nothing). At most 100 entries, notes cut to 200 characters.
- `taskFailed` records every failure with its class. A failed entry makes the row `partial` even when the caller did not also list it in `failedTasks`.
- `done` and `skipped` are reported by the queue drain, the subprocessor notice sweep (whose catch now passes its error) and the analytics snapshot. The other tasks appear only when they fail; each joins with one `cronTasks.done(...)` / `cronTasks.skipped(...)` at its site.
- Status semantics are unchanged: a skipped task does not make a row `partial`; HQ still reads `completed` as `ok`.
