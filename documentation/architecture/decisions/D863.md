## D863 — Telegram for Record a Session: a guided setup read, six steps from stored rows

- **Status:** built by S07 for #2509, part of #2508.
- **Basis:** `origin/main` at `3704a16a`. Worker paths are relative to `cloudflare-worker/src/`.
- **Stacks on:** D684 (the channels, linking and threads), D756 (the Telegram Business channel, its consent and its rollout), D757 §2.11 (only a member who can hold a venture is offered a channel), D527 (one API module per domain).
- **Migration:** none.

### 1. The decision

A founder should be able to connect Telegram to Record a Session without reading `documentation/operations/TELEGRAM.md`. So Axal shows the setup as six steps, each with its live state and, when it is not done, one sentence in our words of what to do in Telegram.

**Every state comes from a row Axal already keeps.** The read never calls the Bot API. The rows were written by the link routes and by Telegram's own `business_connection` and `business_message` updates, so they are as live as Telegram has told us.

### 2. The steps

`services/channels/telegramSetup.ts` builds them. Each step is `done`, `todo` (the member's move) or `blocked` (an earlier step comes first), with a code and an `action` sentence. A done step's action is null.

| # | Key | Done when | Not done |
| - | --- | --------- | -------- |
| 1 | `linked` | the member's `integrations` row for `telegram` is active | `todo`, with `not_linked`, or, from the newest link code, `link_waiting_telegram` (send /confirm) or `link_waiting_axal` (confirm in Axal) |
| 2 | `bot_added` | a `telegram_business_connections` row is the member's | `todo bot_not_added`; `blocked needs_link` before step 1 |
| 3 | `bot_enabled` | that connection's `is_enabled` (`telegramBusinessState`, the state replies and the voice job read) | `todo bot_switched_off`; otherwise blocked |
| 4 | `replies_allowed` | its `can_reply`, from the Bot API 9.0 `rights` | `todo no_reply_right`; otherwise blocked |
| 5 | `first_chat` | a pending or assigned thread on the active link | `todo no_chat_yet` while the bot is on; otherwise blocked |
| 6 | `consent_notice` | the D756 §1.7 notice can go out: Axal sends it, or gives the same words to paste. Both need the notice page's link (`consentNoticeUrl`) | `blocked notice_link_missing`: Axal's to fix, so the sentence says nothing is needed from the member |

Some cases need a ruling, and these are the choices made:
- **A chat that arrived counts** even if the bot was switched off since. Step 5 records a fact, and `next` still points at step 3.
- **An ignored chat does not count.**
- **A disconnect** sets the link to `disconnected` and detaches every business connection (`routes/channels.ts`), so the member is back at step 1.
- **The read keys everything to the active link.** A business connection still carrying the member once the link has ended is not read.

The answer is:

```text
{ account: "@username" | null,
  prerequisites: [{ key: "telegram_premium", required: true, state: null, line }],
  steps: [{ step, key, state, code, action }],
  next: <first step not done> | null,
  ready: <all six done> }
```

### 3. The Premium line

The connector page says "Needs Telegram Premium: chatbots are part of Telegram Business." The setup carries the same line as a prerequisite (`TELEGRAM_PREMIUM_REQUIRED`, `TELEGRAM_PREMIUM_LINE`).

Its `state` is null because Axal cannot see a member's subscription: unknown is NULL. When the owner reports the Premium check (TELEGRAM.md §1 step 5, on #1008), those two constants change, together with the card's line.

### 4. The route and its gate

`GET /api/channels/telegram/setup` (client `channelsApi.telegramSetup`) is signed in and wrapped like `POST /telegram/link`:
- `venture()`: a member who cannot hold a venture gets the same 404 `unknown_provider`;
- then `providerAvailable` and `channelOpenTo`: outside the canary, with the rollout off, or with the secrets unset, the same `503 not_available`;
- then the active-member check.

It is read-only. Linking, the webhook and consent are unchanged.

### 5. Privacy

The response carries codes, our sentences and at most the member's own `@username`. That name is already shown by `GET /api/channels`, and is repeated only when it is a `@username`, never a display name.

It never carries:
- a contact's name or a chat id;
- a business connection id or the member's Telegram id;
- the bot token.

### 6. Verified

`cloudflare-worker/test/telegram_setup_d863.test.ts` (13 tests, on the channels fixture; Telegram's API mocked and every call counted) covers:
- each of the six states for a member at that step;
- the link in progress, waiting on Telegram and then on Axal;
- a disabled bot and missing reply rights;
- a chat kept after the bot is switched off, and an ignored chat;
- the notice blocked with steps 1 to 5 done;
- a disconnect, and a stale connection, both back at step 1;
- the username rule;
- the canary, rollout off, unset secrets, and a member who cannot hold a venture, each refused exactly as `POST /telegram/link` is;
- signed out;
- no Telegram call on any read, and no contact name, chat id, connection id or Telegram id in the response.

18 mutations of the service and the route were all caught.
