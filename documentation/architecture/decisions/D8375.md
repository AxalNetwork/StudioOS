## D8375 — Slow settled capacity history and share the alarm API budget (#8375)

An alarm still arms recovery at 15 seconds before attempting work. Coordination and publication share one lazily acquired installation client, including its call and time budget. A settled failure that needs no remote observation uses zero tokens. A failed acquisition stays failed for the rest of that turn. Authenticated submissions and outcomes continue to arm a one-second wake before their durable writes.

Active attempts, unapplied negatives, due corrections, initial backfill and publication retries keep the urgent alarm. On a successful settled turn, the next visit is in 60 seconds, or 30 seconds if the compact history page is full. An empty authority with retained garbage or orphan evidence gets a 60-second maintenance visit. A clean empty authority disarms. Parent eligibility is still observed for each selected pass. Cursor rotation and the four-publication limit still apply. The delay is per alarm. A given check can wait an entire rotation, and provider errors can extend that wait. At 32,768 same-mode compact records and two compact selections per settled alarm, one sweep takes about 5.7 days at 30 seconds per full page. This operating latency needs explicit review before enablement.

Alarm expiry scans run at most once a minute in a warm instance; restart or a backward clock jump forces a fresh sweep. Admission retains its separate immediate prune. The failed index supplies the compact view directly to discovery. Delivery re-reads authoritative state before acting on a hint.

Counters cover alarm work after seeding. The locked workerd 2026-11-13 binary ran with the configured 2026-11-11 compatibility date. Its sole outbound service was a local GitHub fixture with HTTP counters.

Production-scale SQL cost remains material. Before enabling, confirm the production CPU and billing budgets and permitted worst-case monitoring latency with the operator. This local measurement supplies no production budget or activation approval.
