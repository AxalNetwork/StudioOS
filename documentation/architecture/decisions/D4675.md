## D4675 — HQ's durable acts target what was reviewed, record once, and say what is on record (#4675)

- **Status:** Part 1 built, 2026-11-07. Part 2 built, 2026-11-10: the deadline policy the owner decided on #4675 (Q1 = B, Q2 = B, consistent with the legal reviewer's pick), §6.
- **Scope:** `/admin/security` (HQ Security audit 2 of 6): the data-subject request (DSR) lifecycle, and the Trust-center Publish the page links to. Sessions, sign-out and emergency actions are #4669; security-event identity and backup evidence are #4691.
- **Builds on:** D168 (the DSR ledger, migration 272), D169 (the subject's own outcome), D1075 (the trust-center content store), D4703 (`adminAuditStatement`, an audit row in the same batch as its change).
- **Migrations:** `202611072219_dsr_close_key.sql` (`dsr_requests.close_key`), `202611072220_trust_publish_key.sql` (`trust_content_changes.request_key`). Both are additive, nullable and uniquely indexed where set.

### 1. An act names what was reviewed, and a stale review changes nothing

- **DSR close.** HQ's list now gives each open request its ledger id (`request_id`) and its deadline (`deadline_at`). The close sends the receipt stamp and id it showed. The close applies only while that request is still the open one. If the subject withdrew and asked again, or someone else closed it first, nothing is written and the answer says which (`stale_request`, `no_open_request` with the outcome on record).
- **Publish.** The editor's read carries a review token over everything a Publish would change. A Publish whose token is not current publishes nothing (`changed_since_review`). The batch re-checks the same set as its first statement, so a save landing between the check and the batch also publishes nothing, never part of it.

### 2. The decision and its audit evidence are one unit

- The DSR close writes the outcome, the subject's notice, the `admin_audit_log` row and the actor's `activity_logs` row in one batch. Every row after the outcome is conditioned on it, so an act that lost a race leaves no notice and no audit row.
- The Publish writes both audit rows in its own batch.
- When an audit row cannot be written, the act does not happen.

### 3. A retry is the same act

- Each dialog makes one key when it opens (`frontend/src/lib/opKey.js`) and resends it on every retry. A new outcome is a new key.
- A retry whose key is on record is answered with that record (`replayed: true`). Before this, it read as a refusal. A key used for a different act is refused (`idempotency_key_conflict`, `key_conflict`).
- The subject-notice sweep after a Publish claims each delivery once, so it also runs on a replay and finishes what an interrupted first attempt left.

### 4. Uncertain is not failed

- An answer that never came, a 5xx, or a write whose result could not be read back (`202 outcome_unconfirmed`) is shown as **not confirmed**, with Try again. Try again is safe because the key is kept.
- A 4xx refusal is certain, and says nothing was recorded.
- A Publish that adds a subprocessor reports its notices as `queued`, never as sent.

### 5. The subject's request and cancel move together

- The open flag and the ledger row are written in one batch. If the ledger cannot be written (no migration 272), the flag alone moves, so a member can always ask and cancel.
- A cancel that finds nothing open (HQ closed it first) records nothing as cancelled.
- Before this change, a cancel whose ledger write failed could leave an open ledger row with no open request behind it. Such a row is now recorded as the withdrawal it was, on the next request or close.

### 6. The deadline rule (part 2: the owner's decision)

Part 1 stated and pinned the rule the code had always run, 30 spans of 24 hours, and asked whether it was right. The owner answered on 2026-11-10: Q1 = B and Q2 = B.

**The rule** (`services/dsrRequests.ts`, `dsrDeadlineMs`):

- The clock starts at receipt (`users.deletion_requested_at`, stored in UTC, read through `dsrStampMs`).
- It runs **one calendar month**: to the same day of the next month, or to that month's last day when it has no such day. Received 31 January, it is due 28 February (29 in a leap year); received 31 March, 30 April.
- It ends at the **end of that UTC day**, so a due date is a whole day.
- It is never later than a calendar month, and nothing pauses or extends it. Option C (a recorded extension, and a pause while identity is verified) was not chosen.

**Overdue** (Q2 = B): a request is overdue from the instant after its due day ends. `days_left` counts whole UTC days to the due day: it reads 0 on the due day (the pages say "due today") and -1 from the next instant. Under part 1 it read 0 for the whole day after the deadline had passed. Because the deadline is the end of a UTC day, `days_left < 0` and the new `overdue` field are the same fact at every instant; a test sweeps two months to hold them together.

**What the payload says**: `clock: { rule: 'calendar_month', label }` replaces `clock_days: 30`. A calendar month has no fixed day count, so none is sent, and every page that printed "30-day clock" or "received + 30 days" now prints the named rule. Each open row gains `overdue`.

**The two rules differ in both directions.** A calendar month can be shorter than 30 days (31 January → 28 February) or longer (1 March → the end of 1 April). The tests pin the month ends, both kinds of February, the year end, and a sweep of every day across two years: never later than the same date next month, never under 28 days, always the end of a UTC day, always in the next month.

**Not changed here, and not mine to change.** The Terms of Service template (`templates/legal/tos_v2.md`) still says deletion requests are processed "within 30 days". Under the new rule some requests are due on day 31. Legal text is counsel's, so this is raised on #4675 rather than edited.
