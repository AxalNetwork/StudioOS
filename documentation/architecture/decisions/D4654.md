## D4654 — One authoritative state for each mailbox (#4654)

#4654 (Email audit 2/6, finding 3, slot S16, taken at the owner's request). The public scope is the guarantee below; the audit's evidence lives in the private advisory and is not repeated here.

### The guarantee

- **A member's explicit disconnect stays in force.** A provider operation (a verify, a send, a thread read) that was already in flight when the member disconnected may still finish, and a send that did go is still logged as sent, but it writes no credentials and no status to the mailbox.
- **A newer connection stays authoritative.** Neither a late provider completion, success or failure, nor a connection or a disconnect that began earlier, replaces it.
- **Only an explicitly started connection reactivates a disconnected mailbox.** A connection started before the disconnect does not.
- **A member has one identity per provider and normalized address**, however connects overlap.
- **The replies say what is persisted**: a disconnect answers with the row as stored; a connection that lost answers 409 `connection_superseded` (the OAuth callback redirects with `?mail=superseded`); a disconnect that lost to a newer connection answers 409 `connection_changed`.

### How

Migration `202611071555_mail_account_lifecycle.sql` adds `mail_accounts.lifecycle_rev` (moved by every connect, reconnect and disconnect), `connect_started_at` (when the connection now on the row was STARTED), `disconnected_at`, and `mail_nonces.issued_at` (the start time an OAuth state carries to its callback).

`services/mail/accounts.ts`:
- `markUsed`, `markExpired`, `markError` and `forget` write only while the row is the one the caller read (same `lifecycle_rev`, not revoked) and return whether they did;
- `saveAccount` takes the time the connection started, creates the row with `INSERT OR IGNORE` and treats losing that race as an update, and applies the update only if the connection started at or after the one on the row and after the last disconnect; otherwise it throws `ConnectionSuperseded`.

The same contract covers verify and send (`routes/mail_accounts.ts`) and the inbox thread read (`services/growInbox.ts`, through the revision of the row it read).

### Existing duplicates

The migration merges them before it adds the unique index on `(user_id, provider, lower(address))`. The survivor is the active row with credentials, else the newest row that is not revoked, else the newest. The others' `mail_sent` rows move to the survivor, so sent history and the recipient counts are kept, and the others are removed with their sealed credentials. A migration cannot revoke a token at the provider: a member who connected the same mailbox twice may remove the app's access in the provider's own settings.

### Tests

`cloudflare-worker/test/mail_account_lifecycle_4654.test.ts` holds each ordering: a verify, a send and a thread read completing after a disconnect or a reconnect (success and failure); connections started out of order and across a disconnect; a disconnect that began before a newer connection; concurrent connects ending in one row; the OAuth state's start time; and the migration's merge. `frontend/test/email_lifecycle_4654.test.mjs` holds the `superseded` outcome. Each assertion was seen failing under a mutation.

### What this does not do

It does not change what a send or a verify does at the provider, the nonce, the rate limit, or how credentials are sealed. It does not coordinate delivery reconciliation with Email audit package 1 (#4665): when that lands, its send completion must write through the same guarded functions.
