### 1. Whose files count (owner question 3, A): this changes D642

D642 counted each data room file to its uploader. #2449's owner question 3 takes A, as D1166 §5 records: your own files count to you, and company files count to the company owner's plan.

**A company file** is a row tied to a venture (`project_id`). It counts to one member:
- the company's first primary admin (`user_company_links.is_primary_admin = 1`, earliest link first), when the venture has a company (`projects.company_id`) and that company has a primary admin;
- otherwise, the venture's founder (`projects.founder_id` → `users.founder_id`), as migration 189 bridges the two id spaces.

Exactly one member owns a venture, so no file counts to two plans. A cofounder's venture inside someone else's company counts to the company owner. A file another member uploaded into your data room counts to you. A file you uploaded into another member's room counts to them.

**A personal file** counts to its own owner column:
- research library (`owner_user_id`);
- message attachments (`uploader_user_id`);
- diligence uploads (`uploaded_by_user_id`). These stay personal because an investor's upload to a diligence case is their own file, not the venture's.
- deck reviews (`user_id`). A deck review's `project_id` comes from the uploader's form unchecked (`deck_reviewer.ts`). So it never moves the file to another member's plan: it only names the venture when that venture is the caller's own.
- profile photos and logos (`member_files.owner_user_id`);
- CSV imports (`data_imports.user_id`).

### 3. The new fields

D642's contract stays: `total_bytes`, `files`, `unknown_size_files` and `areas`, each area with `recorded`. The read adds:

- `measured_bytes`: the sum of recorded sizes, which is a floor when some sizes are unknown. It is `null` when an area is unreadable.
- `plan_key`, `allowance_bytes`, `allowance_reason` and `plan_recorded`, from part 1's `storagePlanKey` and `storagePlan`. No figure is held here.
  - A plan that cannot be read gives `null` with the fixed words "Storage plan unreadable" and blanks nothing else.
  - A negotiated plan has a null allowance and the catalogue's reason.
- `state`:
  - `ok` below 80%;
  - `near_80` from 80%;
  - `near_95` from 95%;
  - `full` at exactly the allowance;
  - `over` above it, after a plan change or two uploads at once;
  - `no_allowance` for staff.

  It is computed from `measured_bytes`, so with files whose size is unknown it is a floor, and `unknown_size_files` says how many. When an area is unreadable, or the allowance is negotiated or unread, `state` is `null`. Part 4's guard and notices read this field. Nothing here pauses an upload, deletes a file or hides one.
- `kinds[]`: the canvas's six kinds in palette order (`data_room`, `documents`, `recordings`, `decks_exports`, `brand`, `other`), each with files, bytes, measured bytes, unknown sizes and `recorded`.
- `companies[]`: each of the caller's companies with its ventures, ordered by size. Then one row with `personal: true` for files tied to no company: personal files, plus the ventures not yet in a company.
  - When an area is unreadable, every company keeps its name, but its figures are `null`.
  - When the ventures themselves cannot be read, `companies` is `null`.
- `largest[]` and `largest_complete`: the caller's ten largest files with a recorded size, across every area. Each has:
  - its area and kind;
  - its name, and the version number for an earlier data room version;
  - its bytes and the date it was added;
  - its venture and company;
  - a `ref` that the page uses to open where the file lives. The ref is the row's uid where it has one, and its id otherwise.

  A profile photo or logo has no name. `brand` says which it is (`headshot`, `brand_logo`). A largest-files query that fails leaves its area's figures standing and sets `largest_complete: false`.

**Failures.** One unreadable area blanks only itself and its kind. There is then no total, no company figure and no state. Every company area depends on the ventures read, so if that read fails, those areas are unreadable and personal areas still count.
