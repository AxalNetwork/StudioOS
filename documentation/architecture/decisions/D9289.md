## D9289 — Eadwyn panel: tab keys, a named composer, announced lines, the shared fullscreen focus shell, and room to type on a phone (#9289)

- **Status:** S21, 2026-11-28 (#9289; the supplement's A9, A10 and §11.1 "Accessibility and focus", live findings L5 and L6, on #9271). No migration.
- **Scope:** the panel's tab strip in `frontend/src/components/advisor/EadwynPanels.jsx`; the composer, transcript, current-question line and fullscreen view in `PersonalAdvisor.jsx`; the new `frontend/src/lib/eadwynChatA11y.js`; the shell's focus behaviour in `ui/Lightbox.jsx`, now also exported as a hook.
- **Not claimed:** conformance with WCAG or any other standard. This decision covers the behaviours below, each pinned by a test, and nothing beyond them.

### 1. The tab strip follows the WAI-ARIA tab pattern, from data

`PanelTabs` takes `tabs` (default `PANEL_TABS`) and every rule reads that list. A new tab, such as #9282's Teams, is one more entry and needs no keyboard code.

- **Keys.** Left and Right move to the previous and next tab and wrap at both ends. Home and End go to the first and last tab (`panelTabAfterKey`). Moving selects the tab (automatic activation), because every panel is a cheap read already on the page. Focus follows the selection. Any other key is left alone.
- **Roving `tabindex`.** Only the selected tab is `tabindex="0"`. Tab from the strip goes to the selected panel, not to the next tab.
- **Ids.** Each tab is `<base>-tab-<id>`. The panel is `<base>-panel-<id>`, carries `role="tabpanel"`, names its tab with `aria-labelledby`, and is a Tab stop (`tabPanelProps`). `base` comes from `useId`, so two chats on one page never share an id.
- **`aria-controls` is on the selected tab only.** Only the selected panel is drawn. An `aria-controls` pointing at an id that is not on the page gives a screen reader nothing to go to. Drawing empty hidden panels for every tab would need three layouts that place the Conversation panel differently, which this task leaves alone.
- Three views draw the strip: the card below 1024px, the desktop rail and the fullscreen rail. Each draws its selected panel with the same ids.

### 2. The composer has a name and room to type

- A visually hidden `<label>`, "Message Eadwyn", names the textarea. The placeholder stays as a hint. A placeholder is not a name, and it disappears once the member types.
- The field grows with its text (`useAutoGrow`). It is measured at `height: auto` so it shrinks again, plus its border. It stops at 160px (`max-h-40`, about seven lines) and scrolls after that.
- Below 640px it is at least two lines tall (`max-sm:min-h-[58px]`: two 20px lines, 16px of padding and a 2px border). The placeholder therefore fits, and two lines stay visible when the keyboard takes most of the window. The fullscreen view's height already follows the visual viewport (`useVisualViewportStyle`), and the transcript, not the composer, is what shrinks.
- The active question ("Currently: …") wraps to two lines (`line-clamp-2`) instead of being cut to one. Its full text is in `title` and is the transcript's last line.

### 3. The transcript is a log; Eadwyn's new lines are announced politely

- The scroller is `role="log"`, named "Conversation with Eadwyn", with `aria-live="off"`.
- A visually hidden `aria-live="polite"` region beside it reads the newest line Eadwyn added (`newAssistantLine`). Making the log itself live would also read back the member's own line, and every token of a streamed explanation as it arrives.
- **Not announced:** the member's own line, a tool card (not prose), and a transcript filled with several lines at once from empty. That last case is the history read on load or the card ⇄ fullscreen swap, not something Eadwyn just said.
- **Reading older lines is not interrupted.** The parent used to scroll to the foot on every change. Now the transcript follows a change only if the reader was within 48px of the foot, or if the change is a line they just sent (`shouldFollow`). Its `onScroll` records which of the two applies. A freshly mounted transcript starts at the foot.

### 4. Fullscreen uses the shell's focus trap and gives focus back

- The modal effect and Tab handler of `ui/Lightbox.jsx` are now `useLightboxFocus` in the same file, and `Lightbox` calls it, so the two cannot drift apart. The hook does what the shell did: lock the page's scroll, make the rest of the page inert and `aria-hidden`, join the shared Escape stack, move focus in, keep Tab and Shift+Tab inside, and on close undo all of it and refocus the opener.
- The fullscreen view is no longer a bare `role="dialog"` with `useEscapeClose`. It is that hook on its own frame (`tabIndex={-1}`, `onKeyDown={onTab}`).
- **Return focus.** The fullscreen view replaces the card, so the button that opened it has left the page by the time the view closes. The shell skips an opener that is no longer connected. `useFocusOnReturn` then focuses the card's new fullscreen button when the view mode returns to `normal`. A first mount does not take focus.
