### 1. Never automatic, in code

**Publishing is a capability of its own.** `ProviderImpl` gains:
- `listDestinations`;
- `publishPreview`;
- `publish`;
- optional `retract` and `findPublished`.

**It is never `push`,** so neither `autopush.ts` nor Eadwyn's send targets can reach it. `services/publishCore.ts` is the only caller of `publish`, and only inside a confirm or a Retry the founder pressed. A test walks `src/` to prove it.

**`CONNECTOR_METADATA.publishes`** lists `{ what, target }` with no `default_mode`, so `send_modes` refuses a publish target, and `automaticSendAllowed` is false for every publisher.

**`PUBLISHERS`** sits beside `MONEY_MOVERS`, read from `PUBLISH_TARGETS`. A publisher's trust notice adds "It posts only when you press Send."

### 2. The tables (migration 642)

- **`publish_destinations`:** the places an adapter lists, each switched on for one company. **Off by default.** `daily_cap` is the founder's own lower cap, never above `PUBLISH_DAILY_CAP` (10, #2400 decision D). Disconnect deletes these rows, by cascade.
- **`publish_drafts`:** what the founder writes for one venture and one of the nine places (`PUBLISH_MODULES`). It holds:
  - the shared text and per-destination edits;
  - the quoted records;
  - media keys and alt text;
  - the team-only tick;
  - the members who agreed to be named.
- **`publish_sends`:** one per destination per confirm, `UNIQUE(draft_id, destination_id, idempotency_key)`. It keeps:
  - the envelope (exactly what the preview showed and `publish` sends) and its hash;
  - the state;
  - who confirmed, and when;
  - the provider's post id and link;
  - the error code and `retry_after`;
  - the retract fields.

  `destination_id` is SET NULL when the place goes, so the record stays the company's history. The cap index is `(destination_id, status, sent_at)`.

### 3. Who may (`canPublish`)

These are checked in order, each with its own refusal:
1. not a support session (`support_session`);
2. never an admin, partner, advisor or investor, and only the venture's founder (`isVentureFounder`, #2400 decision A, (a)). A co-founder is refused, even through their own connection (`publish_not_allowed`);
3. the connection is the member's own (`publish_not_allowed`);
4. it is switched on for the venture's company (`company_not_enabled`);
5. the destination is this connection's, this company's, and on (`destination_off`).

The history reads for the venture's team (`isTeamViewer`); anyone else gets 404.

### 4. The check (`publishLint`)

**It runs on each destination's final text and its alt text:**
- **`lintForSend(…, 'public')`:** emails, phones, tax ids, IBANs, card-like numbers, and any member named by email or full legal name.
- **A secret scan:** key prefixes (Stripe, GitHub, GitLab, Slack, AWS, Google), private-key blocks, JWT shapes, URLs with a password, and long mixed tokens with high entropy.
- **A team-only check:** the draft's team-only figures, unless ticked in.

**Every finding blocks (#2400 E, (a)). There is no override.**
- **A named member** passes only once the founder ticks "They agreed to be named".
- **A secret** is masked in the finding.
- **Logs and send rows** keep kinds only.

**The name scan's fix.** `telegramRedactCheck.ts` already read `full_legal_name` on `main`, but it loaded every named user on each call. The match now runs in SQL against the bound, lower-cased body. It loads only the members named (at most 50), and only their id, name and consent.

### 5. The send's state machine

**Confirm** (`POST /drafts/:uid/confirm`, with `{ destination_uid, envelope_hash, idempotency_key }`):
1. **A key already used returns that row** and sends nothing, even if the draft changed since.
2. **Otherwise every check runs again:** `canPublish` with the destination, a fresh preview, and the lint. Then the hash must be the one the founder saw, else `content_changed`.
3. **The row is inserted `ready`,** then claimed `ready` → `sending` in ONE conditional UPDATE that also counts the cap: the destination's sends today plus those `sending`, under its cap. A claim that changes nothing has one of two causes:
   - another press holds the row, so it is answered as it is;
   - the cap is reached: `daily_cap_reached`, and the row stays `ready` with that code.
4. **`publish` runs once.**

**Outcomes:**
- **The provider accepts:** the row is `sent`, with the post id and link.
- **`PublishError('not_created')`** (a 4xx): `failed` with the code. A 429 reads `rate_limited`, with `retry_after`.
- **Anything else** (a timeout or reset after the request left, a 5xx, a throw after the provider said yes): `unknown`.
- **If the record itself fails after a yes,** the row stays `sending` and becomes `unknown` when stranded, never `failed`. Failed could send twice: the double-post window #2400 found in HQ's X flow.

**After a send:**
- **Retry** is pressed by the founder, on the same row, re-checked, with the same content: a `failed` send, or a `ready` one the cap refused. It is never `unknown`, which answers `outcome_unknown`. Nothing retries on its own, not even a 429.
- **Check** asks the adapter's `findPublished`. Found is `sent`; not found stays `unknown`.
- **Mark as not sent** is the founder's word on an `unknown` row (the dialog that warns it may post twice is part 3's). The row becomes `failed`, `marked_not_sent`.
- **A row still `sending` 10 minutes after its claim** becomes `unknown`. That happens on every read of the history and every fifth minute in the cron, on every deployment. It is never re-sent.
- **Retract** works only where the provider deletes (`retract: 'delete'` and an adapter `retract`): `retracted`, with who, when and why. A refused delete stays `sent`, with `retract_error`. Where it cannot, the answer is `not_retractable`, and **Mark as removed** records `removed_outside`.

**Logs:**
- `integration_logs`: `direction 'outbound'`, `event_type 'publish'`, with codes and the post id only.
- `activity_logs`: each switch, confirm, send, failure, retract and mark.

No post body, token or figure is logged.
