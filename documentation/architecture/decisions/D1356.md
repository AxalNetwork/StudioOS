### 1. Three things kept apart

**Identity.** A person is matched only by an exact LinkedIn profile URL (`url_key`) or an exact email (`email_key`), in D1351's normal forms.
- **A name alone never matches.** A person query that is neither a URL nor an email is 400 `person_unmatchable`.
- **The keys live on the shared projection only** (`shared_relationships`). A member's own rows in `network_connections` are never rewritten.
- **`person_key`** (the URL, else the email) is the key kept on an introduction request.

**Provenance** is D1350's `contact_sources`: which source vouches for each row of a member's book.

**Access** is `relationship_shares`, below. It is private by default.

### 2. Teams: derived, never maintained by hand (Q2 = B)

`team_memberships` is a **view** over the memberships Axal already records, so it cannot drift from them:

| Team | `team_key` | Members, from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A venture | `project:<id>` | the founder (`users.founder_id = projects.founder_id`, role `founder`), and `project_members` rows that are `accepted`, not removed, and role `owner` or `cofounder` |
| An investor firm | `firm:<primary user id>` | the primary investor account, and its `investor_seats` that are accepted and not revoked (the seat user's `investor_seat_primary_user_id` must name the primary), as deal flow reads them (D632, D1238) |
| Axal HQ | `hq` | active `admin` accounts |

**Left out:**
- **Advisors.** A venture member with role `advisor` is not its team.
- **Inactive accounts and deleted ventures.**
- **Funds.** `vc_funds` names one GP and one fund-admin user, which is not a membership table. Funds stay out until one exists.

A firm with no seats is a team of one, and no one else sees it.

### 3. Sharing: one opt-in, off per team (Q3 = A)

`relationship_shares` holds one row per member and `team_key`, with `granted_at` and `withdrawn_at`:
- **`'*'`** is the member's opt-in: "my team may see who I know", for every team they belong to.
- **A row for one team** turns it on or off there alone. A team turned off stays off under the opt-in; a team turned on by itself stays on without it.

**Withdrawing** sets `withdrawn_at`, and every read checks it, so it takes effect on the next read.

**The routes:**
- **`GET /api/relationships/sharing`**: `{ opted_in, teams: [{ key, kind, label, shared }] }`.
- **`PUT /api/relationships/sharing`** `{ share }`: the opt-in.
- **`PUT /api/relationships/sharing/teams/:key`** `{ share }`: one of the member's own teams. Another team, or a malformed key, is 404.

**What a teammate sees** is that the member knows a person, and the person's name, company and title, with the source's label and "connected since". **Never** their email, phone, notes or LinkedIn URL. `shared_relationships` has no column for any of those.

### 4. Only eligible sources are shared

A row is in `shared_relationships` only when an eligible source vouches for it:
- `linkedin_csv`: the member's own `Connections.csv`, so theirs to share;
- `csv`: a spreadsheet the member imported;
- `typed`, or a row with no source at all: a row the member typed before D1350.

**API rows** (`linkedin_api`, `linkedin_dma`) are never shared until part 5's data-use flags (#3569) say a source may be. A row that only an API source vouches for is absent from the projection, whatever the member chose.

### 5. Access, in SQL, on every route

Every read is **one statement** joining all of:
- the caller's `team_memberships`;
- the teammate's membership of the same team;
- the teammate's share with that team that is effective now;
- `shared_relationships`.

Nothing is filtered after the read. The caller's own rows are never a result.

**`GET /api/relationships/who-knows?person=…|company=…|fund=…`**, exactly one of the three:
- **person** is a profile URL or an email;
- **company** and **fund** match `company` exactly, case-insensitive and trimmed. A fund is the employer a contact's row names.

It answers `{ query: { kind }, people: [{ ref, teammate: { id, name }, team: { key, label }, person: { name, company, title }, how: { source, source_label, connected_since } }] }`, at most 50.

### 6. Introductions: asked of the teammate; nothing reaches the contact

**`POST /api/relationships/intro-request`** `{ teammate, ref, note? }`:
- **Visibility is checked first,** by the same statement, so a row the caller cannot see is 404 "Not found." whatever the reason.
- **It writes one `relationship_interactions` row** (`intro_requested`, `pending`) and notifies the teammate alone ("Nothing is sent until you decide").
- **It sends nothing to the contact.** The teammate can write to them with the Email composer (#3547).
- **The limits:** 20 requests a day (429 `intro_rate_limited`), and a note under 1,000 characters.

**`GET /api/relationships/intro-requests`** answers `{ sent, received }`, the member's own only.

**`POST /api/relationships/intro-requests/:uid/decide`** `{ decision: made | declined }`:
- **only the teammate** answers;
- **once:** a second answer is 409 `decided`;
- it writes the matching `intro_made` or `intro_declined` row, with `parent_uid`, and notifies the requester.

`relationship_interactions` also carries `meeting` and `outcome`, and an `investor_introductions` or `intro_propositions` id where one exists. No route writes those yet; a later part records them.

### 7. Audit

Every share, withdrawal, who-knows read and introduction request writes an `integration_audit_log` row (provider `relationships`, D1350's events). The rows hold ids and counts only, never an email.

| What | Event | Owner | Subject |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opt in or out; one team on or off | `share` | the member | `grant:<team>` or `withdraw:<team>` |
| A who-knows read | `access` | the reader | `who_knows:<kind>:<count>` |
| Each teammate whose relationship it showed | `access` | that teammate | `who_knows_shown:<kind>` |
| An introduction request | `access` | the teammate | `intro_request:<uid>` |
| The teammate's answer | `access` | the requester | `intro_<made|declined>:<uid>` |

A support session is refused on every route (403 `support_session_refused`). What a member shares is theirs to choose.

### 8. Checks

**`cloudflare-worker/test/relationships_d1356.test.ts`: 9 tests,** on the real migrated schema with synthetic people. `fetch` throws, so nothing can reach a contact. The cast covers:
- a venture's founder, its co-founder and its advisor;
- another venture's founder;
- an investor firm, its accepted seat and its revoked seat;
- two HQ admins.

The tests cover:
- **The key test.** With nobody shared, every member calls every relationships route (who-knows by URL, email, company and fund, and the introduction requests) against every other member's contacts. Every answer is empty or 404, and no interaction is written.
- **Shared:**
  - the teammate's reply is exactly name, company and title, with source and date;
  - none of the email, phone, notes or URL is anywhere in the reply;
  - matching works by exact email too;
  - another venture, the advisor, the firm and HQ see nothing;
  - the member's own rows are never a result.
- **Ineligible and unmatchable:**
  - an API-only row is never shared, and cannot be asked for;
  - a name alone is refused, and has no identity key;
  - a member's own rows are not rewritten.
- **Withdrawal:** for one team and for every team, effective on the next read; a team turned off stays off under the opt-in; another member's team, a malformed key and a non-boolean `share` are refused.
- **Teams:** the firm and its accepted seat, never the revoked seat; HQ admins; a founder is not HQ; an advisor is on no venture's team.
- **Introductions:**
  - only the teammate is notified, and nothing goes to the contact;
  - each side sees only its own requests;
  - only the teammate decides, once;
  - a long note is refused;
  - after withdrawal, a new request is 404.
- **Limits and sessions:** the daily limit holds, and a support session is refused on every route.
- **The audit:** the exact rows, and no `@` anywhere.

**Guards:**
- clean: `check-sql-prepare`, `check-sqlite-tables`, `check-sqlite-columns`, `check-sqlite-dialect`, `check-migration-declarations`, `check-runtime-schema-declared`, `check-refusal-bodies` and `check-secret-logging`;
- every statement is a bound literal.

**Mutation checks, both ways.** They covered:
- the team join, the "not myself" rule, both withdrawal checks and the per-team grant;
- matching by email, and the visibility check behind an introduction;
- the daily limit;
- the teammate-only decision and its once-only rule;
- the owner filter on requests and the team check on sharing;
- each audit write;
- the view's co-founder role, revoked seats, eligible sources and admin-only HQ;
- the support-session refusal.

One survivor on the first pass gained a test: asking the wrong teammate for someone else's contact. One mutant is equivalent: dropping the owner filter on `GET /intro-requests`. The reply also splits sent and received by user in code, so no answer changes. The SQL filter stays, so another member's rows never fill the first hundred.
