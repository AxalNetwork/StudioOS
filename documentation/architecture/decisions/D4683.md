## D4683 — Trust publication is one reviewed act, and every subprocessor notice ends in a recorded outcome (#4683)

- **Status:** Built for #4683 (HQ Security audit 3 of 6), 2026-11-09. Worker only. The presentation belongs to #4802.
- **Builds on:**
  - D1075 (the trust-center store and the subprocessor notice sweep, migration 584);
  - D4675 (Publish names the review it publishes and records once);
  - D4803 (per-notice delivery counts in the Trust workspace).
- **Migration:** `202611092240_subprocessor_notice_recovery.sql`. It is additive: `attempts`, `interrupted` and `sent_at` on `subprocessor_notice_deliveries`.
- **Public record only.** The repository is public. Specifics are not recorded here (SECURITY.md).

### Publication (D4675's mechanism, now pinned under concurrency)

1. **A Publish matches the exact reviewed set.** The editor's read carries a review token over every pending draft. A Publish whose token is not current publishes nothing (`changed_since_review`). The batch re-checks the same set as its first statement, so a save landing mid-flight also publishes nothing.
2. **Concurrent or repeated Publishes make one record.**
   - Two Publishes of the same review at once: one records the change. The other publishes nothing, and its review is stale.
   - The same Publish sent twice at once (one key): one record. The second answers with it (`replayed`).
   - Nothing new was needed in the code for this. The tests now pin both races.

### Notice delivery

3. **An interrupted delivery is recovered and said to be uncertain.**
   - The sweep claims each delivery (`sending`) before it sends. A claim still `sending` after a 15-minute lease was cut off between the send and the write, so whether the email left is **unknown**.
   - The Trust workspace counts such claims as `unknown`, and the notice reads **needs attention**.
   - The next sweep re-claims the delivery in one conditional update, so of two sweeps only one goes on. It counts it in `interrupted` and sends again: a notice of a new subprocessor delivered twice is better than one never delivered.
   - A delivery resent after an interruption is counted (`resent_after_interruption`), because that subscriber may have the notice twice.
4. **A failing delivery stops and says so.**
   - A failed send is retried after an hour, up to six attempts in all, counting attempts made after an interruption.
   - After that it is left failed (`exhausted`) and the notice needs attention, instead of retrying for ever.
5. **Delivery reconciles with the effective date.**
   - Each notice carries `notice_due_by`: ten days (`NOTICE_DAYS`) before the subprocessor starts, the notice period Publish already enforces.
   - A send accepted after that date is counted `late`.
   - After the date, a notice with any subscriber still owed is not "in progress": it needs attention.
   - On the start day the sweep sends nothing more. A notice with subscribers never reached is **missed**, and stays visible for 90 days (D4803).
6. **Delivery reconciles with the public change log.** Each notice names the change-log entry that published its subprocessor (`change_uid`, `published_at`) and whether that entry is on the log (`in_change_log`). A notice is never attributed to an entry the log does not hold.
7. **One state per notice:** `delivered`, `in_progress`, `needs_attention` or `missed`. A notice reads delivered only when every confirmed subscriber's send was accepted by the provider. Whether it reached an inbox is not recorded, as before.

### Not changed

- Who can Publish (the Super Admin write bar), the audit rows in the publish batch, and the ten-day floor on a new subprocessor's start.
- The subscriber store, its double opt-in and unsubscribe, and the encryption of addresses.
- `outcome` keeps 584's CHECK. "Unknown" is read from an expired claim, not stored as a fourth value.
