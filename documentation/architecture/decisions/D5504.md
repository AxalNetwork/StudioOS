## D5504 — Scoped business time and observable UI readiness (#5504)

This first batch replaces live business time and fixed-turn settling in the two
examples named by #5504. The existing coordinated future-clock loader and nightly
job remain unchanged; there is no global loader freeze or timeout increase.

| Candidate | Classification and change |
| --- | --- |
| `platform_overview_d4860.test.mjs` | Live minute-rounded fixture time plus twenty event-loop turns: a candidate timing dependency, not a newly proven product defect. Use a fixed scoped Date clock per test, restored by Node's test context; scheduling remains real. Wait for the rendered source-read outcome, with cleanup on failure. |
| `deal_flow_board_d1240.test.mjs` | Ten zero-delay timers used as readiness: a candidate asynchronous race. Wait for the observable toolbar/major read state; the score assertion explicitly waits for its expected fit, and the intentional loading test explicitly waits for the loading view. Persistent unsupported cell placeholders are not assumed to disappear. |

`_waitUntil.mjs` checks an observable predicate with a real monotonic deadline,
yields through the supplied event/React flush, and reports a bounded observed
state when the deadline expires. It does not equate an arbitrary number of ticks
with completion. A missing/wrong result fails even while Date is frozen.

The shrink-only `scripts/test-clock-allowlist.txt` remains the candidate inventory.
This batch removes two entries (one date, one sleep), adding none. It leaves
941 entries across 870 files. Those remaining entries are candidate
patterns, not 870 confirmed regressions; this batch does not claim their
migration. The Trust Publish product repair remains #5495. Worker business clocks
and further asynchronous fixtures remain later batches.

New coverage checks before, exactly at, and after the 24-hour silence boundary
across UTC month/year ends, leap day and explicit DST-transition offsets. A
mounted-page assertion verifies the scoped observation time. Every new assertion
is inverted and restored; removing the clock, returning from a wait early, or
changing the exact stale boundary must fail.

Local baseline: 34 affected/helper tests passed concurrently. The existing
future-clock proof confirmed JavaScript, SQLite and a clean-env child shell all
seven days ahead; 39 affected/helper/harness tests passed with libfaketime and
real monotonic scheduling. The first preload attempt lacked the library and is
excluded from this evidence. No hosted nightly result is claimed here.
