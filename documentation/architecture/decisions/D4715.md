## D4715 — Advisor reads show another member only what is theirs to see: part 1, the directory and slot projections (#4715)

- **Status:** Built for #4715, 2026-11-07. Worker and frontend.
- **Part:** 1 of #4715. The access policy for the whole advisor domain, which defines one coded refusal and who may read and write each advisor record, comes in a later part.
- **No migration.**

### What was decided

1. **The directory and the match list carry no account email.**
   - `GET /api/advisors` and `GET /api/advisors/match` build each advisor with `directoryAdvisorDto`, which is `advisorDto` without `email`. The public profile (`GET /:uid`) never carried it.
   - `id` and `user_id` stay. The pages key advisors by `id`, and the trust badge and the booking button read `user_id`.
2. **A slot's meeting link and notes belong to its advisor.** `GET /api/advisors/:uid/slots` returns the slot whole only to:
   - the advisor;
   - an admin;
   - a founder holding a `confirmed` or `completed` booking on that slot.

   Everyone else gets `publicSlotDto`, with no `meeting_url` and no `notes`. It adds `has_meeting_link` instead. A pending or cancelled booking does not open the link.
3. **The page reads the flag.** `slotView` gains `hasMeetingLink`, taken from `has_meeting_link` when present and otherwise from the slot's own link. The directory's video badge reads `hasMeetingLink`, so it still shows on a slot that has a link.

### Partner office-hour slots

`GET /api/partner-office-hours/partners/:uid/slots` handed every signed-in member each slot's meeting link and the partner's slot notes. It now follows the same rule as the advisor slots:

- the slot is whole for the partner who owns it (`users.partner_id`), an admin, and a founder holding a `confirmed` or `completed` booking on it;
- everyone else, another partner included, gets `publicSlotDto`: no `meeting_url`, no `notes`, plus `has_meeting_link`.

The founder booking page (`SpinoutLabOfficeHoursPage`) reads neither field, so no page changes. `cloudflare-worker/test/partner_public_slots_4715.test.ts` covers it with six tests on the schema baseline; seven source mutations were checked, and each one fails a test.

### A booking's meeting link waits for the confirmation

A founder held a session's meeting link the moment they asked for it, not when the host agreed. Two places did this:

- **Partner bookings list.** `GET /api/partner-office-hours/bookings/me` joined the slot's `meeting_url` onto every row, whatever its status. It now goes through `founderBookingDto`: `meeting_url` only on a `confirmed` or `completed` booking, and `has_meeting_link` on every row. The partner's own list (`/me/bookings`) is unchanged. The advisor booking lists never carried the link.
- **Tentative calendar copy.** When a founder books, the tentative calendar event (advisor and partner, D4703) went out with the slot's link as its location. It now goes out with none. The confirmation's push (`deferCalendarConfirm`) still carries the link, so the founder's calendar gains it when the host confirms.

The Office Hours list says "link on confirmation" from `has_meeting_link`, and Join still needs a confirmed booking that carries its link. `cloudflare-worker/test/booking_meeting_link_4715.test.ts` (five tests, six mutations checked) and `frontend/test/booking_meeting_link_4715.test.mjs` (two tests, two mutations checked) cover it.

### The advisor access policy

Every advisor route now asks the same three questions, in this order. `cloudflare-worker/src/services/advisorAccess.ts` holds them.

1. **Eligibility.** Does the account hold the Advisor licence today? It counts as the primary role, or as an additional licence that has not been revoked (D4472). `requireAuth` reloads the account on every request, so a demotion or a revoked licence takes effect on the next request. An admin also passes for the profile and practice routes. Cohort reads stay licence-only (`mayHoldCohortAssignment`).
2. **Ownership.** Is the advisor row the request acts on the account's own? That is the `advisors` row whose `user_id` is the account. The stored `users.advisor_id` is a shortcut to that row and never a grant on its own.
3. **The record.** Is the booking, engagement, deliverable or slot on that row? Routes filter by the owned row's id, never by a path id.

A founder's side of a booking (the founder who made it) needs no licence.

What changed:

- **Who may create a profile.** `POST /me` and `GET /me` use `mayActAsAdvisor`, which is the same rule as before (D4670). It is now one export, and its refusal is the coded `advisor_licence_required`.
- **Booking transitions and reviews.** Confirm, complete, no-show, cancel and the advisor's review decided "the advisor" by comparing `users.advisor_id` with the booking's advisor. They checked neither the licence nor that the row was the account's own. They now use `actsForAdvisor`, which is eligibility plus ownership. An admin and the booking's founder keep the access they had.
- **Cohort list and guidance edits.** `GET /me/cohort` lists no cohort for an account without the licence. Its assignment rows stay, so a restored licence brings them back. `PATCH /me/guidance/:uid` needs the licence, as the other cohort writes already did through `requireOwnCohort`.
- **Every `/me/*` practice route** already resolved its row through `myAdvisor`. That is now `actingAdvisor`, so those routes follow the same rule with no change in behaviour.

What a demotion does:

- The account keeps its advisor row, its bookings and its cohort assignments. Nothing is deleted.
- It can no longer read or write anything as the advisor.
- Its founders keep their side: cancelling, paying and reviewing still work.
- An admin can still act on every booking.

`cloudflare-worker/test/advisor_access_policy_4715.test.ts` has six tests on the migrated schema. They cover a former advisor, a live advisor, an admin, a pointer to another account's row, an additional licence before and after it is revoked, reviews, the cohort list and a guidance edit. Six source mutations were checked, and each one fails a test.

### A booking keeps the exact offer it was made on

Item 3 of #4715. A booking stored only its `session_type_id`, so what had been booked was always read back from the session type as it stood now. If the advisor renamed, retimed or repriced the type, every booking already made on it changed too. The booking pages also showed the advisor's general hourly rate, even when the founder was booking one particular offer.

- **The copy.** Migration `202611082030_advisor_booking_offer` adds `advisor_bookings.offer_json`. When a booking is written, `POST /slots/:id/book` copies the session type's name, length, pricing mode (`free`, `included`, `price` or `unset`), price and cadence note into it. A price is kept only in `price` mode. Re-booking a cancelled slot takes a fresh copy at that moment.
- **The read.** `bookingDto` returns `offer`: the copy, or null for a booking made before the column existed or made without a session type. Every list that uses `bookingDto` carries it, including the founder's list, the advisor's list and the booking response.
- **The pages.**
  - **Detail panel:** opened for a session type, it states that offer (for example "Strategy call · 45 min · $150") instead of the hourly rate. The rate stays when no type is chosen.
  - **Booking form:** says "You are booking: …" before the founder submits.
  - **Public booking confirmation:** names the offer the Worker recorded.
  - **Lists:** the founder's booking list and the advisor's request card show the recorded offer.
  - **One helper:** `offerFromType` and `offerLine` in `advisory/kit.jsx` produce the same shape the Worker copies.

Billing (`PATCH /me/bookings/:id/billing`) is unchanged. It still prices from the advisor's own entry; the "price, then platform-rate change, then billing-state edit" sequence is covered by item 8.

`cloudflare-worker/test/advisor_booking_offer_4715.test.ts` has five tests on the migrated schema, and `frontend/test/advisor_booking_offer_4715.test.mjs` has five tests, including a mounted `AdvisorDetail` and booking form. Ten source mutations were checked, and each one fails a test. Three older test files that build their own schema now apply the new migration too.
### Correcting a signed engagement's terms

This is item 4 of #4715. Before this change, `PATCH /me/engagements/:id` overwrote scope, price, shape, client name and term end in place, whatever the lane. A signed contract's terms could change with nothing recording that they had, when it happened, or why.

**What counts as signed.** An engagement is signed once `started_at` is set. That covers the `signed`, `renewal_due` and `ended` lanes.

**The rule.**
- A draft or a proposal is still edited freely.
- For a signed engagement, a change to any term needs a `correction_reason` (1 to 500 characters). Without one the Worker answers 409 with code `correction_reason_required`, names the changed fields, and writes nothing.
- The terms are `client_name`, `shape`, `scope_label`, `scope_includes`, `scope_excludes`, `amount_cents` and `term_ends_at`.
- Linking the client's Axal account (`founder_user_id`) is not a term and needs no reason.

**The record.** Migration `202611082054_advisor_engagement_corrections` adds an append-only table. Each row holds:
- the lane at the time of the correction;
- the reason;
- each changed field with its value before and after;
- who made the correction.

The row is written in the same batch as the update. The update only applies if the row is still at the version this request read, and the correction is only written if that update applied. A write that lands in between therefore wins, and the correction writes nothing (409 `engagement_changed`). A caller may also send `expected_updated_at`, and a stale value is refused before any write.

**The history.** `GET /me/engagements/:id/events` merges the corrections, as `kind: "corrected"` with `changes` and the reason in `note`, with the signing, renewal and ending events, oldest first.

**The page.** Each signed card under "Scope on file" in Practice · Engagements gains two buttons:
- **Correct terms** opens a form with the current terms and a required reason. It sends only the changed terms and the version it opened on. If another change lands first, the page reloads the engagement.
- **History** lists the engagement's history, for example "Terms corrected · 2026-10-05 · Amount: $5,000 → $4,500 · Reason: …".

**Tests.**
- `cloudflare-worker/test/advisor_engagement_correction_4715.test.ts` has seven tests on the migrated schema, covering drafts, the refusal, the record and history, a no-op write, a stale view, a write that lands between read and update, and an ended engagement.
- `frontend/test/engagement_correction_4715.test.mjs` has three tests, including a mounted correction and history run.
- Thirteen source mutations were checked, and each one fails a test.
- Two earlier tests changed. `advisor_engagements_scope` edited a signed row with no reason, so it now sends one. `advisor_practice_pr2` lists the page's reads by name, so it now includes the history read.

### The audit's action sequences, run end to end

Item 8 of #4715. The audit found faults that only appeared across a series of legal actions, which the single-route tests had missed. Each sequence below now runs as one test.

**Through the mounted Worker, on the migrated schema.** These are in `cloudflare-worker/test/practice_sequences_4715.test.ts`, eight tests in all.

1. **Send, relink, then the recipient reads.** A version sent to client A stays with A after the engagement is relinked to client B. A still lists and opens it; B neither lists it nor can open it.
2. **Price, platform-rate change, then a billing-state edit.** A line priced at 15% keeps its 15% rate and cut when it is marked collected after the rate moves to 12%. A settled line also refuses a reprice.
3. **Complete racing confirm.** Two presses at once leave the booking in one state or the other, and any loser is refused. A second, deterministic test lands a completion between confirm's read and its write. The confirm then changes nothing and records no acceptance.
4. **Signed, renewal due, then ended.** The intermediate lane adds no cycle. Ending is refused as a lane move and recorded through the renewal decision, the history reads signed then ended, and an ended engagement cannot move again.
5. **An empty account, then its first engagement and deliverable.** The lists and totals read none, then one, and the deliverable takes its engagement's client.
6. **A block, then a booking attempt.** A blocked hour is refused with `slot_blocked`, without repeating the advisor's reason, keeps no booking row, and is not offered in the slot list.

**On the page.** These are in `frontend/test/practice_sequences_4715.test.mjs`.

- **Period switch before the response returns.** Earnings switches to last quarter while this quarter's note is still in flight. When the late answer arrives, it is dropped rather than shown under the new period.
- **Empty account, then its first engagement.** The empty state's own button opens the form, and the first engagement replaces the empty state on the board.

All of these pass against the current code. The fixes the audit asked for had already landed (D4684, D4679, D4695, D4713, D4687), so this part adds no source change, only the regressions.

Seven source mutations were checked, one per guarded rule, and each one fails a test. Ending is guarded twice: `/advance` refuses to move an ended engagement, and the lane graph lists no move out of `ended`. Removing either guard alone leaves the test passing, so that pair was checked by removing both, which fails it.
### The booking and review dialogs, and copy that described the build

Items 6 and 7 of #4715.

**The two dialogs.** The booking form and the review form were plain fixed `div`s. They had no dialog role and no name. Their close control was an unnamed × icon. Their labels were not joined to their fields, and errors were drawn in red but never announced. Both now open in `Lightbox`, which gives them:
- a dialog role, `aria-modal`, and a name taken from the title;
- focus moved inside on open, kept inside while open, and returned on close;
- a close button named "Close", and Escape to close;
- below 640px, a bottom sheet (`phone="sheet"`).

Inside the dialogs:
- Each field has a `<label htmlFor>`.
- The booking topic is marked required. A missing topic sets `aria-invalid` and points `aria-describedby` at the error.
- Errors carry `role="alert"`.
- Submit buttons are real `type="submit"` buttons, set `aria-busy` while sending, and say "Sending request…".
- Controls show a visible focus outline.

**Ratings.**
- The rating you set is a named group of five buttons ("1 star" … "5 stars"), each with `aria-pressed`, at least 44px square.
- A rating you only read is one image labelled "Rated 4 of 5", not five buttons that do nothing.

**Slot buttons.** Each slot's "Book" button now names its slot, for example "Book Tue 10 Nov, 10:00, 45 minutes", is at least 44px tall, and shows a focus outline.

**Product copy.** Some text on Practice pages explained the page by its implementation history: design-file artboards, a table's column count, a 501, "the worker refuses". These passages now say the same limits in product terms:
- Delivery: the follow-ups card, the send rule, and reading time.
- Opportunities: who can book, where requests come from, and template usage.
- Sessions: the batch summary.
- Engagements: renewal notices, the drag-free board, and why a draft has no decision.

The history remains in code comments and in these decision files. Two existing pin tests had quoted the old wording ("used 6", "Five pieces", "consent-gated"). They now check the new statement of the same absence.

**Tests.** `frontend/test/advisor_dialogs_a11y_4715.test.mjs` has three tests. They cover the booking dialog's role, name, close control, labels, the announced and linked error, and Escape. ### The advisor router split

Item 5 of #4715. `routes/advisors.ts` had grown to 5,903 lines, holding the catalogue, slots, bookings, cohorts, engagements, deliverables, reviews and money in one router. Six domains now live in their own route modules:

| Module | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `routes/advisors_services.ts` | What an advisor offers and charges (migration 203). |
| `routes/advisors_proof.ts` | Proof items and their attestations (migration 204). |
| `routes/advisors_money.ts` | What a session was worth, the take rate, payouts, earnings and tax (migrations 205 and 241), including `POST /bookings/:id/pay`. |
| `routes/advisors_cohorts.ts` | An advisor's assigned cohort and the founders who opt in or out (migration 206), plus HQ's assignment writes. |
| `routes/advisors_engagements.ts` | Engagements: their lanes, renewals and term corrections (migration 238). |
| `routes/advisors_deliverables.ts` | What an advisor sends a client, and the client's own list, open and acceptance of it (migration 239). |

`routes/advisors.ts` (now 1,971 lines) keeps the directory, matching, slots, bookings, reviews and the Sessions settings. **One implementation of each shared rule.** What more than one module needs moved to `services/advisorPractice.ts`, and every module imports it from there:

- the advisor row and the booking row, their DTOs, and the booking's offer;
- the engagement row and its owned-record loader, `requireOwnEngagement`;
- the caller's advisor, `myAdvisor` and `requireMyAdvisor`;
- the money helpers, `advisorMoney` and `parsePriceCents`;
- the validation refusal and the list page sizes.

Nothing in that service names a router. The access policy stays in `services/advisorAccess.ts`. The booking transitions are used only by the booking routes, so they stay in `advisors.ts` with them.

**Routes do not change.** `advisors.ts` mounts each module with `advisors.route('/', …)` at the point where its routes used to sit. The router's `use('*')` store bootstrap runs before every one of them, and `/api/advisors` and `/api/mentors` mount the same router as before. The 89 routes were listed from the router before and after the split, and the method, path and order are identical. Every name `advisors.ts` exported is still exported from it, so no importer changed.

**Why the route registry does not mount them.** The registry (#4105) mounts a module after every hand-written mount in `index.ts`. That would move these routes behind others that share their prefix, and outside the router's `use('*')`. So they mount inside `advisors.ts`, as `admin_cohort.ts` and `admin_licences.ts` mount theirs, and `index.ts` is untouched.

**How the split was made.** It was done mechanically from the TypeScript syntax tree: whole top-level statements moved, and no statement body changed. One boundary was corrected by hand: the client's accept route sat under the Sessions banner, so it moved to the deliverables module with the client's open route. Each module's router is named `advisors`, as the original was, so a search for `advisors.post('/bookings/:id/pay'` still lands on the handler.

**Tests that read the source.** These follow the code to where it now lives:

- Eight Worker pins now read the module that holds their code.
- `rateLimit_advisor_charge` also checks that `advisors.ts` mounts the money module.
- `expiry_gate_datetime_d124` reads `advisors_engagements.ts`, where the one gate that was in `advisors.ts` now is, so the count stays at 18.
- The frontend pins that read "the worker" as a whole use `frontend/test/_advisorRoutesSource.mjs`. It reads `advisors.ts`, every `advisors_*.ts` module it imports, and the shared service, so a handler moving between those files never leaves the test's sight.

Each repointed pin was mutation-checked against the moved code, and each mutation fails it.

`frontend/src/lib/branchFreeze.js` names the route files that call the branch freeze and the program-grant gates, and two parity tests hold that list to the worker. The mentor-assignment writes that call both gates are in `advisors_cohorts.ts` now, so the Programs row names that file.

### The dialogs, checked in a browser

Item 7 asks for validation at 390px as well as on desktop, which source tests cannot do. The built page was loaded in Chromium at 390×844 and at 1280×900. Every `/api/*` call was answered in the browser with invented fixtures, so nothing reached a server. Both widths passed:

- the advisor directory, the advisor's sheet and the booking dialog all render;
- the dialog is named "Book <advisor>", is `aria-modal`, and takes focus;
- Tab stays inside the dialog;
- Escape closes it and returns focus to the slot's Book button, whose name says which slot it books;
- both fields are labelled, and an empty topic is announced as an alert;
- the focus outline is visible, and the Book button is 44px tall;
- at 390px the dialog fills the width and the page has no sideways scroll.

The check found one defect. `Lightbox` pads its header and leaves its body to the caller, and both forms used `p-1`. So at 390px the fields sat 4px from the screen edge while the title sat 20px in, and on desktop the fields touched the dialog's sides. Both forms now use `px-5 py-4`, the inset most other dialogs give their body. `advisor_dialogs_a11y_4715.test.mjs` pins it, and putting `p-1` back on either form fails it.

`practice_first_use_4712.test.mjs` typed a slot's end as the start's hour plus one, as text. From 23:00 to 23:59 UTC that produced 00:xx on the same date, an end before the start, which the form correctly refused. The full suite failed on it during this work. The test now adds an hour to the start as an instant.
