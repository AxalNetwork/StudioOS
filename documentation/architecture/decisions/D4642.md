## D4642 — A support entry's amendment is guarded, attributed and recorded (#4642)

**Date** 2026-11-09 · **Status** accepted · **Area** Worker route
`portfolio_support.ts`, migration `202611092215_support_entry_revisions.sql`,
the Value-add desk (`IP3`).

### The defect

`PATCH /api/portfolio-support/:uid` was an unconditional `UPDATE … WHERE uid =
?`. Three things followed from that, all of them reachable by two members
doing ordinary work:

1. **A stale save won.** Two members who both opened revision N both saved;
   the second overwrote the first, and neither was told. The ledger's purpose
   is to survive an LP asking what was done for a company, and a store whose
   last writer wins silently cannot answer that.
2. **An amendment was unattributable.** `recorded_by` says who *created* the
   entry. Nothing said who amended it, what it said before, or that it had
   been amended at all.
3. **A refusal was reported as an unreadable ledger.** The page wrote a
   refused write into `state.error`, so one entry's 409 blanked every stat to
   "Unavailable" and said the support ledger could not be read — about a
   ledger it had just read and was still drawing.

A fourth, found while fixing the first three: an amendment to an entry already
`delivered` but carrying no `delivered_at` **back-stamped today**, inventing a
delivery date for a delivery that happened at some other time.

### The decision

Follow the shape D4636 gave a company update, so the two read alike.

**1. `revision` is the compare-and-swap key.** `portfolio_support_entries`
gains `revision INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 1`. A client that read revision N
sends `expected_revision: N`; the `UPDATE` carries `WHERE uid = ? AND revision
= ?`, so two editors who both opened N cannot both write it. The loser is
refused `409 changed_since_open` **and handed the row as it now stands**, which
is what preserves the newer accepted work rather than merely declining to
destroy it. Rows written before the migration default to 1, which is the
revision their first read reports, so an older client that sends nothing keeps
working.

The guard is in the statement, not in the read before it. The route checks
`expected_revision` early for a clear refusal, but a writer that lands *between*
that read and the `UPDATE` is caught by the `WHERE` clause: `meta.changes !== 1`
gives the same `changed_since_open`, read back from D1 rather than inferred.

**2. The caller may name the record it means.** `project_uid` is optional on
the PATCH; when present and not the entry's company, the amendment is refused
`409 record_mismatch` rather than applied to whatever that uid resolves to. A
stale list in one tab must not edit another company's entry.

**3. Order of refusals: the cause, not the symptom.** `expected_revision` is
checked **before** the terminal-state rule. A member who opened a `promised`
entry a colleague has since delivered gets `changed_since_open` ("their change
is shown, apply yours again"), not `state_is_terminal` ("this is already
delivered"), because the stale read is the cause and the terminal state only
the symptom of it.

**4. Only a transition stamps a date.** `delivered_at` and `withdrawn_at` are
filled from the request, else from the day **only when the state actually
moves**. Amending a terminal entry that carries no date leaves it carrying
none; a date the store never had is worse than an absent one.

**5. Every accepted amendment is recorded.**
`portfolio_support_entry_events` takes one row per amendment, written in the
same `batch()` as the `UPDATE` and never changed afterwards — no route issues
an `UPDATE` or a `DELETE` against it. It carries the revision it produced, the
actor, the transition when there was one, and the names of the fields that
changed. `UNIQUE (entry_id, revision)` means the history cannot hold two
amendments for one revision.

The history `INSERT` selects the row **the `UPDATE` just wrote**: it matches on
the new revision *and* on this request's own `updated_at` and `updated_by`.
Matching the revision alone was not enough, and a test found it: a writer that
lands mid-save bumps the row to that same revision, this request's conditional
`UPDATE` is refused — and a predicate of `revision = nextRevision` would still
have found *their* row and recorded this request's rejected change as history.

**6. A request that changes nothing is not an amendment.** A replayed or
double-clicked save whose fields all match returns `200 … unchanged: true`
without bumping the revision or writing a history row, so the history stays a
record of changes rather than of clicks.

**7. The desk reports the write, not the read.** The page keeps a `notice`
for one entry's write outcome, separate from `state.error` (the ledger read).
A refusal names the company, says what the member can still do, and offers
"Reload the ledger"; the stats beside it stay as they were, because the read
they came from succeeded. A conflict adopts the row the server handed back, so
the page shows the accepted work. A draft re-checks its company against the
reloaded book before saving, and both writers refuse a second submit while one
is pending.

**8. The attribution is visible.** An amended row says how many times, and
offers its recorded history: verb, actor, date, revision and fields changed.
An entry at revision 1 says it has not been amended rather than offering an
empty list, and an unreadable history says so rather than reading as "no
amendments". An amendment by a member no longer on the account is named as
that, not dropped.

### What this does not change

The state machine: `promised → delivered | withdrawn`, both terminal, enforced
by migration 237's `CHECK` and by the route. Amending a *terminal* entry's
hours, outcome or description was always allowed and still is — what is new is
that it is recorded and attributed rather than applied silently. The write
stays open to an investor rather than becoming admin-only (the route argues
for that gate in its own header); the book scope is unchanged, and the history
read is behind the same `canViewLpData` and the same accessible set, so an
entry outside the caller's book answers `Not found` rather than an empty
history.
