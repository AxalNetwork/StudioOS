## D3678 — Eadwyn AI joins Google Meet signed in as its own Google account (#3678)

- **Status:** built 2026-11-04 (#3678). The owner chose answer A (a Workspace user, `eadwyn@axal.vc`) on 2026-11-01, and set the account and its three secrets on 2026-11-04.
- **Number:** D3678 is the issue's number (D4106).
- **Migration:** none.

### Why

The bot opened Meet as an anonymous guest. A Workspace that admits only
signed-in users refuses an anonymous guest ("You can't join this video call",
D1374), and every live Meet run so far stopped on the pre-join screen. The
owner wants joining to be simple: Eadwyn AI asks to join, the host clicks
Admit, and nobody changes a Workspace or Meet setting.

### What changed

- **The account.** `eadwyn@axal.vc`, used only for joining calls, with
  2-Step Verification on an authenticator app. Hosts see its display name,
  "Eadwyn AI (Axal VC)", and admit it as any external participant.
- **The secrets** are `MEET_BOT_GOOGLE_EMAIL`, `MEET_BOT_GOOGLE_PASSWORD` and
  `MEET_BOT_GOOGLE_TOTP` (the authenticator's setup key), on the **site**
  Worker only (PRODUCTION.md §4).
- **How they reach the bot.** The bot host is a separate Worker and never holds
  them. A Meet run that is still `joining` asks over its signed callback,
  `POST /api/meeting-bots/:uid/meet-credentials`, as Zoom's run credentials
  already arrive (D886 §12):
  - `{}` returns the email and password;
  - `{"step":"code"}` returns a six-digit code and how long it stays valid.
  The TOTP key never leaves the site Worker: `services/meetBotGoogle.ts`
  computes the code (RFC 6238, HMAC-SHA-1, 30-second step). A run asks at most
  `MEET_CODES_MAX` (4) codes, and at most once every 2.5 s. A Zoom run, or a
  run past joining, gets 403. Answers are `no-store`. The owner diagnostic has
  its own callback, `/api/meeting-bots/diagnostics/:id/meet-credentials`, for a
  Meet diagnostic inside its window.
- **The sign-in.** `meeting-bot/src/adapters/meet/signin.ts` signs in on
  accounts.google.com (the meeting link is not in that URL) before the bot
  opens the meeting:
  1. the email, then the password;
  2. the authenticator, through "Try another way" when Google offers another
     method first;
  3. the code, retried once with the next code if Google refuses it.
  Signed in, Meet shows no guest name field. If it does, Google dropped the
  session, and the run stops rather than join as a guest under a lost session.
- **Each failure has its own code**, as `platform_blocked@<code>` in the run's
  `failure_detail` and in Diagnose's join row, each with its fix:
  - `signin_rejected`: wrong email or password;
  - `signin_challenged`: a captcha, "Verify it's you", or "this browser may not be secure";
  - `signin_code_refused`: the code was refused twice;
  - `signin_no_code_method`: no authenticator code was offered;
  - `signin_unknown_page`: a sign-in screen the hooks do not match;
  - `signin_unavailable`: the Worker did not give the account;
  - `signin_not_kept`: signed in, but Meet showed the guest field.
  To carry these, a join failure now reports the adapter's own detail when it
  has one (`lifecycle.ts`), and otherwise its last step as before (D1368).
- **Fallback.** With any secret missing, the callback answers 404
  `meet_signin_unconfigured`, and the bot keeps the guest join.
- **Gates kept:** consent, notice-before-capture, the canary and the Chromium
  sandbox are unchanged. Privacy (D886): log lines are codes only; the email,
  password and codes are typed into the page and never logged or thrown.

### Not verified

The sign-in hooks follow Google's page as it is widely documented, and
`meeting-bot/test/fixtures/google_signin.html` is constructed to match them,
not recorded. Google may also challenge an automated sign-in from a data
centre. The live check decides both: after the deploy, the owner runs
"Diagnose Eadwyn AI" on a Meet call and posts its "Stops at" row on #3588.
If Google challenges the sign-in, the next step is a stored session the owner
refreshes, decided then.
