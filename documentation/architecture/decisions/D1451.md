## D1451 — Meeting bot: the Teams join path and its rollout switch (#5297)

- **Status:** part 1 of 5 of #3709, built by S24 on #5297, 2026-11-10. The owner accepted S1's picks on 2026-11-09: Q1 = C (the browser guest path now), Q2 = B (a trial tenant plus a Teams free link for the live check), Q4 = A (the owner turns Teams on, in a PR). The specification is S1's comment of 2026-10-31 on #3709. Parts 2 and 3 amend this file.
- **Migration:** none. `meeting_bots.platform` already allows `teams` (531), and `failure_detail` (655) already holds a run's last step.
- **Builds on:** D886 §10 (the Teams adapter, B5), D1346 (whether Eadwyn AI can join, read before Send), D1359 (a Teams meeting created on the member's Microsoft connection), D1368 (a failed run says where it stopped).

### 1. Why

On `main` before this part, the Worker reported Teams as joinable whenever Meet was: `botConfigured` had a platform gate for Zoom only. But the packaged runtime hands `TeamsAdapter` a raw Playwright page (`meeting-bot/src/runtimeAdapters.ts`, the `as unknown as TeamsPage` cast), which has none of the `TeamsPage` methods the adapter calls. A Teams run therefore fails at its first `page.query(...)` and ends as `platform_blocked`. Under `canary` a Super Admin could send the bot to a Teams call that was certain to fail, and pay a token hold for it.

The link hosts also disagreed. The Worker's `botPlatform`, the Graph meeting parser and the frontend accepted `teams.cloud.microsoft`; the bot's `teamsJoinUrl` and the session setup check did not. A `teams.cloud.microsoft` link was accepted, held, started, and then failed as `link_invalid` in the container.

### 2. The join path: the browser guest, now; Graph later, on evidence

- **Taken: the guest browser path** that D886 §10 already describes. Eadwyn AI opens the meeting link in the browser as an anonymous guest named "Eadwyn AI", waits in the lobby, and is admitted by the organizer. Tenants that turn anonymous join off, block detected bots or show a captcha refuse it; part 3 gives the member an honest sentence for each.
- **Recorded, not built: the Microsoft Graph Communications media bot.** It needs an Azure Bot registration, tenant admin consent, Windows media hosting and an ongoing Azure cost. It is decided **after a month of blocked counts**: once Teams is on, the share of Teams runs refused as guests-off, bot-blocked or captcha (part 3's derived `blocked` reason) is read for a month, and the owner decides whether that share justifies the Graph path. Until then nothing in the repository depends on Azure, and no Microsoft secret is needed.

### 3. The switch: `MEETING_BOT_TEAMS`

- A Worker **var, not a secret**, declared in `wrangler.toml` `[vars]`, `[env.production.vars]` and `[env.preview.vars]`, all as `"off"`, and typed in `cloudflare-worker/src/types.ts`.
- **On only when the value is exactly `on`**, the same spelling `MEETING_BOT` uses. Missing, `off` or anything else is off.
- `botConfigured(env, 'teams')` returns false while it is off, before any runtime check. From that one line:
  - `botAvailability().platforms.teams` is false while Zoom and Google Meet keep their own answers, so Record a Session never offers Send for a Teams link and the capabilities row reads Teams as not joinable;
  - `POST /api/founder/validate/sessions/:id/bot` with a Teams link answers `503 bot_unavailable` before the active-run read, the budget check, the `meeting_bots` insert or any token hold.
- It narrows `MEETING_BOT` and never widens it: with `MEETING_BOT` `off` Teams is off whatever this says, and under `canary` it still applies to Super Admins only (`botMemberEnabled`).
- **Who turns it on:** the owner, in its own reviewed PR, after part 5's live check (Q4 = A). This part turns it on nowhere.

### 4. One set of Teams hosts

Every parser of a Teams link accepts the same three hosts, matched exactly on the parsed hostname, lower-cased, over `https:` only:

| Host | What it is |
|---|---|
| `teams.microsoft.com` | work or school meetings |
| `teams.cloud.microsoft` | the newer address of the same work or school meetings; the bot opens it as a `work` link |
| `teams.live.com` | Teams free (personal) meetings |

The parsers: the Worker's `botPlatform` (`services/meetingBots.ts`), `callRefusal` (`services/sessionSetup.ts`), `teamsJoinUrl` (`services/channels/teamsMeet.ts`), the frontend's `botPlatform` (`pages/founder/meetingBotSession.js`), `meetingLinkProblem` and `meetingHref` (`pages/founder/recordSetup.js`), and the bot's `teamsJoinUrl` (`meeting-bot/src/adapters/teamsSelectors.ts`). Each keeps its own path rule (the dispatch and the bot want a join path; the setup and Graph checks only want the host). A look-alike host (`evilteams.microsoft.com`, `teams.microsoft.com.attacker.invalid`), a sub-domain, a userinfo trick, `http:` and the US government clouds are refused by every one.

#5575 closes the userinfo gap in `callRefusal`, `meetingLinkProblem`, and `meetingHref`. Each rejects a URL with a username or password.

### 5. Correction to D886

D886's B2 follow-up says "Zoom/Teams factories remain fail-closed until their native/browser integration is supplied." For Teams that stopped being true when the packaged factory began building `TeamsAdapter` on the raw Playwright page: the factory does not fail closed, the run fails because the page lacks the driver's methods. From this decision the fail-closed gate for Teams is `MEETING_BOT_TEAMS` in the Worker, before any hold; the Playwright `TeamsPage` driver is part 2. D886 carries a pointer here.
