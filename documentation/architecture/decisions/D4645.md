## D4645 — Portfolio health says what it could not read, and a reminder is claimed before it is sent (#4645)

- **Status:** Built for #4645, 2026-11-09. Worker only. Package 4 of 5 of the
  Portfolio audit of 2026-11-06.
- **Builds on:** D1041 (`studioGlance`'s refusal to total over an unreadable
  source); D637 (`logActorAct`); D4703 (a conditional write and its 409, the
  same shape the reminder claim takes here).
- **No migration.** Every store this uses already exists.
- **Scope.** The Worker half S1 asked for on 2026-11-09: source-availability
  states, the health response contract, and the reminder eligibility, cooldown
  and deduplication lifecycle. The screen that consumes it waits for the #4817
  canvas. Reminders stay member-initiated: nothing here sends by itself.

### 1. The five states, in one place

`services/sourceState.ts` holds the platform vocabulary the owner approved on
2026-11-07 — `0`, Not recorded, Unavailable, Not applicable, Unreadable — as a
type and a read, rather than Portfolio inventing its own set. Four of those are
absences that mean different things; the fifth is a number, and it is in the
list because it is the state the other four kept being mistaken for.

`readSource` runs one read and keeps what happened: a value is `recorded`
(including `0`), `null` means the store answered and holds nothing
(`not_recorded`), and a **throw** is `unreadable` — logged, and never handed
back as a value. `unreadableLabels` names the sources that failed, which is how
a derived figure decides it cannot be stated at all.

The module exports only what is called. The related rule it does **not** yet
carry a helper for — that a total over sources is `null` when any of them was
unreadable, because a total which quietly skips one is smaller than the truth
and indistinguishable from a real total (D1041's `pending_total`) — is stated
in the module's header and gets its helper when a total needs one, rather than
shipping an export nothing uses.

### 2. What Portfolio health was doing, measured

`computeScore` in `routes/portfolio.ts` read four sources and ended every read
in `.catch(() => null)`, so a failed read and an empty store were the same
answer. Then:

| Source | Unreadable became | What that asserted |
|---|---|---|
| `score_snapshots` | `50`, via `drivers.scoring != null ? … : 50` | a measurement this platform never took |
| `tickets` | `0`, via `Number(t?.open \|\| 0)` | "no open tickets" |
| `activity_logs` | `0`, via `Number(a?.c \|\| 0)` | "no activity in 30 days" |

The third is the worst of them: activity is a **positive** signal, so an
unreadable activity log *lowered* the company's score, and a low enough score
sets `intervention: true`. A store nobody could read made a company look
dormant and could raise a flag against it, and no part of the response said so.

A fourth defect was in the same statement:

```sql
details LIKE ('%project=' || ? || '%')
```

A substring. Project 1 collected the activity of 12, 19, 100 and every other id
beginning with a 1 — and `activity_logs.project_id` is a column, so this was a
stringified copy of that column being matched loosely.

### 3. The rules now

`services/portfolioHealthSources.ts` holds the reads and the scoring, out of
the route so both can be tested without a request.

1. **An unreadable input means no score.** `score` is null, there is no badge,
   `intervention` stays off, and no snapshot row is written — `score` and
   `badge` are NOT NULL on `portfolio_health_snapshots`, so an unmeasurable
   score has nowhere to go, and inventing one is what the old `: 50` did. The
   project keeps whatever snapshot it had (yesterday's measurement is still
   true of yesterday) and the caller is told which source failed.
2. **A not-recorded input is a fact**, so the score is computed over the
   signals that answered and their weights are renormalised to the full
   measured share (0.9). A missing signal is neither invented at 50 nor counted
   as zero, and `measured_weight` says how much of the score was measured.
   With every signal present the arithmetic is **identical** to the old one —
   pinned by a test over four input sets, so this is not a re-scoring of the
   portfolio.
3. **With no positive signal at all there is nothing to score**, and that is
   said rather than scored.
4. **Activity is matched on the column**, exactly. Two delimited GLOBs keep the
   legacy rows that recorded their project only inside `details`: the id must
   be followed by a non-digit or end the string.
5. **Every figure states its window.** `window_days` is in the response rather
   than implied by the key `activity_30d`.

Two things worth writing down because they cost time:

- A GLOB whose pattern is **concatenated from a bound parameter**
  (`details GLOB ('*project=' || ? || '[^0-9]*')`) matches **nothing** in
  SQLite, while the identical pattern bound in one piece matches. Measured, and
  the reason `legacyActivityGlobs` builds the whole pattern in TypeScript. The
  test caught it; the statement looked right.
- In SQLite GLOB, `[^0-9]` is the negated class. `[!0-9]` is **not** a
  negation — it matches a literal `!` or a digit.

### 4. The health responses

- `GET /health` no longer drops a project that has no snapshot. They are
  reported apart, in `not_measured`, with the state of the snapshot store for
  them, so the overview's population is the population the caller may see and
  not "the projects that happen to have been scored". `population` names it.
  A `badge` or `intervention_only` filter is a request for a subset, so
  `not_measured` is null there rather than listing projects that are in no
  subset.
- Each item carries `age_days` and `stale`, against one documented
  `stale_after_days` (7) reported with the response. `as_of` is the **newest**
  snapshot in the list and says nothing about the oldest; a forty-day-old row
  read as current underneath it.
- `GET /health/:uid` states its `window` and distinguishes "never scored" from
  "nothing in this window", which `latest: null` could not.
- `POST /health/recompute` counted the projects it **walked**, not the ones it
  scored, so a run in which every read failed reported full success. The two
  are counted apart and each unscored project names its reason.
- `POST /health/recompute/:uid` answers 409 `health_not_measurable` with the
  state of each input, rather than a score nothing measured.

### 5. "Ask again" is claimed before it is sent

`POST /portfolio-updates/asks/:askUid/ask-again` checked the cooldown, notified
every recipient, and only then wrote `asked_again_at` — by id, unconditionally.

- Two presses at once (a double click, a retried request) both passed the check
  and both notified everyone; the second write overwrote the first's timestamp.
- A `notify` that threw part-way through left `asked_again_at` unset, so a retry
  re-sent to everyone already told. The recipients at the front of the list got
  the reminder twice and nothing recorded how far the first attempt had got.
- The response's `asked_again` was the **length of the recipient list** — a
  claim of delivery the handler never checked.

The cooldown is now **claimed in SQL before anything is sent**, with the same
predicate `canAskAgain` applies written into the UPDATE's WHERE clause and
bound, including the "no reply yet" arm. Only the request whose write changes a
row sends; the loser gets the 409 it has earned. `changes()` is the lock —
`transition`'s shape from D4703, reused rather than reinvented.

The recipients are read **after** the claim, so the list is the one current when
the reminder actually goes out. Each one is notified separately: `notify` is
best-effort and answers with the inbox row's id or null, so one recipient's
failure cannot cost the rest their reminder. The response reports `recipients`,
`notified` and `failed` apart, so an outcome can be reconciled against the list.

What it guarantees is stated as narrowly as it is true: one reminder per ask per
window, and at most one inbox row per recipient from a request. Delivery beyond
the inbox is the notify pipeline's and is **not** exactly-once, and the response
says so rather than implying a provider guarantee nothing here holds.

### Tests

- `cloudflare-worker/test/portfolio_health_sources_4645.test.ts` — a recorded
  zero against an unreadable store; an unreadable input yields no score, badge
  or intervention (and the test shows what badge the old figure *would* have
  produced); a missing scoring snapshot is 90 and not 60; the arithmetic is
  unchanged over four input sets; project 1 gets 3 rows and not 6; the window
  cuts at its stated edge.
- `cloudflare-worker/test/portfolio_ask_again_4645.test.ts` — the response's
  three counts and its delivery wording; a claim injected between the check and
  the write wins and the late press sends nothing; two concurrent presses yield
  one send and one inbox row per recipient; a reply landing after the check
  still stops the reminder; the cooldown counts from the last ask; a delivery
  that fails is counted and not thrown, and the window stays claimed so a retry
  cannot re-send; only someone who may operate the update can ask again.

Mutation-checked both ways: the claim made unconditional and by id, the reply
arm dropped from the claim, the unreadable branch disabled, and the `50`
substitute restored. Each one fails a test. The three existing suites that
cover this handler (`share_update_d654`, `cycle_grow_d664`,
`update_lifecycle_d4636`) still pass unchanged.
