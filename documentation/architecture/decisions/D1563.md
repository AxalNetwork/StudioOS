### 1. A document as an outline (`services/decks/documentOutline.ts`, beside `deckExtract.ts`)

- **Where.** The text comes from `deckExtract.extractDeck` (the router's metered conversion) or from a paste. The mapper is its own module, not part of `deckExtract.ts`: that file is on the advisor's and the crons' import paths, and the catalogue (`newDeck.ts`) reads the venture's sources, which the member bank-data guard keeps off those paths (`mercury_member_privacy_d752`).

- **Sections.** `documentSections` splits a document's markdown at its headings. With no headings it splits at blank lines, and a short line on its own with no closing punctuation becomes the heading of the paragraph that follows it.
- **Mapping.** `outlineFromDocument` maps each section to the catalogue card whose phrases (`CARD_KEYWORDS`) it names most.
  - A heading's match counts three times a body's.
  - Phrases match as whole words, so "task" is never the round's "ask". No pattern is ever built from the document.
  - Each card is used once. A section that names no card is counted as unmatched and left out.
- **The rows:**
  - The document's first top-level heading is the Title card.
  - A section's heading becomes its card's title, and its first sentence that names no number becomes the card's message.
  - A heading or a sentence that names a number gives way to the catalogue's own words, and is counted in `figures_left_out`. Figures come from the records, never from a document (D897).
- **Order and length.** The document's order is kept. A short document is padded to the standard twelve: each missing card goes before the first row that follows it in the standard order. A long one stops at the catalogue's sixteen cards.

### 2. A .pptx deck as editable cards (`services/decks/pptxCards.ts`, through D11's parser)

- **One parser.** `pptxTemplate.ts` gains what reading slides needs, and nothing else reads a .pptx:
  - `readPptxSlides` reads each slide in order, at most 60;
  - `slideShapesOf` reads each shape with its words; a slide's own text boxes count as content, where a layout's drawn shapes are decoration;
  - `paragraphsOf` reads a shape's paragraphs (`<a:p>`/`<a:t>`, entities unescaped);
  - `layoutOfRels` finds the layout a slide names in `ppt/slides/_rels/slideN.xml.rels`;
  - `areaOn` snaps one box to whole cells. `placeLayout` now calls it, unchanged in what it answers.
- **Inheritance.** A placeholder the slide does not place takes its layout's box, by index and then by type, else its master's, as PowerPoint draws it.
- **Placing.**
  - Each shape that holds words becomes one text block on whole cells. The title placeholder is the card's title and its heading. A shape of several paragraphs is a list; otherwise it is a paragraph.
  - An edge within `SNAP_TOLERANCE` snaps to its line. An edge between lines takes the nearer line, and a box smaller than one cell takes the cell it starts in. Both are counted as `moved`.
  - A box that would share a cell with a block already placed is nudged below it, or else beside it. When neither fits, its words join the text block under it as more lines (`merged`).
  - Words that would land on the title go to the card's notes, under "Not placed on the grid", and are counted as `not_placed`. Nothing is lost.
  - Date, footer, slide-number and header placeholders are not content and are not counted.
- **The counts** are the import's own: `text_chars`, `placed_chars` and `placed_share`, the share of the deck's characters in an editable block. `placed_share` is null for a deck with no words, never 0. #3810's target is ≥ 90%:
  - the 15-slide fixture places 99.5%;
  - the real deck in `attached_assets/` places 100%, all of it on whole cells.
- **Kept as written.** The words are the founder's own file, kept as text. A figure in them is the founder's, shown as text and never bound to a record. Pictures, charts and tables carry no words here.

### 3. The route (`routes/deck_imports.ts`, before `decks.ts`)

- **`POST /api/decks/imports?project_id=`** takes either JSON `{ text, audience? }` (a paste of at most 40,000 characters) or a multipart `file` with `audience`.
  - The file may be a .docx or a PDF of up to 20 MB, or a .pptx of up to 50 MB.
  - The kind comes from the file's name, else from its type. Anything else is refused 415 `file_type`.
- **Paste, DOCX and PDF** answer 201 `{ kind: 'outline', source, proposal, proposal_hash, outline: { rows, proposed_by: 'Your document' }, counts }`.
  - The outline is stored as the venture's outline proposal, for the audience asked or else New deck's fast-path audience.
  - The page then opens New deck on it (`?proposal=`), where Build deck quotes first and lands on the shared OneClickBuild review.
- **A .pptx** answers 201 `{ kind: 'cards', source: 'pptx', proposal, proposal_hash, counts }`.
  - The cards (`sanitizeSlides`) are stored as the venture's proposal and open straight on that review (Accept / Accept all, `keep-slides`).
  - It is switched by `deck:pptx_import`: off, it answers 404 `not_switched_on`.
- **Refusals:**
  - 400 `text_required`;
  - 413 `too_large`;
  - 422 `unreadable`, `empty`, `not_a_pptx` or `no_slides`;
  - 403 for anyone but the venture's writers (`projectOwned`);
  - 402 off New deck's plan.
  - A refusal stores nothing.
- **Audit.** `deck_outline_imported` or `deck_imported`, without the words.
- **Untrusted.** No model reads the document in the import: the outline is mapped by code, and a .pptx runs no model at all. The rows reach the build run only inside one untrusted block (`newDeck.buildMessage`, `record_text`).
- **Privacy.** All SQL is bound. Nothing logs the file, its name or its words.
- **Shared code.** `deck_new.ts` exports `writeProposal` and `proposalRow`, so an import writes the same row a fill does.
