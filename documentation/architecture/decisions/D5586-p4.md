### 1. The read route (`/api/work-graph/swarm`)

| Route | Answers |
| --- | --- |
| `GET /capacity?tenant_id&company_id[&since]` | The coordinator's snapshot of this venture as part 1's capacity read model, the bottleneck signals since `since` (default 7 days, at most 30), the two recorders below, the retention counts, and the list of signals nothing records |
| `GET /audit?tenant_id&company_id[&ticket_uid][&limit]` | D1's copy of the venture's admission audit (`swarm_admission_events`) and the coordinator's entries not yet copied |

- **Off by default.** Unless `WORK_GRAPH_SWARM_READ` is exactly `on`, both answer 404 `swarm_read_off` before any
  session or D1 read, as the release routes do with their flag.
- **The gate**, in order: a session; a link to the company that is not archived (`resolveActiveCompany`); the role
  that runs the company's work (Owner, Admin, Founder or the primary admin, `canEditAsMember`, the same role that
  manages its team agents); and no WorkGraph or swarm record of the company under another tenant
  (`work_graph_assets`, `work_graph_release_lines`, `swarm_admission_events`). That last check is the siblings'
  (`companyHeldElsewhere`, `companyUnderOtherTenant`) with the swarm's table added.
- **One 404.** Every gate failure is the same 404 `not_found` body. The siblings answer `not_member` and `wrong_tenant`
  with 403s. This route is a read of cost and capacity, and the issue asks that a cross-tenant read reveal nothing,
  so a company the caller is not in, one under another tenant and one that does not exist are indistinguishable.
  A platform admin without a seat is refused too: HQ's whole view is a separate route.
- **Server-trusted scope.** The tenant and company are named in the query, as on every sibling WorkGraph route, and
  are used only after the gate has checked them. The coordinator is asked through `swarmSnapshotFor(env, scope)` with
  that scope, so it answers only that venture's tickets and audit. Every D1 statement binds the same tenant and company.
- **Not zero.** No broker record holds a venture's policy yet, so concurrency, budget and storage are `not_recorded`.
  A coordinator that cannot be reached makes the snapshot sections `unavailable` (with the client's code), and D1's
  signals still answer. A recorder whose statement fails is `unavailable`. A rate over nothing is `not_recorded`.

### 2. Why tenant comes from the query

No table maps a company to its tenant. The WorkGraph tables carry both columns, and the siblings take both from the
request and refuse a company whose records already live under another tenant. Naming the wrong tenant for a company
with no records reveals nothing, because there is nothing under that pair. This route keeps that rule and does not
invent a mapping. If a company → tenant record is added, the gate takes the tenant from it (question A below).

### 3. Retention (`swarmRetention.ts`)

- **What retires.** A settled ticket (by `settled_at`), or a withdrawn one (by its new `ended_at`; a withdrawal from
  before this part has none and is aged from its enqueue), once it is `SWARM_RETENTION_MS` old (30 days). A ticket whose
  #5570 lease still holds a slot is kept. A lease-lane ticket takes its released slot with it.
- **Bounded.** At most `SWARM_RETENTION_BATCH` (200) tickets and 200 audit entries per storage transaction, at most
  `SWARM_RETENTION_MAX_BATCHES` (10) transactions per call, oldest first. `more: true` says work is left.
- **Idempotent.** A second call over the same state finds nothing due and changes nothing. The clock is read once.
  A clock behind the state's last decision is refused `clock_regressed`, and nothing retires.
- **A tombstone, not a hole.** Each retired ticket leaves `{ venture, state, bill, ended_at }` under its uid:
  - A redelivered request is refused `ticket_retired`. It is never admitted again, so it never runs or bills twice.
  - A repeated settle answers the first bill with `first: false`.
  - A withdraw answers the end state, only to its own venture.
  - The coordinator still treats the uid as the swarm's and that venture's (`ticketOwnerKey`). Another venture gets
    `ticket_not_found`, and a #5570 call on it gets `swarm_ticket`.

  The tombstones are bounded (`SWARM_RETIRED_MAX`, 5000). The oldest are evicted and counted in `retired.dropped`.
  A redelivery older than the 30 days plus the 5000 newer retirements would find no tombstone. That is far beyond any
  queue's redelivery window.
- **What was spent stays spent.** The budget counts settled charges (`budgetCommitted`), so deleting settled tickets
  would hand a venture its budget back. Retention carries each retired charge per venture (`retired.charged`), and for
  `brand_image` and `video_render` per piece (`retired.render`). Both `budgetCommitted` and
  `renderApprovalCommitted` add the carry.
- **The audit.** Only a leading run of in-state entries that D1 already holds (at or below `audit_flushed_seq`) and
  that is old enough is removed, counted in `retired.audit_compacted`. The cap's `audit_dropped` is now
  `first kept seq − 1 − audit_compacted`. That is the same number as before when nothing is compacted, and compacted
  entries never count as lost. A later copy still finds the right gap.
- **D1 is not touched.** `swarm_admission_events` is append-only, and its triggers refuse DELETE (D5586-p2 §2). It is
  the durable record retention relies on. Pruning it would mean dropping those triggers (question B).
- **Only this part's state.** #5570's own released leases (slots no swarm ticket holds) are not retired. Their
  replay safety is #5570's to define.
- **The schedule.** `runSwarmRetention(env)` posts `/retire` to the one coordinator. The schedule would be once a
  day off the hour, for example `17 3 * * *` UTC: one call retires up to 2000 tickets, and a `more: true` answer can be
  followed by another call in the same run. No cron trigger is added here. Wiring one into `wrangler.toml` and the
  `scheduled` handler is its own change, with the owner's yes.
- **The scoped snapshot.** `scopedSwarmState` now strips `retired` to the venture's own uids, carry and render keys.
  A venture's key prefix cannot match another's (`t7:c7|` and `t7:c70|`).

### 4. Recorders (`swarmRecorders.ts`)

| Signal | Source | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registered agents | `team_agents`, company-scoped or on the company's projects, not removed (`registered`, `active`, `paused`) | recorded |
| Image-model capacity | `ai_usage_logs` calls by those agents to a catalogued `draw` model since `since`: calls, `all_models_failed`, budget refusals, fallbacks and a failure rate | recorded (the venture's agents' calls only; a person's own call carries no company) |
| GitHub PR/CI wait | nothing records it | `not_recorded` |
| Preview transcoding | nothing venture-scoped (`learning_stream_*` is learning content) | `not_recorded` |
| Egress | nothing records it | `not_recorded` |
| Cost: CI minutes, Stream output, egress, storage GB-month | no priced record | `not_recorded` |
| Venture policy (concurrency, budget, storage quota) | needs a broker record (out of scope) | `not_recorded` |

The route lists the `not_recorded` names in `not_recorded`, so a reader sees what is missing instead of a 0.

### 6. Open questions

**A. Where does a company's tenant come from?** **A1 (my pick):** keep the siblings' rule (named, then refused if
held elsewhere) until a company → tenant record exists. **A2:** add that record now (a migration), and have every
WorkGraph route read it.

**B. Should D1's swarm audit be pruned too?** **B1 (my pick):** no. It is append-only, it is the record retention
leans on, and it is small (one row per transition). **B2:** an expand migration that replaces the DELETE trigger with
one that allows deleting rows older than a year, run by the same schedule.

**C. May retention be scheduled?** **C1 (my pick):** a daily cron (`17 3 * * *` UTC) in all three `wrangler.toml`
tables calling `runSwarmRetention`. **C2:** a Durable Object alarm on the coordinator, which needs no cron but runs
inside the object that serializes every admission.

### Rollback

Revert the commit. The flag is off by default, so nothing reads the route. A retention call has run only if someone
called `runSwarmRetention`, and nothing calls it. Tombstones and carry in the coordinator's state are optional fields.
Code from before this part reads a state that holds them, but ignores them. If retention has run, a revert therefore
loses two things: the carry (the budget counts fewer settled charges) and the `ticket_retired` refusal. Revert only
before the first retention run, or keep `swarmAdmission.ts`'s reads of `retired`.
