### 4.1 Backup retention: what is verified

#7739 and #7742 cite "30-day Time Travel and a 365-day object-locked nightly export", while [D1_RECOVERY.md](D1_RECOVERY.md) §7 describes a 35-day default and a 365-day monthly prefix. Read against the code on 27 November 2026, the two figures describe **two different buckets**, and neither retention period is set or checked by code in this repository.

| Copy | Where the figure is stated | What code verifies | Status of the period |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 Time Travel, `studioos-db` | D1_RECOVERY.md §1–§2 | Nothing in the repo sets it; it is the D1 platform's point-in-time window | 30 days: platform property, DECISION BASIS for this register |
| Primary nightly export, `studioos-backups/d1/<database>/backup-YYYY-MM-DD.sql` and its manifest | `backup-d1.yml` header comment and [INCIDENT_RESPONSE.md](INCIDENT_RESPONSE.md) § Backups: object lock 365 days, Standard-IA after 30 days, delete after 365 days, "configured one-time in the Cloudflare dashboard, NOT here" | The workflow exports nightly at 02:10 UTC, uploads with `scripts/d1-backup-upload.sh` (a plain multipart `aws s3 cp`, no lock or lifecycle header), reads the object back and checks its sha256. No step sets, reads or asserts a lock or lifecycle rule on `d1/` | 365 days: **DECISION BASIS** only. Live bucket lock and lifecycle rules were not inspected |
| Independent encrypted copy, outside-account bucket, `d1/<db>/daily/` and `d1/<db>/monthly/` | D1_RECOVERY.md §7 and the `scripts/dr-second-copy.sh` header: 35-day default lock, daily expiry 35 days, monthly prefix kept 365 days (35 daily and 12 monthly recovery points) | The script writes each export write-once (`If-None-Match: *`) under `daily/<recovery point>/`, and the month's first export also under `monthly/<YYYY-MM>/`. The step runs only when `DR_SECOND_COPY_ENABLED` is `true`; until the owner provisions the account nothing is copied. No code sets the bucket's retention | 35/365 days: DECISION BASIS; whether the copy is enabled at all is not recorded here |

So D1_RECOVERY.md is not wrong about the copy it describes; its 35-day figure is not the primary nightly export's period. What this register may state as verified from code is the *shape* of each copy (nightly, verified upload, write-once daily/monthly keys), not how long either survives. A record removed from live D1 (for example by a D7742 removal or an account erasure) can therefore persist in Time Travel for up to 30 days and in the nightly exports for as long as their live retention actually is, which only an inspection of the bucket settings (`wrangler r2 bucket lock list studioos-backups` and the lifecycle rules, kept in the restricted record) can establish. Until that is done, cite the 365-day figure as the decision basis, not as configuration.

The questions this raises for the owner are in [§7](#7-open-owner-designations).

## 5. Operating update and verification protocol

1. **Detect a new field/vendor:** the PR's Help/privacy impact decision identifies changed collection, processor integration, AI provider, tracking, role or storage. Link its route/schema and data class to a row here before publication. No editor should hand-type a new retention date without approved evidence.
2. **Route to owners:** Privacy/Legal owns purpose and legal basis; Engineering names live calls and stores; Security checks access/scope/region; Product explains the customer workflow; Finance/GP reviews investor/financial obligations; Trust owner prepares the public subset and notice date.
3. **Verify the flow with synthetic data in an approved environment:** sign up, consent and revoke, contact, recording and delete audio, optional connector grant/revoke, paid test checkout, KYC synthetic case, DSR request and manual fulfillment. Capture a redacted request trace, D1 store names, R2 key class, any processor transmission, expiry/hold result and replay/backup behavior **in a restricted evidence location**. No production-user payloads or actual identity documents in GitHub.
4. **Review vendor record:** service, real legal counterparty, data categories and roles, purpose, geography, transfer basis, contract/DPA/version, approved access, subcontractors, retention, deletion API/procedure, incident notice, owner and review date. Status may be **proposed**, **configured**, **active-verified**, **off**, **retired** or **unknown**.
5. **Publish through the existing Trust workflow:** owner-approved subset, contract-confirmed fields only; use the pre-publication notice and review/revision controls already owned by #4683. Keep sensitive contracts, account bindings and individual evidence out of public GitHub.
6. **Reconcile DSR:** #4675 consumes the approved row-level retention/hold matrix and outputs per-store, per-vendor evidence of erased, held-until-date or unresolved; never call a DSR complete based on a checkbox alone.
7. **Review cadence:** on every relevant PR/release, each vendor change and any material policy change. Schedule periodic legal/vendor reconciliation, but record the actual reviewer/time only after review has happened. A stale row must not show as verified.
