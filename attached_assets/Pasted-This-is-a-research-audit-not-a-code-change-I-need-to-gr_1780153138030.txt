## 1 · EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Market Intelligence module is **architecturally well-built and intelligence-empty**.

The platform ships a 32-connector "sources catalog" (`services/market_intel/sources/*.ts`), 7 scored dimensions, 4 lens dashboards, a watchlist + email digest system, an investor-signals aggregator with proper k-anonymity, and a tier-gated public surface. The plumbing is real and well-engineered.

**But of the 32 advertised data sources, exactly zero are pulling live data today.** 12 throw `paid_contract_not_signed` (Gartner, PitchBook, CB Insights, SimilarWeb, Glassdoor, LinkedIn, Indeed, Ahrefs, BuiltWith, NewsAPI, Semrush) and 20 return deterministically-seeded synthetic numbers (`seededAround(seed, mid, spread)`) from a per-day random generator. The Sector Compass that an investor or partner sees — "AI Infra composite 78.4, demand 84.1, capital 71.2" — is mathematically valid arithmetic over **invented inputs**. The numbers are stable within a calendar day (seeded), drift smoothly day-to-day, and have **zero correspondence to any real-world signal**.

Three further dashboards (Market Pulse, High-Conviction Plays, Studio Benchmarks) are **not aggregated at all** — they are hand-typed TypeScript object literals (`market_intel.ts:37-102`) that change only on code deploy. "TALC Positioning", which the marketing page advertises as one of six MI dashboards, **does not exist** in the codebase.

The one feature that does produce real, defensible intelligence is **Investor Signals** (`services/investor_signals/`), which aggregates investor-profile data with proper k-anonymity (MIN_CELL_SIZE = 5), correct quantile statistics, masked thin cells, and a 6-hour snapshot cadence. It is the only piece of this module that an institutional reviewer would respect — and it is sparse by construction (currently below k-threshold for most cells).

**Verdict.** As a product sold to investors, partners, or enterprise users today, Market Intelligence would not survive even a casual diligence review. Three rounds of dashboards are decorative; the headline scoring system is sophisticated arithmetic over fake inputs; the marketing claim of "real-time intelligence from 30+ providers refreshed nightly" is, as currently implemented, **materially false**. The marketing surface and the data surface are misaligned to a degree that exposes the company to credibility and (depending on subscription pricing) potential misrepresentation risk.

The good news: the **scaffolding is sound**. Wiring 6–8 genuinely free, high-credibility public APIs (SEC EDGAR, USPTO, OpenAlex, Hacker News, GitHub Trending, World Bank, OECD, Crossref) into the existing aggregator can be done in 30 days and would turn the Sector Compass from fiction into a defensible, citation-backed second-tier intelligence product. The investor-signals primitive can be the wedge for a genuinely differentiated capability nobody else owns. But the hardcoded vanity surfaces — Market Pulse, High-Conviction Plays, Studio Benchmarks, TALC — should be **removed before the next investor demo**, not improved.

**Recommended actions, in priority order:**

1. **This week.** Remove or clearly badge as "preview only" every dashboard backed by stub data. Stop marketing "30+ providers".
2. **30 days.** Wire 6 free, credible providers end-to-end. Rebuild Sector Compass on real data only.
3. **90 days.** Wire 6 more. Add provenance rail. Ship Geography Lens with real per-country rollups.
4. **180 days.** Define the intelligence wedge — Axal cannot win as a generic PitchBook alternative; it can win as an evidence-graph layer that connects founder activity inside Axal to public signal outside.

## 2 · ARCHITECTURE FINDINGS

The system is in three layers, all of which are technically competent.

**Connector layer** (`services/market_intel/sources/*.ts`, registered in `registry.ts`). 32 connectors with a uniform `CommonRow` output shape, dimension tagging (demand/supply/capital/talent/research/sentiment), per-source `weight ∈ [0.4, 0.9]`, quota tracking (`market_intel_quota` table), graceful stub fallback on rate-limit/error. The registry pattern is excellent and would scale to 100+ sources without architectural change.

**Aggregation layer** (`services/market_intel/aggregator.ts` + `scoring.ts`). Time-decayed weighted average per (sector, dimension) with a 14-day half-life and 30-day window. Composite headline is the unweighted mean of six dimensions. Idempotent UPSERT into `market_intel_indexes` on `(sector, geo, period_key, dimension)`. This is a defensible, transparent, **boring** algorithm — exactly what you want for intelligence work. No black-box ML; no LLM rewriting numbers; no AI synthesis. A reviewer can read scoring.ts and understand exactly why a sector got an 84.1.

**Serving layer** (`routes/market_intel.ts`). Three-tier cache (edge → KV 24h → DB recompute on demand), tier-gating per dashboard (free composite-only, Growth+ dimensions, Professional+ investor lens, Investor-role-only investor signals), HMAC-verified public unsubscribe surface. Cron schedule is sensible (`wrangler.toml`: hourly at :00, daily at 02:30, weekly Sun 02:45, recompute at 04:00, digests at 09:00).

**Architectural verdict:** A+ for the skeleton. The connector/aggregator/index split is exactly how this kind of system should be built. The problem is not architecture; the problem is that the connectors don't connect to anything.

**One real architectural risk:** `market_intel_rows` has no retention policy. The 30-day scoring window means anything older than 30 days is dead weight. With even modest live ingestion (say 32 sources × 12 sectors × 1 row/day = 384 rows/day), the table grows ~140K rows/year per geo. Once geography rollups land (the spec implies eventually 50+ geos), this becomes 7M+ rows/year with no TTL. Add a nightly purge of rows older than 90 days before live providers turn on.

## 4 · METHODOLOGY FINDINGS

The math is **defensible where it exists**, **opaque where it matters**, and **fictional in places that present as analysis**.

**Defensible.** The aggregator's recency-decayed weighted average (`scoring.ts:38-64`) is a textbook approach. Exponential half-life decay, per-source authority weights in [0.4, 0.9], composite = mean of 6 dimensions. A reviewer can read this in 90 seconds and form an informed view. Investor Signals' k-anonymity (`investor_signals.ts:543-547`, MIN_CELL_SIZE=5) is correctly implemented. Quantile statistics on ticket bands use order statistics without interpolation — pedantically correct.

**Opaque.** Three numbers drive most user-facing claims and **none are documented**:
- Per-source `weight` in `registry.ts` is in [0.4, 0.9]. Why is Gartner 0.7 and PitchBook 0.9? Why is Reddit 0.5 and Crunchbase 0.75? No rubric is documented. These weights propagate into every composite.
- Investor Lens score = `0.6 × capital + 0.4 × sentiment` (`market_intel.ts:332`). Why 60/40? No rationale in code or comments. An LP looking at a sector ranking generated from this would have to take the 60/40 split on faith.
- Founder Lens "Opportunity Gap" = `demand - supply`. This treats demand and supply as commensurable scalars, which is only true by construction (both are 0–100 composites of incommensurable underlying signals). A 12-point opportunity gap could mean "real demand-supply imbalance" or "Google Trends is spiking and BLS isn't" — the user can't tell.

**Fictional.** Three categories of output present as analysis but are not derived from data:

1. **Hardcoded constant arrays.** `MARKET_PULSE` (9 sectors with hand-written multiples, sentiment, technographic_signal, hiring_surge, gap_opportunity prose), `HIGH_CONVICTION` (6–7 plays with hand-written reasoning), `STUDIO_BENCHMARKS` (15 KPIs, all round numbers — `avg_time_to_inc_days: 11`, `founder_match_rate: 88`, etc.). These are TypeScript object literals in `market_intel.ts:37-102`. They change only on code deploy. They are presented to users in elaborate dashboard UI as if they were measured.
2. **Threshold-keyed narrative strings.** `compassReasoning()` (`MarketIntelPage.jsx:1097`) returns hand-written sentences keyed off composite buckets: `composite > 70 → "Hot sector — high demand + capital deployment is accelerating."` Users may interpret these as model-generated insights. They are CSS.
3. **The 32 source stubs themselves.** `seededAround(`${KEY}|${sector}|${day}`, 0.55)` is a deterministic PRNG masquerading as observation.

**No LLM is used in this module.** Zero `env.AI.run()` calls in any MI service file. This is, paradoxically, a **strength** — there is no hallucination risk because there is no generation. The risk surface is entirely "fake precision" and "arbitrary weighting", not "hallucinated content".

**Statistical validity verdict per artefact:**

| Artefact | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Sector Compass composite | Valid arithmetic, fictional inputs — **misleading** |
| Founder Lens opportunity gap | Heuristic, undefended assumption that subscores are commensurable — **speculative** |
| Investor Lens 60/40 score | Heuristic, undocumented weights — **arbitrary** |
| Investor Signals (k≥5 cells) | Statistically valid — **defensible** |
| Investor Signals (k<5 cells) | Correctly masked — **defensible** |
| Market Pulse multiples | Hand-typed constants — **fake** |
| High-Conviction reasoning | Hand-typed prose — **fake** |
| Studio Benchmarks KPIs | Hand-typed constants — **fake** |
| Live Yahoo Finance quotes | Real, current, 30-min cache — **valid** |
| RSS headlines | Real ingest, no relevance filter — **noise** |
| compassReasoning() text | Hand-typed thresholds — **fake** (presents as analysis) |

## 15 · LONG-TERM STRATEGIC ROADMAP

The 6-18 month thesis: **stop chasing the PitchBook category; own the categories nobody owns.**

**Quarter 1 (months 4-6) — Evidence-graph layer.** Axal's true unfair advantage is that the platform sits between the founder's private workspace (project profile, customer interviews, OKRs, financial model) and public market evidence. Build an explicit "evidence graph" that links founder activity to public signal:

- Founder writes a problem statement → system surfaces patents and S-1 filings citing similar problem language.
- Founder logs a customer interview tagged with pain X → system surfaces investors whose theses mention X.
- Founder ships a product wedge → system surfaces competing GitHub repos and HN threads.

This product is impossible for Crunchbase, PitchBook, or CB Insights to build because they don't have the private founder side. It is impossible for a founder workspace tool (Notion, Linear) to build because they don't have the market intelligence side. Axal sits in the middle.

**Quarter 2 (months 7-9) — Service-provider signals.** Extend the Investor Signals primitive to legal, design, recruiting, technical DD, finance. K-anonymized aggregation of which providers serve which sectors at which stages. Becomes a two-sided marketplace signal that complements existing matchmaking. No competitor owns this.

**Quarter 3 (months 10-12) — Outcome telemetry.** As Lab cohorts graduate, the platform accumulates the most valuable asset in venture: dose-response data. "Cohorts that hit milestone X by week Y had a 3.2× higher Series A conversion rate." This is what every investor wants to know and nobody can buy. Axal can publish it (privacy-respecting, k-anonymized) and become the citation source for "early-stage venture outcome benchmarks."

**Quarters 4-6 (months 13-18) — Open the API.** Once the underlying signal is credible and unique, expose a metered API for partners (corporate VC arms, sovereign funds, accelerators). Pricing per query against the Evidence Graph. The competitive moat is no longer the data (which is partly public) but the synthesis layer (which is proprietary because it sits across founder + market).

**Strategic risk to manage.** Through all of this, the temptation will be to claim breadth ("30+ providers, real-time intelligence") when reality is depth and synthesis. The right marketing posture is honesty about coverage paired with a clear story about the unique synthesis. "We don't have more rows than PitchBook. We have rows nobody else has, joined to rows nobody else can join."
