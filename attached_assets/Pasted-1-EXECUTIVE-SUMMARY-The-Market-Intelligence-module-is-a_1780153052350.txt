1 · EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Market Intelligence module is architecturally well-built and intelligence-empty.
The platform ships a 32-connector "sources catalog" (services/market_intel/sources/*.ts), 7 scored dimensions, 4 lens dashboards, a watchlist + email digest system, an investor-signals aggregator with proper k-anonymity, and a tier-gated public surface. The plumbing is real and well-engineered.
But of the 32 advertised data sources, exactly zero are pulling live data today. 12 throw paid_contract_not_signed (Gartner, PitchBook, CB Insights, SimilarWeb, Glassdoor, LinkedIn, Indeed, Ahrefs, BuiltWith, NewsAPI, Semrush) and 20 return deterministically-seeded synthetic numbers (seededAround(seed, mid, spread)) from a per-day random generator. The Sector Compass that an investor or partner sees — "AI Infra composite 78.4, demand 84.1, capital 71.2" — is mathematically valid arithmetic over invented inputs. The numbers are stable within a calendar day (seeded), drift smoothly day-to-day, and have zero correspondence to any real-world signal.
Three further dashboards (Market Pulse, High-Conviction Plays, Studio Benchmarks) are not aggregated at all — they are hand-typed TypeScript object literals (market_intel.ts:37-102) that change only on code deploy. "TALC Positioning", which the marketing page advertises as one of six MI dashboards, does not exist in the codebase.
The one feature that does produce real, defensible intelligence is Investor Signals (services/investor_signals/), which aggregates investor-profile data with proper k-anonymity (MIN_CELL_SIZE = 5), correct quantile statistics, masked thin cells, and a 6-hour snapshot cadence. It is the only piece of this module that an institutional reviewer would respect — and it is sparse by construction (currently below k-threshold for most cells).
Verdict. As a product sold to investors, partners, or enterprise users today, Market Intelligence would not survive even a casual diligence review. Three rounds of dashboards are decorative; the headline scoring system is sophisticated arithmetic over fake inputs; the marketing claim of "real-time intelligence from 30+ providers refreshed nightly" is, as currently implemented, materially false. The marketing surface and the data surface are misaligned to a degree that exposes the company to credibility and (depending on subscription pricing) potential misrepresentation risk.
The good news: the scaffolding is sound. Wiring 6–8 genuinely free, high-credibility public APIs (SEC EDGAR, USPTO, OpenAlex, Hacker News, GitHub Trending, World Bank, OECD, Crossref) into the existing aggregator can be done in 30 days and would turn the Sector Compass from fiction into a defensible, citation-backed second-tier intelligence product. The investor-signals primitive can be the wedge for a genuinely differentiated capability nobody else owns. But the hardcoded vanity surfaces — Market Pulse, High-Conviction Plays, Studio Benchmarks, TALC — should be removed before the next investor demo, not improved.
Recommended actions, in priority order:
This week. Remove or clearly badge as "preview only" every dashboard backed by stub data. Stop marketing "30+ providers".
30 days. Wire 6 free, credible providers end-to-end. Rebuild Sector Compass on real data only.
90 days. Wire 6 more. Add provenance rail. Ship Geography Lens with real per-country rollups.
180 days. Define the intelligence wedge — Axal cannot win as a generic PitchBook alternative; it can win as an evidence-graph layer that connects founder activity inside Axal to public signal outside.
