Investor Experience — UX/UI Audit & Redesign Roadmap
Scope: the full investor panel at axal.vc — the Studio home, Sourcing (Deal Flow, Pipeline Board, AI Matches, Watchlist & Journal), Diligence (Scoring, Due Diligence, Market Intelligence, Risk Matrix), Commit (IC Decisions, Legal & Capital, Capital & Investment), Support (the 8 portfolio/fund pages), Account, onboarding, and the tier/quota system — reviewed as one connected product system.
Constraints honored (mirroring the founder audit): the top-level investor sidebar groups (Home / Sourcing / Diligence / Commit / Support / Account) are unchanged; the investment lifecycle is designed as a module on the investor home, not a new sidebar section; simplification is preferred over new pages.
Method: every claim verified against the shipping code (frontend/src/, cloudflare-worker/src/routes/, cloudflare-worker/sql/). File references included so each finding is actionable.
Executive summary
The investor panel has the opposite disease from the founder panel. The founder saw studio-operator tools wearing a founder costume; the investor sees a seat at the studio's own console with the investor-shaped product left unplugged. The parts of the codebase built specifically for investors — a scored proprietary deal-flow payload, quota meters, a 9-step thesis onboarding, syndication tools — are computed and then never rendered, while the pages the investor actually lands on are shared admin/partner surfaces with studio-grade write access. Six systemic problems:
The investor home renders none of its investor data. /studio (Dashboard.jsx) is byte-for-byte the founder page plus a trial banner. Meanwhile the dashboard API (dashboard.ts) assembles proprietary_deal_flow (scored, NDA-masked), ai_scored_opportunities, quick_stats, and syndication_tools for investors on every load — and Dashboard.jsx reads none of them. The quota meters (InvestorQuotaBars) are mounted only on /partner-portal, from which investors are redirected away (App.jsx:1443): an investor can never see their own intro/deal-room usage.
There is no deal spine, and the funnel stages don't hand off. Nothing answers the investor's core questions: which deals am I in, at what stage, and what's my next action? Deal Flow is a firm-wide funnel of all deals (not "mine") whose only action is a stage-advance button; Pipeline Board is the studio's internal MVP kanban; AI Match cards are read-only dead ends (no pass / intro / add-to-watchlist); the Decision Journal's next_check_at follow-up exists in the worker but is never surfaced. Downstream it's the same: a due-diligence case can't attach to an IC decision, and an IC "invest" verdict creates no capital record — the only wired hand-off in the entire Source→Exit funnel is scoring → deal memo → IC (from_scoring).
The thesis is captured twice and used for neither. Onboarding's 9 steps write investor_profiles (sectors, stages, geos, thesis, anti-thesis, value weights) — which feeds the founder's investor-match view, not the investor's own deal filtering. The Matches page has a second "Investor Preferences" modal writing a separate user_preferences table. The two stores overlap ~80% and are never synced. Worse, onboarding collects accreditation_status, country, firm_name, and LP intent and silently drops them (never passed to saveInvestorProfile).
Role bleed in both directions. Investors get studio-operator write powers they shouldn't want: dragging the studio's MVP pipeline, creating pipeline startups, triggering AI reviews, and deciding Kill gates (pipeline.ts ADVANCE_ROLES includes investor); a single "advance" button on every deal in Deal Flow. Meanwhile Due Diligence lives at an /admin/* URL in the investor nav. The investor is treated as staff, not as a customer with a funnel.
The Support group is 8 pages where ~4 workspaces would do. The LP holdings table renders identically in Funds and Liquidity; TVPI/DPI is computed live in Funds and hand-authored in LP Reporting; capital calls appear in Funds and Capital; Reserve Allocation and Exit Waterfall are two pages over the same simulator engine, the same fund selector, and the same fund_scenarios table, with StatCard/SliderInput components duplicated verbatim; the exit lifecycle is split across three destinations (model in Waterfall, execute in Liquidity, distribute in Funds).
Quality erosion at the edges. Watchlist/Journal frontend fields (source, tags, external_*, statuses passed_on/archived, outcomes hit/miss/partial) don't match the worker's persisted schema (watching|passed|converted, win|loss|pending) — silent data loss; the Account group is an 11-item bucket where Advisors (a mentee-facing booking directory) and Jobs (a job board) have no investor purpose; deal/score cards are re-implemented ~5 times with different status pills and color maps.
The fix is not more pages. It is one deal-centric lifecycle spine on the investor home, one preference store, sourcing pages that act on deals instead of describing them, a Support group consolidated around Portfolio / Fund Ops / Modeling / Liquidity, and a clean split between "investor as customer" and "studio operator" permissions.
Section-by-section audit
1) Home — /studio (Dashboard.jsx)
What's there. The same component all five roles get: welcome header, semantic search, notification bell, "Refresh Scores", PersonalAdvisor, ProfileFitSection, and two conditional operator cards. Investor-specific rendering is exactly one component: InvestorTrialBanner. RoleBadge doesn't even have an investor style (falls through to gray).
Critique. The dashboard API already does the hard work — it builds a scored, NDA-masked proprietary_deal_flow, ai_scored_opportunities, quick_stats (earnings, syndicates, avg AI score), and syndication_tools for investors — and the page throws it all away. This is the single highest-leverage gap in the panel: the investor-shaped home exists in the API and just needs a front end. Separately, InvestorQuotaBars renders only for investors but is mounted only on PartnerPortal, which redirects investors to /studio — dead UI.
Recommendation — make /studio the investor's deal desk (role-conditional composition inside the existing page, no new route):
Deal Lifecycle module (spec below): my deals by stage + next best action.
Scored opportunities strip: render the already-computed payload as actionable cards (open deal room, add to watchlist, request intro).
Quota + plan card: mount InvestorQuotaBars here — one glance = intros left, deal rooms used, plan, upgrade path.
Keep PersonalAdvisor and search; drop ProfileFitSection for investors (skills/values radar is a founder/talent concept).
2) Onboarding & preferences
What's there. A genuinely good 9-step wizard: investor type, accreditation, check size, stages, sectors, geos, thesis paragraph, anti-thesis exclusions, value-weight sliders, LP intent → investor_profiles.
Critique. Three defects: (a) accreditation_status, country, firm_name, lp_intent, lp_target_usd are collected and never persisted; (b) the profile powers the founder-side matcher but not the investor's own deal flow — that reads a second store, user_preferences, edited in the Matches modal; (c) nothing after onboarding ever shows the investor their own thesis or lets them edit it.
Recommendation. One preference store — make investor_profiles canonical, migrate user_preferences rows once, retire that path; every scored surface filters by the same thesis. Persist the dropped fields (LP intent pre-fills the Funds LP portal). Add a compact "My thesis" card in Settings so the thesis is visible and editable after day one.
3) Sourcing — Deal Flow, Pipeline Board, AI Matches, Watchlist & Journal
What's there.
Deal Flow (234 lines): firm-wide funnel (applied → scored → funded); the only action is a stage-advance button any investor can press on any deal; no pass, no intro request, not scoped to "my" deals.
Pipeline Board (743 lines): the studio's MVP kanban (Idea → MVP Dev → Traction Review → Decision Gate → Spin-Out Ready → Iterate) — investors have full write access including Kill gate decisions.
AI Matches (481 lines): three investor tabs of scored, LLM-explained cards — all read-only.
Watchlist & Journal (749 lines): the best-designed investor surface — watchlist, thesis-before-vote decision journal, and an Anti-Portfolio tab (vindicated/regret analysis). But the frontend writes fields the worker never persists and uses enums the worker doesn't have; next_check_at exists in the route with no UI and no reminder.
Critique. Four pages, four data models, ~5 different deal-card implementations, and no concept of "my deals" anywhere. A scored match card with no action is a brochure.
Recommendation.
Make matches actionable: every scored card gets three verbs — Add to watchlist, Request intro (finally consuming the intro quota), Open/join deal room.
Scope Deal Flow to the investor: "My deals" default filter; per-relationship actions replace the naked advance button (view room, pass with reason → auto-journal entry).
Pipeline Board: strip investor write access to the studio's gates — an investor deciding "Kill" on a studio startup is a permissions bug wearing a feature costume.
Fix the Watchlist/Journal contract: align worker enums/fields with the UI; surface next_check_at as a visible date + notification — the cheapest path to the "next action" concept the whole panel is missing.
4) Diligence — Scoring Engine, Due Diligence, Market Intelligence, Risk Matrix
What's there.
Scoring Engine: 100-point, 6-category rubric with a Practice/Official toggle — but investor runs always write OFFICIAL scores (7-day cooldown). Deal-memo generation can seed an IC decision (from_scoring, ic.ts:106) — the one wired hand-off in the whole funnel.
Due Diligence (/admin/due-diligence): a real case system (11 section types, 9 connectors, NDA-gated verdicts) — but the investor gets a scoped reviewer view inside an admin console URL and cannot start diligence on a deal.
Market Intelligence (2,947 lines): a 21-tab research portal. Rich, real data — one page, twenty-one doors.
Risk Matrix: portfolio-wide company × 10-layer heatmap; read-only.
Critique. Depth without direction. The most consequential trap: exploring the scorer silently writes an official score. The most consequential gap: diligence — the group's namesake — is a console the investor can only peek into at a URL that says "you don't belong here". Market Intelligence is the opposite failure: insight never becomes action.
Recommendation. Scoring: practice-by-default with explicit official-submit confirm; promote "Generate deal memo → open IC decision" to the primary CTA. Due Diligence: investor-facing home (no /admin framing); open a case from a deal you're in; reviewer items feed the lifecycle module. Market Intelligence: regroup 21 tabs into 4–5 lenses with an overview; one "insight → action" verb per view. Risk Matrix: fine — cross-link from Portfolio Health.
5) Commit — IC Decisions, Legal & Capital, Capital & Investment
What's there. IC Decisions: a real draft → voting → decided workflow (thin but sound). Legal & Capital: investor read-only except the LP Portal tab (syndicate Commit/Paid/Decline). Capital & Investment: fund-ops KPIs and calls; investor read-only plus "Mark Paid" on own calls.
Critique. Individually defensible, collectively unplumbed. A DD case cannot attach to an IC decision. An IC "invest" decision creates no capital record — the decision evaporates. Capital calls live in two stores. The "thesis before the vote" journal is not connected to the IC vote where the thesis gets tested.
Recommendation (wire the chain): DD → IC (dd_case_id on IC decisions); IC → ledger (invest outcome prompts a pre-filled position/LP record); IC → journal (auto-drafted entry per vote — the anti-portfolio builds itself); one capital-call store, with /capital folding into Fund Ops.
6) Support — the portfolio & fund-ops eight
What's there. Portfolio Health (computed daily scores; rich), Company Updates (founder-submitted KPIs; thin), Cap Table (positions; thin), Funds (fund ops + a genuinely good LP portal; 597 lines), LP Reporting (thin), Reserve Allocation + Exit Waterfall (two views of the same simulator engine and scenarios table), Liquidity & Exits (secondary market; "Mock real settlement" placeholder).
Critique. The information is good; the packaging triples it. An LP asks "what am I in, what's it worth, what's been called, what came back?" — today that's five different pages. A GP asks "how do I model the fund?" — that's two nearly identical pages. Exit is three places.
Recommendation — four coherent workspaces (sidebar rows stay, deep-linking to tabs): Portfolio (Health · Updates · Positions), Fund Ops (Funds admin · LP Reporting · capital calls), My LP Portal (canonical holdings · reports · calls), Fund Modeling (Reserves · Waterfall — they already share everything else). Liquidity reads holdings from the canonical source; label mock settlement as simulation.
7) Account group, tiers & quotas
What's there. Eleven items, several with no investor purpose (Advisors, Jobs, Articles, Partners). The tier system (free/professional/institutional; 3/25/100 intros, 1/5/unlimited deal rooms) is well-built in the worker and well-priced — but experienced only as lock icons and paywalls; consumption is invisible.
Recommendation. Trim Account to account things; surface the quota card on the home and beside every 402; locked items preview (blurred cards) instead of lock icons.
The Deal Lifecycle module (on the investor home)
Stage	Investor's goal	Existing surfaces	Top action	System surfaces first
Source	Fill the funnel with thesis-fit deals	AI Matches, deal strip, Watchlist	Add 3 matches to watchlist	New scored matches vs thesis
Screen	Decide what deserves work	Deal Flow ("my deals"), Watchlist, Scoring	Pass or open deal room	Score, trust/risk badges, conviction
Diligence	Verify before committing	DD cases, Market Intelligence, Risk Matrix	Open/complete a DD case	Open checklist items, doc requests
Commit	Decide and paper it	IC Decisions, Legal & Capital, journal	Record IC decision + journal entry	Pending IC votes, unsigned docs
Support	Track and help the position	Portfolio, Funds/LP portal	Read latest company update	Health alerts, updates, calls due
Exit	Realize returns	Waterfall (model), Liquidity (execute), Funds (distribute)	Model or list a position	Open listings, distributions, outcomes vs journal
Mechanics: the spine is the deal relationship, not a new stage column — "my deals" derives from existing tables (investor_dealroom_members, investor_introductions, watchlist/journal, dd_cases, ic_decisions, portfolio_positions, secondary_listings) via one aggregation route (GET /api/dashboard/investor-lifecycle). Next actions come from data that already exists (journal next_check_at, open DD items, pending IC votes, calls due, unread updates) — no new task system. UI: compact stage rail + next-best-action list + opportunities strip, reusing the founder audit's rail/checklist components. No wizard, no new sidebar entry.
