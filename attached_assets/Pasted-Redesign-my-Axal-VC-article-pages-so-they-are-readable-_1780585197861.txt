Redesign my Axal VC article pages so they are readable, editorial, and premium instead of dense and hard to scan.

The current articles lack spacing, section titles, hierarchy, and visual rhythm. Rebuild the article experience as a long-form reading layout that feels like a high-end venture journal.

Goals:
- Make articles feel like polished editorial essays, not default CMS posts.
- Improve readability with clear section breaks, generous spacing, strong heading hierarchy, and better paragraph rhythm.
- Keep the tone premium, calm, modern, and credible.
- Avoid generic startup templates, loud visuals, glowing gradients, and “AI-looking” design.

Page structure:
1. Hero section with category, title, short intro/deck, author, reading time, and date.
2. Article body with clear H2 and H3 headings, readable paragraphs, blockquotes, lists, callouts, and dividers.
3. Sticky table of contents on desktop, generated from headings.
4. Reading progress indicator.
5. Inline share actions.
6. Related articles at the bottom.

Typography and layout:
- Use an editorial layout with a narrow reading column, around 65–75 characters per line.
- Add generous vertical spacing between paragraphs, headings, lists, quotes, and sections.
- Make H2s clearly distinct with strong spacing above and below.
- Style H3s as meaningful subheads, not just slightly bolder body text.
- Use large, readable body text with strong line-height.
- Support long-form content properly: blockquotes, key takeaways, callout boxes, images with captions, tables, and code blocks.
- Keep the article easy to scan from headings alone.

Visual style:
- Minimal, Swiss-inspired, premium, and institutional.
- Neutral palette with one restrained accent color.
- Excellent whitespace and subtle borders.
- No heavy shadows, no gradient overload, no decorative blobs, no icon circles, no generic SaaS-card look.

Responsive behavior:
- Mobile-first.
- On mobile, simplify the layout and keep only the essentials.
- Collapse the table of contents into a compact top section or dropdown.
- Preserve reading comfort on small screens.

Technical requirements:
- Use semantic HTML for article, section, aside, nav, header, and footer.
- Make this reusable as a template for all article pages.
- Add anchor links to headings.
- Ensure accessibility, keyboard navigation, and strong contrast.
- Make it fast and lightweight.

Design reference:
- Think Stripe Press meets a modern venture journal.

Success criteria:
- A long article should instantly feel easier to read.
- The structure should be obvious at a glance.
- Paragraphs should no longer look like one dense wall of text.
- The result should feel like an experienced operator-investor wrote it with clarity and authority.
